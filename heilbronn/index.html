Heilbronn's Triangle Problem

Put n points in a square of side 1, and look at the triangles with corners at three of them. How large can the smallest of these triangles be? For nine points, Lean proves that the answer is H9 = (9√65 − 55)/320 ≈ 0.0548760, and that only one configuration reaches it, up to the symmetries of the square. Here you can move the points and watch the smallest triangle. Its area never gets above H9.

    How to play

    Drag a point to move it. Points stay in the square: drag one past a side and it slides along the side. The triangles of least area are shaded, and those a little larger are dashed. Three points on a line make a triangle of area 0, drawn as a red line.

    Improve is a local search. Step by step it moves all the points to make the smallest triangle larger, and it stops where no small move helps. That is usually a local maximum below the proved value, as the problem has many. Place the optimal configuration, drag a point a little way, and Improve brings it back.

    Click a point to type its coordinates; fractions like 1/3 work.

    Keys, on the board: Enter picks a point, the arrows move it, Shift takes bigger steps, and Esc lets it go.

    The value, the configuration and its eleven triangles are those of the Lean library lean4-heilbronn, whose README states the definitions and the theorems, and Lean's kernel checks every step of the proof. More on the project: Heilbronn's triangle problem in Lean 4. The configuration is from F. Comellas and J. L. A. Yebra, New lower bounds for Heilbronn numbers, Electron. J. Combin. 9 (2002), R6.
