IKC

Iterative k-core Clustering

Walk through Wedell, Park, Korobskiy, Warnow + Chacko's IKC algorithm one iteration at a time on the 32-node comdet fixture.

Draft. JS port of community-detection/src/ikc/run_ikc.py against the 32-node fixture; gate verdicts traced per iteration. Shipped IKC's modularity gate is hard-coded to always pass; the page recreates the paper formula for display, with a strict-gate toggle on the outer-loop walker.

If a cluster is supposed to mean every member connects to enough others inside it, the densest structure that satisfies the constraint is the \(k\)-core: the maximal subgraph where every node has at least \(k\) intra-subgraph neighbours. On this fixture the \(K_5\) is a 4-core, B's two \(K_4\)'s are 3-cores, C's two triangles are 2-cores, and A's periphery dangles below any \(k \ge 2\). IKC walks these tiers from top down: extract the densest core of the residual, accept every connected component whose nodes still hit the \(k\) threshold inside the component, peel the accepted nodes, repeat. The loop ends when the residual's max core drops below the floor.

Where Louvain and Leiden grow communities by absorbing neighbours, IKC strips communities of their loosely-attached members. Coverage is loose; per-cluster core density is high. Wedell 2022 situates IKC as Stage 1 of a four-stage kmp-clustering pipeline (\(k\)-validity, modularity, \(p\)-validity for noncore augmentation); this page covers Stage 1 only.

k-core the maximal induced subgraph where every node has degree \(\ge k\). Computed by iterative peel: repeatedly remove every node currently below \(k\); whatever survives is the \(k\)-core.
k-validity a candidate cluster \(C\) is \(k\)-valid iff every node in \(C\) has \(\ge k\) other \(C\)-nodes among its neighbours within the kcore subgraph. Stricter than belonging to the \(k\)-core globally: a node in the global \(k\)-core can still fail \(k\)-validity for its component if its \(k\) qualifying neighbours sit in a different component of the kcore subgraph. The \(k\)-core is necessary, not sufficient.
modularity gate paper formula \[\mathrm{mod}(s) = \ell_s/L - (d_s/2L)^2\] with \(L\) = total edges of the original graph. Shipped IKC short-circuits this to a constant, so the gate is effectively a no-op. The strict-gate toggle on the outer-loop walker switches the constant off and runs the formula.

why the residual matters

Each accepted cluster removes its nodes from the residual, which lowers the residual's max core and exposes the next tier. Without the removal step the loop would keep finding the same top core every iteration. The walker's iteration counter and \(K^*\) readout track this descent.

on this fixture, by region

At \(k_{\mathrm{floor}} = 2\): A's \(K_5\) clears at \(k = 4\) and the periphery never recovers (every periphery node has at most one periphery neighbour). B's 8-block clears at \(k = 3\) thanks to the bridge edge \((15, 16)\), which gives every \(K_4\) member a third intra-cluster neighbour. C's two triangles each meet \(k = 2\) and clear independently. D's two 2-cliques fail (each member has only one internal neighbour). Outliers 30 + 31 stay singletons.

Slide the floor on the walker to see the partition shrink at higher \(k\) and barely filter at lower \(k\). At \(k = 0\) the partition reduces almost to connected components of the residual, with size-1 components stripped by the output writer.

shipped vs paper

The shipped run_ikc.py is a strict subset of Wedell 2022. Four places where the binary departs from the paper:

  1. Modularity gate is dead. The shipped modular() begins with a constant return, so the paper's \(\mathrm{mod}(s)\) formula at the bottom of the function is never reached. \(k\)-validity is the only filter that actually fires. The strict-gate toggle on the walker re-enables the formula for comparison; on this fixture every accepted cluster has positive paper-modularity, so the toggle leaves the partition unchanged.
  2. m-validity is never enforced. Paper §2.2.2 calls a cluster m-valid iff its core-induced subgraph is connected and has positive modularity. The connectedness half holds by construction, since the gate sees only weakly-connected components of the kcore. The modularity half rides on the dead gate above, so m-validity collapses to "connected", which is always true.
  3. p-validity is not implemented. Paper §2.2.3 defines a four-stage kmp-clustering pipeline; \(p\)-validity is the Stage 3 augmentation that grows each accepted core cluster by attaching outside nodes adjacent to enough core members. Shipped src/ikc/ covers Stage 1 only, so every accepted cluster is exactly its core.
  4. Modularity column is a placeholder. Each accepted cluster is written with modularity = 1, the dead-gate constant, not a measurement. Downstream consumers reading the column see 1 for every cluster regardless of structure.
