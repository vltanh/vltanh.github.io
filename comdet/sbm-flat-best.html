SBM-flat-best

flat stochastic block model, lowest description length

Three flat SBM variants (degree-corrected, non-degree-corrected, planted-partition) run from one shared seeded init on the 32-node fixture; pairwise \(\Delta\Sigma\) ranked under Peixoto's Bayes-factor reading.

Draft. Anchor page for the flat SBM family. The page walks the algorithm one micro-step at a time: posterior, four description-length terms, single MH visit, full sweep, equilibration, three-way verdict. All three variants run the same MCMC chain (uniform proposal, \(\beta = 1\), 20 sweeps) from one shared seeded random init; each variant minimises its own microcanonical description length under its own prior. Absolute \(\Sigma\) values across variants are not directly comparable in nats: the priors differ, the likelihoods differ, and the description-length terms differ in count. What is comparable is which partition each variant picks at the end of the chain. The verdict labels (\(|\Delta\Sigma| < 5\) nats inconclusive, \(5\) to \(10\) suggestive, beyond \(10\) decisive) follow Peixoto 2017 §VII.A, read pairwise rather than as an absolute scoring. Bit-equality of the JS kernel against the cpp tracer holds across the 17-fixture 3-tier stress panel under matched MT19937; cross-check against the canonical graph-tool pipeline (different RNG, dense entropy, multilevel agglomerative merges) is a separate exercise.

Community detection read through the Bayesian lens. The flat stochastic block model posits a generative process: each node carries a discrete block label \(b_i \in \{1, \ldots, B\}\), the model fixes the count of edges between every pair of blocks \(e_{rs}\), and the observed graph is a uniform draw from the simple graphs that meet those constraints. The inference problem is the inverse: given the graph, find the partition that compresses it the most.

The rest of the page walks that recipe. Stages 1 through 3 derive the posterior, name the three variants, and decompose the description length. Stages 4 through 7 take each of the four cost terms in isolation. Stages 8 through 11 step through one Metropolis-Hastings visit, then one sweep, then equilibration. Stages 12 onward place the three variants side by side and read the verdict.

DC degree-corrected sparse entropy. Likelihood conditions on the per-node degrees \(\boldsymbol{k}\); the vertex term in the entropy carries \(\log(e_r!)\) (Peixoto Eq 43). Survives heavy-tailed degree distributions and clusters that mix hubs with leaves.
NDC non-degree-corrected sparse entropy. No degree conditioning; the vertex term carries \(e_r \log n_r\) instead (Peixoto Eq 22). Useful when degree variation carries cluster signal that DC would absorb into the model.
PP planted-partition (Zhang and Peixoto 2020). Two-rate Bernoulli likelihood with intra-block density \(p_{\text{in}}\) and inter-block \(p_{\text{out}}\). The strongest assortative prior, parsimonious by construction.
\(\Sigma\) total description length under the variant's likelihood plus the shared partition prior plus the variant-specific edges DL. Lower is better.
\(\Delta\Sigma\) pairwise gap between two variants on the same partition. Negative means the named variant compresses better.
Bayes factor \(\Lambda = e^{-\Delta\Sigma}\) when comparing two variants as alternative explanations of the same data. A gap of 10 nats corresponds to roughly 22,000-to-1 odds in favour of the lower-\(\Sigma\) variant.

stage 1 · the posterior

From generative model to inference target.

The generative direction is the easy one. Sample a partition \(\boldsymbol{b}\) from a prior \(P(\boldsymbol{b})\); given \(\boldsymbol{b}\) plus the inter-block edge counts \(\boldsymbol{e}\), draw the adjacency \(\boldsymbol{A}\) uniformly from the simple graphs that match \(\boldsymbol{e}\). The model never has to choose where individual edges go beyond the block-level counts. The inference problem reverses the arrow: the graph \(\boldsymbol{A}\) is given, and the partition \(\boldsymbol{b}\) is the unknown to recover.

Bayes's rule turns the generative likelihood into a posterior over partitions:

\(P(\boldsymbol{b} \mid \boldsymbol{A}) = \dfrac{P(\boldsymbol{A} \mid \boldsymbol{b}) \, P(\boldsymbol{b})}{P(\boldsymbol{A})}\).

In plain terms: the probability the model assigns to partition \(\boldsymbol{b}\) given the observed graph is proportional to how likely \(\boldsymbol{A}\) is under \(\boldsymbol{b}\), times how likely \(\boldsymbol{b}\) is a priori. The denominator \(P(\boldsymbol{A})\) is a partition-independent constant; it normalises the posterior but does not enter any ranking of partitions. Two properties. The posterior shifts under any change of prior: \(P(\boldsymbol{b})\) is a modelling choice, not an objective fact about the graph. And the posterior is well-defined for every partition of \(N\) nodes into \(B \in [1, N]\) blocks, so the inference target is a discrete distribution over a finite (if astronomically large) set.

why Bayesian and not just maximum-likelihood

Without the prior \(P(\boldsymbol{b})\), the maximum-likelihood partition is degenerate. Every node alone wins the likelihood (each edge sits in its own one-by-one block-pair cell, perfect fit), but that partition tells no compressive story about the graph. The prior penalises both extremes and forces the optimum to sit at a mid-\(B\) partition where data fit and parsimony trade off. The trade-off is set by the priors, not by a tunable resolution knob like CPM's \(\gamma\).

stage 2 · three variants of the flat SBM

Same posterior, different choices of likelihood and prior.

The three flat-SBM variants differ in what counts as the data and what counts as the prior. All three are principled, none is universally right, and the standard recipe for an unseen network is to run all three and let the description length decide.

DC · degree-corrected

The likelihood conditions on the observed degree sequence \(\boldsymbol{k}\); the vertex term in the entropy carries \(\log(e_r!)\) per block.

Fits when degree heterogeneity sits in the model rather than the likelihood, so a hub and a leaf can share a block.

NDC · non-degree-corrected

No degree conditioning; the vertex term carries \(e_r \log n_r\). Every block member is exchangeable.

Fits when degree variation carries cluster signal that DC would absorb into the model.

PP · planted-partition

Collapses the per-pair \(\boldsymbol{e}\) matrix into two scalars: total intra-block edges \(E_{\text{in}}\) and inter-block edges \(E_{\text{out}}\).

Fits when the only question is whether the graph is denser inside blocks than between.

The three differ in what they cannot see. DC has no way to penalise a block whose internal connectivity is sparse but whose degree sequence is well-explained; NDC has no way to absorb a hub-leaf degree gap; PP cannot distinguish two blocks with the same intra-density but different internal topology. Knowing which blindspot bites is itself a Bayesian question, which is why this page runs all three and reads the gap.

stage 3 · description length anatomy

One posterior, three or four cost terms.

Negating the logarithm of the posterior turns it into a description length, the total cost (in nats) of writing down the graph if a receiver already knew the block labels:

\(\Sigma(\boldsymbol{b}) = -\log P(\boldsymbol{A}, \boldsymbol{e}, \boldsymbol{b}) = \underbrace{-\log P(\boldsymbol{A} \mid \boldsymbol{e}, \boldsymbol{b})}_{S} + \underbrace{-\log P(\boldsymbol{e} \mid \boldsymbol{b})}_{L_e} + \underbrace{-\log P(\boldsymbol{b})}_{L_p} \;[+\; L_d]\).

In plain terms: the partition's quality is the total cost of three or four counting problems. The sparse entropy \(S\) pays for the per-edge placement once the edge-count matrix is known. The edges DL \(L_e\) pays for transmitting the matrix itself. The partition DL \(L_p\) pays for the labelling. The DC variant adds a fourth term, the uniform degree DL \(L_d\), that prices the per-block degree sums given block populations. Two properties. The partition that minimises \(\Sigma\) is the maximum-posterior partition under the chosen prior, no calibration needed. And dropping any one of the terms recovers a degenerate optimum: keep only the data fit, the minimum is \(B = 1\); keep only the priors, the minimum is \(B = N\). The mid-\(B\) optimum requires all three.

stage 4 · cost term 1, sparse entropy

What the data fit costs, in three forms.

The sparse entropy prices the placement of individual edges given the block-pair counts \(\boldsymbol{e}\). The form depends on the variant. For DC,

\(S_{\text{DC}} = \sum_r \log(e_r!) \;-\; \sum_{r

where \(e_r\) is block \(r\)'s total degree (each internal edge contributes twice, once per endpoint stub), \(e_{rs}\) is the count of edges between block \(r\) and block \(s\), and \(e_{rr}\) follows the doubled-diagonal convention that internal edges contribute twice. In plain terms: cost the graph by ordering the \(e_r\) half-stubs at every block (the \(\log(e_r!)\) terms), discount the redundancy from pairing them across off-diagonal pairs and inside diagonal blocks, and subtract one factor of \(\log 2\) per internal edge because the two stubs at a diagonal endpoint are interchangeable. Two properties. The factorial form is exact and microcanonical; no Stirling approximation is used, which is what allows direct comparison with the NDC and PP variants in the same units. And the term is partition-dependent only through the entries of \(\boldsymbol{e}\); a partition that concentrates edges inside blocks drops the vertex factor by less than it drops the edge factor, which is the source of the data-fit bonus for assortative partitions.

NDC replaces \(\log(e_r!)\) with \(e_r \log n_r\):

\(S_{\text{NDC}} = \sum_r e_r \log n_r \;-\; \sum_{r

In plain terms: the cost of a degree-free Poisson-style placement, where every internal edge picks two of the \(n_r\) members independently and pays \(\log n_r\) per stub. Two properties. The NDC formula makes the model invariant under relabelling of nodes inside a block, which is the right assumption when degrees are roughly homogeneous within communities. And the formula penalises hubs harshly: a node of degree 9 inside a block of size 12 pays \(9 \log 12 \approx 22.4\) nats where DC under the same node pays \(\log(9!) \approx 12.8\) nats, a 9.6-nat gap per hub. On a graph with many hubs the NDC vs. DC \(\Delta\Sigma\) at a hub-anchored partition is dominated by this gap.

PP collapses the matrix into two binomials:

\(S_{\text{PP}} = \log\!\binom{M_{\text{in}}}{E_{\text{in}}} + \log\!\binom{M_{\text{out}}}{E_{\text{out}}}\),

with \(E_{\text{in}} = \sum_r e_{rr}/2\) the total intra-block edge count, \(E_{\text{out}} = E - E_{\text{in}}\) the inter-block count, \(M_{\text{in}} = \sum_r \binom{n_r}{2}\) the available intra-block pair slots, and \(M_{\text{out}} = \binom{N}{2} - M_{\text{in}}\) the inter-block slots. In plain terms: throw \(E_{\text{in}}\) edges uniformly into \(M_{\text{in}}\) slots inside and \(E_{\text{out}}\) edges uniformly into \(M_{\text{out}}\) slots outside; pay the log of the count of distinct ways to do both. Two properties. The model has a strong assortative inductive bias: a partition that raises the contrast between intra-block and inter-block density wins on the likelihood, regardless of degree shape. And the formula depends on the totals only, so moving one node updates \(E_{\text{in}}\) and \(M_{\text{in}}\) but recomputes both binomials from scratch; the kernel falls back to a full recompute on every PP virtual move, where DC and NDC use a closed-form O(degree) subset-entropy delta.

stage 5 · cost term 2, edges DL

What it costs to transmit the edge-count matrix.

Once the partition is known, the model still has to send the entries of \(\boldsymbol{e}\). For DC and NDC the cost is

\(L_e \;=\; \log\!\binom{B(B{+}1)/2 + E - 1}{E}\),

which is the log of the number of weak compositions of \(E\) total edges into the \(B(B{+}1)/2\) upper-triangular cells of the symmetric matrix. In plain terms: count the distinct ways to distribute \(E\) indistinguishable edges among the available block-pair cells; the log gives the bits required to specify one of them. Two properties. The term grows with \(B\), so it acts as a penalty against over-splitting; doubling \(B\) roughly quadruples the cell count and the cost goes up by roughly \(E \log 2\) nats. And the term is independent of which partition is chosen at fixed \(B\); it shifts the absolute \(\Sigma\) but not the ranking of partitions of the same block count.

For PP the matrix collapses to a single scalar \(E_{\text{in}}\) (with \(E_{\text{out}} = E - E_{\text{in}}\) implied), so the edges DL collapses too:

\(L_e^{\text{PP}} \;=\; \log\!\big(\min(E, M_{\text{in}}) + 1\big)\).

In plain terms: the model has to name one integer in \([0, \min(E, M_{\text{in}})]\), the count of intra-block edges; the log of the range gives the bits. Two properties. The PP edges DL is much smaller than the DC and NDC term at \(B \ge 3\); part of why absolute \(\Sigma\) values are not comparable across variants. And the term is partition-dependent through \(M_{\text{in}} = \sum_r \binom{n_r}{2}\); a partition with one giant block has \(M_{\text{in}} \approx \binom{N}{2}\), and the term grows logarithmically with \(N\).

stage 6 · cost term 3, partition DL

What it costs to name the labelling itself.

Identical across all three variants. The cost decomposes into three sub-terms:

\(L_p \;=\; \underbrace{\log(N!) - \sum_r \log(n_r!)}_{\text{labelling at fixed sizes}} \;+\; \underbrace{\log\!\binom{N - 1}{B - 1}}_{\text{choosing block sizes}} \;+\; \underbrace{\log N}_{\text{choosing } B}\).

In plain terms: the first piece is the multinomial coefficient that counts which node-permutations give the same block sizes; the second piece counts compositions of \(N\) into \(B\) positive integers; the third piece is a uniform prior over the integer \(B \in [1, N]\). Two properties. The term penalises both extremes symmetrically: at \(B = 1\) the labelling and size-choice terms vanish but the prior over \(B\) pays \(\log N\); at \(B = N\) the labelling term pays \(\log(N!)\) but everything else collapses; the mid-\(B\) regime sits between. And the term has no notion of edges; partition DL is what differentiates the SBM from a pure data-fit model.

stage 7 · cost term 4, degree DL (DC only)

What conditioning on the degree sequence costs.

The DC variant pays a fourth term for naming the per-node degrees given the per-block degree sums:

\(L_d \;=\; \sum_r \log\!\binom{n_r + e_r - 1}{e_r}\).

In plain terms: once a block of \(n_r\) members has a total degree \(e_r\), the number of integer compositions of \(e_r\) over \(n_r\) bins is \(\binom{n_r + e_r - 1}{e_r}\); the log gives the bits required to specify which member got which degree under a uniform prior on compositions. Two properties. The term is the price DC pays for conditioning on \(\boldsymbol{k}\); NDC reads the degrees implicitly through the data fit and skips this term, which is one reason DC and NDC are not directly comparable in absolute \(\Sigma\). And the formula is partition-dependent only through \(n_r\) and \(e_r\); a partition that lines up high-degree nodes inside one block pushes \(e_r\) up there and the cost grows roughly as \(e_r \log(e_r/n_r)\).

the degree constant · cancels in \(\Delta\Sigma\), surfaces in absolute \(\Sigma\)

The DC sparse entropy carries an additive term \(\sum_i \log(k_i!)\) that depends on the per-node degrees but not on the partition. The kernel subtracts this constant from the reported \(\Sigma\) so the absolute value is commensurable with NDC and PP at the same partition; if the constant were left in, DC's reported \(\Sigma\) would be systematically larger by tens of nats with no partition-dependent meaning. The MH chain accepts or rejects moves on \(\Delta\Sigma\), which is independent of the constant, so the trajectory is the same either way.

stage 9 · the four sub-steps of one MH visit

Pick a vertex, build the pool, score every candidate, flip the coin.

The MCMC walker is a single-vertex Metropolis-Hastings sampler. One sweep visits every vertex once in shuffled order; one visit runs four sub-steps. The walker on this stage shows them on the DC chain (seed 7); rerolling seeds at stage 8 re-runs the same trajectory under a fresh seed.

  1. Pick a vertex \(v\). The chain processes vertices in a shuffled order; the visit shows which \(v\) is current.
  2. Build the candidate pool. Every currently non-empty block is a candidate, plus one fresh empty slot when \(v\)'s current block holds more than one member.
  3. Score every candidate. For each candidate \(s\) in the pool, compute \(\Delta\Sigma(v \to s)\) via the subset-entropy delta: apply on a transient state, recompute only the entropy terms touching the two affected blocks, revert.
  4. Sample, accept or reject. Draw one target \(s\) uniformly from the pool. If \(\Delta\Sigma(v \to s) \le 0\), accept without further draws. Otherwise draw \(u \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1)\) and accept iff \(u < e^{-\beta \Delta\Sigma}\) at \(\beta = 1\).

The acceptance ratio at inverse temperature \(\beta\) is the symmetric MH form

\(\alpha(\boldsymbol{b} \to \boldsymbol{b}') = \min\!\big(1, \, e^{-\beta \, \Delta\Sigma}\big)\),

where \(\Delta\Sigma = \Sigma(\boldsymbol{b}') - \Sigma(\boldsymbol{b})\). In plain terms: any move that lowers \(\Sigma\) is accepted; any move that raises \(\Sigma\) is accepted with probability decaying exponentially in the cost. Two properties. The chain has \(P(\boldsymbol{b} \mid \boldsymbol{A}) \propto e^{-\Sigma(\boldsymbol{b})}\) as its stationary distribution at \(\beta = 1\), so long sweeps sample the posterior; the partitions visited after equilibration are approximately draws from the posterior, not just one mode. And at \(\beta = 0\) the chain becomes a uniform random walk over partitions, at \(\beta \to \infty\) it becomes pure greedy descent; the comdet pages run at \(\beta = 1\) for posterior sampling, but the kernel exposes \(\beta\) as a parameter.

The proposal is the \(c \to \infty\) limit of Peixoto's local-neighbourhood proposal \(P(s \mid v, c)\). The canonical proposal weighs candidates by neighbour-block frequencies under a tunable \(c\) that controls the bias toward local neighbourhoods; at \(c = 1\) the proposal heavily favours blocks the vertex's neighbours already inhabit, at \(c \to \infty\) the weights flatten into uniform. The page kernel uses the limit. In plain terms, the simplification is pedagogical: the uniform proposal exposes the structure of \(\Delta\Sigma\) at every candidate clearly, where the local proposal's bias would obscure why an assortative move is accepted. The uniform proposal is symmetric in the MH sense (every \(\boldsymbol{b} \to \boldsymbol{b}'\) and its reverse have equal proposal probability), so the acceptance ratio reduces to \(e^{-\Delta\Sigma}\) with no Hastings correction.

tie-break at \(\Delta\Sigma = 0\)

The kernel accepts the move at \(\Delta\Sigma \le 0\) without drawing the uniform random sample, which keeps the RNG stream aligned with the cpp tracer at exact-tie boundaries. \(\Delta\Sigma\) is computed via exact lgamma arithmetic, so a true \(\Delta\Sigma = 0\) only arises when the proposed target is the vertex's current block (a no-move, which the kernel detects and short-circuits) or when the move exchanges two structurally equivalent block ids; the non-trivial cases all have \(|\Delta\Sigma| > 0\).

stage 10 · one full sweep

\(N\) visits in shuffled order, one pass through the graph.

A sweep is the unit the equilibration trace plots. The kernel shuffles the identity permutation once at the start of the sweep, then runs the four-step visit on each vertex in that order. The shuffle is independent across sweeps; the same vertex does not get the same neighbours in the visit order from one sweep to the next.

Per-sweep cost is linear in \(N\) plus the total degree (because the subset-entropy delta is closed-form in \(O(\deg(v) + B_{\text{ne}})\) per visit), so the chain scales to graphs of many thousands of vertices. In plain terms: moving vertex \(v\) from block \(r\) to block \(s\) changes only the entropy terms that involve row \(r\), column \(r\), row \(s\), or column \(s\) of the \(\boldsymbol{e}\) matrix, plus the per-block sums; the kernel maintains a running tracker of these quantities and computes the before-after difference directly, never recomputing the full entropy. Two properties. The closed-form delta is exact under the doubled-diagonal convention, so the chain's accept-reject decisions are byte-equal to a full-recompute reference; the kernel's stress-panel verification confirms this across the 17-fixture 3-tier stress panel. And the delta calculation is sensitive to the operand order: the kernel walks the sorted neList prefix in a fixed order so the floating-point sum is deterministic; reordering the operands would introduce sub-ulp drift that on long chains would surface as different tie-break decisions.

The walker at stage 9 already shows one sweep when the reader holds next visit: the visit counter climbs from 1 to 32 and the partition recolours as accepted moves commit. Pressing finish chain jumps to the end of all 20 sweeps, and stage 11 plots the per-sweep summary that results.

stage 11 · equilibration over the chain

\(\Sigma\) per sweep, one trace per variant.

All three chains start from the shared seeded random partition over \(B_0 = 8\) blocks. Each variant evaluates \(\Sigma\) under its own formula, so the absolute values are not directly comparable; what the traces show is whether each variant has converged on the fixture and how fast. Early sweeps see fast drops as obvious moves commit. Later sweeps oscillate as the chain explores partitions of similar quality near the posterior mode.

per-sweep \(\Sigma\) for DC (blue) / NDC (ochre) / PP (green) all traces drawn equal weight

The shape of each trace is informative. DC typically falls steepest in the first three sweeps and plateaus quickly: degree correction already absorbs the hub-vs-leaf gap, so the obvious "snap a node into its neighbour block" moves are abundant and unambiguous. NDC's trace usually has a longer transient because the model has to find a partition that explains the degree variance with block-membership choices, which takes more moves. PP's trace can either drop fastest (when the partition reaches an assortative configuration where intra-block density visibly exceeds inter-block density) or plateau high (when the data does not support a clean two-rate model, as on the disconnected C). On this fixture all three chains usually equilibrate within 10 to 15 sweeps; the page runs 20 to leave headroom.

A still-falling trace at the right edge means the chain has not equilibrated and the sweep count is too low. Rerolling the seed shows the equilibrium \(\Sigma\) is roughly stable but the equilibrium block count wobbles by 1 or 2. The wobble means the model is genuinely uncertain about whether to merge or split a marginal block, which is the expected behaviour of a posterior sampler on a small fixture.

on this fixture, by region

A's \(K_5\)-plus-periphery is the cleanest test case. DC consistently anchors a single A block: the hub-to-leaf degree gap lives in the model, so the partition reads as cohesive even when degrees range from 9 (node 0) to 2 (most periphery leaves). B's bridged \(K_4\)s show DC and NDC keep B as one block (the single \((15, 16)\) bridge reads as evidence the two \(K_4\)s belong together under both data-fit terms); PP also keeps them together because the intra-density bonus exceeds the penalty for one inter-edge. D's two 2-cliques are the seed-sensitive case across all three.

what the verdict label means

The "below by X nats (decisive)" tag translates the pairwise \(\Delta\Sigma\) into Peixoto's Bayes-factor reading. The three variants compete as alternative explanations of the same data. The lower-\(\Sigma\) variant of a pair has a posterior probability \(e^{|\Delta\Sigma|}\) times that of the higher-\(\Sigma\) one. At \(|\Delta\Sigma| < 5\) nats the gap is comparable to within-chain MCMC noise, and reseeding the chain often flips the ranking. At \(|\Delta\Sigma| \ge 10\) nats the gap sits well outside MCMC noise on the comdet fixture, and the same variant tends to come out on top across seeds.

caveat · \(\Sigma\) across variants is not directly comparable

The three variants compute \(\Sigma\) under different priors and different data-fit terms, so the absolute values sit on different scales by construction. The Bayes-factor reading applies pairwise, treating each pair of variants as competing explanations of the same data; it is not an absolute scoring across model classes. What the verdict reports is which partition each model picks at the end of the chain, and how separated the picks are in pairwise nats. On the 32-node fixture the pairwise gap is usually wide enough that the ranking is stable across seeds.

stage 13 · worked example on \(K_3\)

The smallest fixture where all four DC terms are non-zero.

To make the formulas concrete, evaluate \(\Sigma\) on \(K_3\) (the complete graph on three nodes, three edges) under two partitions: everything in one block, and everything in singletons. Use the DC variant for the worked numbers.

Partition 1: \(B = 1\), all three in one block.

\(N = 3\), \(E = 3\), \(B = 1\), \(n_1 = 3\), \(e_{11} = 6\) (doubled diagonal: three edges, each contributes 2), \(e_1 = 6\) (sum of stubs in block 1).

Raw exact entropy: \(\log(e_1!) - \log\!\big((e_{11}/2)!\big) - (e_{11}/2) \log 2\) \(= \log(720) - \log(6) - 3 \log 2\) \(\approx 6.579 - 1.792 - 2.079\) \(\approx 2.708\) nats.

Per-vertex degree constant \(\sum_i \log(k_i!) = 3 \log(2!) \approx 2.079\) nats, subtracted from the DC entropy for cross-variant commensurability.

Net DC sparse entropy: \(2.708 - 2.079 \approx 0.629\) nats.

Edges DL: \(\log\binom{1 + 3 - 1}{3} = \log\binom{3}{3} = 0\) nats (only one cell, no choice).

Partition DL: \(\log(3!) - \log(3!) + \log\binom{2}{0} + \log 3\) \(= 0 + 0 + \log 3\) \(\approx 1.099\) nats.

Degree DL: \(\log\binom{3 + 6 - 1}{6} = \log\binom{8}{6} = \log 28\) \(\approx 3.332\) nats.

Total \(\Sigma_1 \approx 0.629 + 0 + 1.099 + 3.332 = 5.060\) nats.

Partition 2: \(B = 3\), every node alone.

\(N = 3\), \(E = 3\), \(B = 3\), \(n_r = 1\) for every block, \(e_{rr} = 0\) (no internal edges), \(e_{rs} = 1\) for every off-diagonal pair (each edge contributes one to each direction), \(e_r = 2\).

Raw exact entropy: \(3 \log(2!) - 3 \log(1!) - 0 \approx 2.079\) nats (off-diagonal log(1!) terms vanish; diagonals are zero).

Net DC sparse entropy: \(2.079 - 2.079 = 0\) nats (the vertex factor and the degree constant cancel exactly).

Edges DL: \(\log\binom{6 + 3 - 1}{3} = \log\binom{8}{3} = \log 56\) \(\approx 4.025\) nats.

Partition DL: \(\log(3!) - 3 \log(1!) + \log\binom{2}{2} + \log 3\) \(\approx 1.792 + 0 + 0 + 1.099 = 2.891\) nats.

Degree DL: \(3 \log\binom{1 + 2 - 1}{2} = 3 \log\binom{2}{2} = 0\) nats.

Total \(\Sigma_2 \approx 0 + 4.025 + 2.891 + 0 = 6.916\) nats.

\(\Sigma_1 = 5.060\) and \(\Sigma_2 = 6.916\); the gap is \(\Delta\Sigma = -1.86\) nats in favour of the one-block partition. The Bayes factor is \(e^{1.86} \approx 6.4\), inside the inconclusive band: \(K_3\) is too small to support a non-trivial community-structure verdict, and the model prefers the simpler partition. Under NDC the gap shifts (NDC has no degree DL and uses \(e_r \log n_r\) for the vertex factor); under PP it shifts again (PP collapses the matrix into two scalars and the edges DL becomes \(\log 4 \approx 1.39\) nats). The exercise is worth doing once because it makes the four terms concrete: every quantity in the table above traces to a counting problem on \(K_3\).

The example does not surface the resolution limit. On \(K_3\) every partition is small, and the partition DL is the dominant balancing force. On a graph the size of the comdet fixture (\(N = 32\), \(E = 52\)) the sparse entropy term is the largest of the four for DC and NDC, the edges DL becomes non-trivial at \(B > 2\), and the degree DL contributes a few nats per block. The interplay between the terms is what produces the mid-\(B\) optimum.

stage 14 · failure modes

Three places where the flat SBM disagrees with intuition.

The first is the disconnected-block blindspot. C's two 3-cycles share the same ground-truth label even though no edge connects them. Every flat SBM variant splits them: with zero cross-edges the data fit favours a clean partition, and no term in \(\Sigma\) penalises a block that fails to be connected. The connectivity post-processors (CC, WCC, CM) patch this gap as a post-hoc step; the SBM by itself reads "one community" as "one block in a generative model" rather than "one connected component".

The second is the seed-sensitive resolution boundary. D's two 2-cliques sit at the edge of what \(L_p\) and \(S\) trade off. Under some seeds the chain settles on D as one block; under others it splits D into the two 2-cliques. The wobble is posterior uncertainty rather than a numerical artefact: the same seed on the canonical pipeline produces the same kind of wobble. D shows this wobble under all three variants and is the most volatile region of the fixture under reseeding.

The third is convergence under dense fixtures. The page chain runs uniform proposals (the \(c \to \infty\) limit), which is slower than Peixoto's local-neighbourhood proposal on assortative networks. On the comdet fixture 20 sweeps are enough; on a dense network of a few hundred nodes the uniform proposal can leave \(\Sigma\) still drifting at 20 sweeps. The canonical graph-tool pipeline uses \(c = 1\) plus agglomerative merges and reaches the mode in fewer sweeps on big graphs; the trade-off is that the agglomerative merges are harder to walk through one step at a time.

stage 15 · how the SBM family fits the gallery

Five sister algorithms, five different views of "community".

Every comdet gallery page optimises a different objective on the same 32-node fixture. The contrasts are clearest when each algorithm's failure mode is named.

Modularity (Louvain page) scores a partition by edges-inside minus expected edges under a degree-preserving null. The modularity resolution limit (Fortunato and Barthélemy 2007) absorbs small communities into larger ones on graphs with \(\sqrt{2m}\) above the planted-community total degree, which on the comdet fixture puts roughly any community below 10 in degree-sum at risk. The SBM family does not have this resolution limit because the partition prior penalises small \(B\) (one giant block) and large \(B\) (singletons) symmetrically, so the mid-\(B\) optimum is set by the priors rather than tuned with a slider.

CPM (Leiden-CPM page) scores a partition by edges-inside minus a quadratic-in-size penalty controlled by a free resolution parameter \(\gamma\). CPM is resolution-limit-free in the technical sense (Traag et al. 2011): at any \(\gamma\), the optimum partition is exactly the one where average density inside every community is at least \(\gamma\) and between any two communities is at most \(\gamma\). The catch is the free parameter: changing \(\gamma\) by an order of magnitude can shift the optimum from one community per node to one giant block. The SBM family replaces \(\gamma\) with a fixed Bayesian prior; the trade-off between block count and data fit is set by the priors, not by a slider.

Modularity-Leiden (Leiden-Mod page) optimises modularity with Leiden's refinement-aggregation loop. The objective is the same as Louvain and the resolution limit is the same; Leiden is the better local-optimum finder for that objective. The SBM-vs-Leiden contrast is on the objective, not on the search.

Infomap (Infomap page) optimises the map equation, the codelength of a two-level codebook describing a random walk on the graph. Infomap reads "community" as "flow trap"; the SBM reads "community" as "block in a generative model". On directed graphs the two objectives diverge sharply (Infomap's framing is asymmetric, the SBM's is symmetric); on undirected graphs they often agree on assortative structure and disagree on disconnected blocks (Infomap cannot place two disconnected components in one community because the walker cannot cross between them; the SBM can).

IKC, CC, WCC, CM (the IKC, CC, WCC, CM pages) are post-processors. They take any partition and split or merge communities to enforce a structural constraint (k-edge-connectivity, simple connectivity, weak well-connectivity, clique decomposition). The SBM-flat-pp page is post-processable in this sense; the same modes appear under the SBM-flat-pp + CC / WCC / CM chain in the gallery's pipeline scripts. The SBM by itself has no connectivity prior; the post-processors patch this gap.

why "flat-best" instead of a single canonical variant

The flat SBM family is three principled models that differ in what counts as data and what counts as prior, and none of the three is universally right. Peixoto (2017 §VII.A) on an unseen network proposes running all three and picking the lowest \(\Sigma\), treating the choice as a Bayesian model-selection step. The "flat-best" page is that procedure walked end to end. The output is a verdict at three resolutions (inconclusive, suggestive, decisive) plus the three mode partitions side by side, so the reader can see both the ranking and the partitions the ranking is over. Stage 12 is where the verdict appears; stages 1 through 11 lay out the cost terms and the chain so the verdict reads as a summary, not a black-box pick.

stage 16 · the five sibling pages

Where each variant gets its own walker.

The three flat variants have their own walker pages, and the post-processing chain has a fourth. Each page runs the same MCMC walker over its variant-specific entropy, with the per-candidate \(\Delta\Sigma\) panel that this page already shows at stage 9 for the DC chain.

  • SBM-flat-dc. Degree-corrected sparse entropy with the uniform degree DL. The walker page surfaces the per-candidate \(\Delta\Sigma\) on each MH visit, the equilibration trace, and the final mode partition vs. ground-truth.
  • SBM-flat-ndc. The same walker without the degree DL and with the NDC vertex factor \(e_r \log n_r\) in place of \(\log(e_r!)\). The page shows how the hub at node 0 (degree 9) behaves under an exchangeable-within-block prior.
  • SBM-flat-pp. Two-rate Bernoulli likelihood. The walker page shows the chain reaching an assortative configuration where intra-block density exceeds inter-block density. Also the standard SBM input to the gallery's connectivity-enforcing post-processors (CC, WCC, CM).
  • SBM-nested-best. The hierarchical-prior counterpart of this page, comparing the two nested variants (DC and NDC; the nested-PP variant has no canonical counterpart in graph-tool).
