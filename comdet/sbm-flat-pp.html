SBM-flat-pp

planted-partition flat stochastic block model

Per-vertex Metropolis-Hastings over a microcanonical two-rate Bernoulli likelihood. Collapses the per-pair edge matrix into two scalars: intra-block edges and inter-block edges.

Draft. Walker for the PP variant of the flat SBM. The family-level Bayesian framing, the description-length anatomy, and the cross-algorithm contrasts live on the SBM-flat-best anchor page; this page focuses on the PP-specific likelihood and what it does on the 32-node fixture. The JS PP likelihood is the microcanonical two-rate form (Zhang and Peixoto 2020), distinct from graph-tool's PPBlockState (the degree-corrected Peixoto 2018 variant); the cpp tracer mirrors the JS form. Byte-equal to the cpp tracer under matched MT19937 across the 17-fixture 3-tier stress panel.

The planted-partition variant collapses the SBM's full per-pair edge-count matrix \(\boldsymbol{e}\) into two scalars: \(E_{\text{in}}\) is the total intra-block edge count (the sum of \(e_{rr}/2\) over all non-empty blocks), and \(E_{\text{out}}\) is the inter-block count (\(E - E_{\text{in}}\)). In plain terms: instead of asking "how do edges distribute across all \(B(B{+}1)/2\) block pairs", the PP model asks only "how do edges distribute between inside-block and outside-block placements". The DC and NDC sisters (flat-dc, flat-ndc) carry the full matrix; PP is the most parsimonious of the three.

The description length the chain minimises is

\(\Sigma_{\text{PP}}(\boldsymbol{b}) = S_{\text{PP}}(E_{\text{in}}, E_{\text{out}}, M_{\text{in}}, M_{\text{out}}) + L_e^{\text{PP}}(E_{\text{in}}, M_{\text{in}}) + L_p(\boldsymbol{b})\),

where the PP likelihood \(S_{\text{PP}}\) is a sum of two log-binomial terms (one for the intra-block placement, one for the inter-block placement), the PP edges DL \(L_e^{\text{PP}}\) prices the single scalar \(E_{\text{in}}\) under a uniform prior, and the partition DL \(L_p\) is identical to the DC and NDC variants. In plain terms: PP pays much less in the edges DL than DC or NDC do, because there is one number to write down instead of a \(B(B{+}1)/2\)-entry matrix; in exchange, the data-fit term carries a strong inductive bias that the partition is assortative (denser inside than between). Two properties. The PP likelihood is global: it depends on the totals \(E_{\text{in}}\) and \(M_{\text{in}}\) only, not on the per-pair entries; this is what makes the model parsimonious but also what prevents a closed-form subset-entropy delta. And the PP form has no nested counterpart in graph-tool, so the nested-best page compares only the nested-DC and nested-NDC variants.

The PP likelihood explicitly is

\(S_{\text{PP}} = \log\!\binom{M_{\text{in}}}{E_{\text{in}}} + \log\!\binom{M_{\text{out}}}{E_{\text{out}}}\),

where \(M_{\text{in}} = \sum_r \binom{n_r}{2}\) is the total count of intra-block pairs (the slots an internal edge can sit in) and \(M_{\text{out}} = \binom{N}{2} - M_{\text{in}}\) is the count of inter-block pairs. In plain terms: each binomial coefficient counts the distinct ways of placing \(E_{\text{in}}\) edges into \(M_{\text{in}}\) intra-block slots (or \(E_{\text{out}}\) into \(M_{\text{out}}\) inter-block slots); the log gives the bits required to specify the placement under a uniform prior. Two properties. The PP likelihood is the microcanonical two-rate model: at the partition that maximises \(p_{\text{in}} = E_{\text{in}}/M_{\text{in}}\) minus \(p_{\text{out}} = E_{\text{out}}/M_{\text{out}}\), the likelihood is at its peak; any partition that flattens the contrast pays more. And the formula is symmetric under the swap "inside" vs. "outside", but the prior over \(E_{\text{in}}\) breaks the symmetry: the edges DL favours the side with fewer slots (typically inside-block, since most graphs have far more inter-block pairs than intra-block pairs at moderate \(B\)).

The PP edges DL is

\(L_e^{\text{PP}} = \log\!\big(\min(E, M_{\text{in}}) + 1\big)\).

In plain terms: \(E_{\text{in}}\) is an integer between 0 and \(\min(E, M_{\text{in}})\), and a uniform prior over this range pays \(\log(\min(E, M_{\text{in}}) + 1)\) nats. Once \(E_{\text{in}}\) is fixed, \(E_{\text{out}} = E - E_{\text{in}}\) follows deterministically. Two properties. The PP edges DL is almost negligible in absolute size on most graphs (a few nats), where the DC/NDC edges DL grows with \(B^2 \log E\); this is the structural source of PP's parsimony. And the term grows logarithmically with \(\min(E, M_{\text{in}})\), so it never dominates the total \(\Sigma\); the data fit and the partition DL are what move under partition changes.

PP likelihood \(S_{\text{PP}} = \log\binom{M_{\text{in}}}{E_{\text{in}}} + \log\binom{M_{\text{out}}}{E_{\text{out}}}\). The microcanonical two-rate Bernoulli (Zhang and Peixoto 2020).
PP edges DL \(L_e^{\text{PP}} = \log(\min(E, M_{\text{in}}) + 1)\). Uniform prior over the single scalar \(E_{\text{in}}\).
partition DL \(L_p = \log(N!) - \sum_r \log(n_r!) + \log\binom{N - 1}{B - 1} + \log N\). Same as in the DC and NDC variants.
\(p_{\text{in}}, p_{\text{out}}\) the two rates: intra-block density \(p_{\text{in}} = E_{\text{in}}/M_{\text{in}}\), inter-block density \(p_{\text{out}} = E_{\text{out}}/M_{\text{out}}\). The PP model wins when \(p_{\text{in}} \gg p_{\text{out}}\).

The 32-node fixture, painted by planted ground-truth.

The comdet gallery shares one small graph across every algorithm. A (12 nodes, blue) is a \(K_5\) on \(\{0..4\}\) plus a 7-node periphery hanging off the \(K_5\). B (8 nodes, ochre) is two \(K_4\)s bridged by the single edge \((15, 16)\). C (6 nodes, green) is two 3-cycles with no edge between them, both labelled the same in ground-truth. D (4 nodes, plum) is two 2-cliques joined by \((27, 28)\). Outliers 30 and 31 dangle off A.

32 nodes · 52 edges · 4 planted communities + 2 outliers colour = planted ground-truth

What's interesting about this fixture from the PP angle. The fixture is dominantly assortative: A's \(K_5\) is dense (10 internal edges among 5 nodes), B's two \(K_4\)s are dense (6 internal each), and the inter-block edges are sparse. A clean planted-partition split produces \(p_{\text{in}} \approx 0.35\) and \(p_{\text{out}} \approx 0.02\), which is a 17-fold contrast and decisive for the PP likelihood. C is the model's structural challenge: zero edges between two disconnected 3-cycles labelled identically means \(p_{\text{in}}\) is zero on that pair, and PP reads C as two separate blocks. The PP variant is the page in the SBM family that comes closest to the kind of structural reasoning the CC, WCC, and CM post-processors enforce.

stage 1 · random init

Random partition with \(B_0\) blocks; \(\Sigma_0\) on display.

MCMC starts from any valid partition. The chain initialises with \(B_0 = \) 8 blocks: the kernel shuffles the identity permutation under MT19937 and assigns block \(i \bmod B_0\) to vertex \(\sigma(i)\). The shuffle is backward Fisher-Yates, matching the std::shuffle direction in the cpp tracer.

\(\Sigma_0\) is large because the random partition produces near-equal \(p_{\text{in}}\) and \(p_{\text{out}}\): the two Bernoulli rates have no contrast, the likelihood is at its uniform maximum, and the chain still pays the partition DL cost. MCMC's job is to push \(\Sigma\) down by re-routing nodes until the within-block pairs are denser than the between-block pairs.

stage 2 · one move at a time

Each visit: pick a vertex, propose a target block, accept or reject.

One sweep visits every vertex once in shuffled order. Each visit runs four steps:

  1. Build the candidate pool: every currently non-empty block, plus one fresh empty slot when the vertex's current block holds more than one member.
  2. Sample a target uniformly from the pool (the \(c \to \infty\) limit of Peixoto's local-neighbourhood proposal).
  3. Compute \(\Delta\Sigma\) for the move. PP's likelihood depends on the global aggregates \(E_{\text{in}}\), \(M_{\text{in}}\), so the kernel falls back to a full PP recompute rather than the analytic subset path DC and NDC use.
  4. Accept with probability \(\min(1, e^{-\beta\Delta\Sigma})\) at \(\beta = 1\). On accept, commit; on reject, leave the partition unchanged.

The PP variant's lack of a closed-form subset-entropy delta is structural, not an implementation gap. In plain terms: the PP likelihood involves binomial coefficients of the aggregates \(M_{\text{in}}\) and \(E_{\text{in}}\), both of which depend on every block's \(n_r\) sum; moving one vertex shifts both aggregates by a known amount, but the log-binomial does not decompose into a sum of per-block contributions the way DC and NDC do. The kernel computes the full PP likelihood twice (before and after the candidate move) and takes the difference. Two properties. The PP per-visit work is \(O(B_{\text{ne}})\) instead of DC's \(O(\deg(v) + B_{\text{ne}})\); the difference is dominated by the small constant in the binomial computation, so PP is roughly the same wall-clock speed as DC on the comdet fixture. And the PP per-visit \(|\Delta\Sigma|\) values tend to be smaller in absolute size than DC and NDC because the model collapses fine-grained matrix information into two scalars; a move that shifts \(E_{\text{in}}\) by 1 and \(M_{\text{in}}\) by a few has a sub-1-nat effect on the likelihood at most non-trivial partitions.

Moves with \(\Delta\Sigma \le 0\) always commit and the kernel does not draw the uniform random sample (the RNG stream stays aligned with the cpp tracer at exact ties). Moves with \(\Delta\Sigma > 0\) commit with probability exponentially decaying in the cost. Over enough sweeps the chain samples from the posterior \(P(\boldsymbol{b} \mid \boldsymbol{A}) \propto e^{-\Sigma}\); on the 32-node fixture, 20 sweeps reach the mode under most seeds.

on this fixture, by region

A's \(K_5\) plus periphery is the cleanest assortative region: high \(p_{\text{in}}\), close to zero \(p_{\text{out}}\) once isolated. B's two \(K_4\)s split readily under PP because the single bridge edge contributes one edge to \(E_{\text{out}}\) and zero to internal density. C's two 3-cycles split into two blocks since cross-block density is exactly zero. D is the most ambiguous case under PP and the most seed-dependent: cross-edges to A and B push \(p_{\text{out}}\) up, sometimes triggering D's two halves to merge with their neighbour blocks.

PP-flat is the SBM input to the connectivity post-processors

The `run_cd.sh` pipeline pairs SBM-flat-pp with three post-processors that enforce connectivity constraints the SBM does not have: CC splits the partition by simple-connected components, WCC uses a weak well-connectivity threshold, CM decomposes into cliques. The chain runs PP first (which gives a strong assortative partition), then applies the post-processor (which patches the connectivity gaps). On the comdet fixture the SBM-flat-pp + CC chain almost always produces the planted ground-truth, because the CC step splits C's disconnected triangles and the PP step handles the rest. The CC page walks the post-processing step on this same fixture.

caveat · PP form diverges from graph-tool's PPBlockState

The JS kernel implements the Zhang and Peixoto 2020 microcanonical two-rate PP form, with a uniform prior on \(E_{\text{in}}\), partition DL, and uniform-random target proposal. The form diverges from graph-tool's PPBlockState (the Peixoto 2018 degree-corrected PP variant). Byte-equality vs. the cpp tracer holds across the 17-fixture 3-tier stress panel under matched MT19937. Cross-check vs. graph-tool's PP output is on the follow-up; the JS PP partitions shown here are the JS-leg mode under the simpler two-rate form.
