SBM-flat-pp

planted-partition flat stochastic block model

Collapse the full \(\boldsymbol{e}\) matrix into two scalars \(E_{\text{in}}\) and \(E_{\text{out}}\); minimise \(\Sigma\) by single-vertex Metropolis-Hastings.

Draft. Walker over a JS port of the Zhang + Peixoto 2020 planted-partition kernel (10.1103/PhysRevResearch.2.043271), with the partition prior from Peixoto's review (10.1002/9781119483298.ch11) Eq 17. The JS PP likelihood is the microcanonical two-rate form, distinct from graph-tool's PPBlockState (the degree-corrected Peixoto 2018 form); the cpp tracer mirrors the JS form exactly. Proposal is uniform over current blocks (the \(c \to \infty\) limit of sample_block). Byte-equal to the cpp tracer under matched MT19937 across the 17-fixture 3-tier stress panel.

Community detection as Bayesian inference: posit a generative model where each node carries a block label \(b_i \in \{1,\dots,B\}\) and the model freezes the count of edges inside and across blocks. The inference problem is the inverse: given the observed graph, find the partition that compresses it the most.

The microcanonical formulation gives a clean target: the partition that minimises \(\Sigma = -\log P(\boldsymbol{A}, \boldsymbol{e}, \boldsymbol{b})\). Each candidate partition carries a cost in nats, split between data fit and model complexity. The minimum sits in the middle.

The PP variant collapses the full \(B(B+1)/2\)-parameter matrix \(\boldsymbol{e}\) into two scalars: \(E_{\text{in}}\) (the total intra-block edge count) and \(E_{\text{out}}\) (the inter-block count). Likelihood is a uniform draw from the simple graphs that have exactly those two aggregate counts: \(\log\binom{M_{\text{in}}}{E_{\text{in}}} + \log\binom{M_{\text{out}}}{E_{\text{out}}}\), where \(M_{\text{in}} = \sum_r \binom{n_r}{2}\) is the count of intra-block pairs and \(M_{\text{out}} = \binom{N}{2} - M_{\text{in}}\) is the count of inter-block pairs. Strong assortative prior: the partition wins by maximising the contrast between intra-block and inter-block densities. The walker pushes \(\Sigma\) down by single-vertex MH moves: pick a vertex, sample a target block uniformly, compute \(\Delta\Sigma\), accept with \(\min(1, e^{-\beta\Delta\Sigma})\) at \(\beta = 1\). Iterate until \(\Sigma\) stops dropping.

PP likelihood the data-fit term: \(\log\binom{M_{\text{in}}}{E_{\text{in}}} + \log\binom{M_{\text{out}}}{E_{\text{out}}}\). Two-rate Bernoulli; drops when within-block density rises above between-block density.
edges DL (PP) the cost of writing down the two scalars: \(\log(\min(E, M_{\text{in}}) + 1)\), a uniform prior on \(E_{\text{in}}\) (\(E_{\text{out}}\) follows from \(E\)). Almost negligible in absolute size; the model is parsimonious by construction.
partition DL the cost of writing down which node is in which block: \(\log N! - \sum_r \log n_r! + \log\binom{N-1}{B-1} + \log N\). Same Bayesian-hierarchical prior as the DC and NDC variants.
no per-block edge counts the planted-partition model tracks only the two aggregates \(E_{\text{in}}\) and \(E_{\text{out}}\); the per-pair \(e_{rs}\) values are stored internally so that \(\Delta\Sigma\) can be computed in \(O(\deg(v))\), but the entropy collapses everything to the two scalars before scoring.

stage 1 · random init

Random partition with \(B_0\) blocks; \(\Sigma_0\) on display.

MCMC starts from any valid partition. The chain initialises with \(B_0 = \) 8 blocks: the kernel shuffles the identity permutation under MT19937 and assigns block \(i \bmod B_0\) to vertex \(\sigma(i)\). The shuffle is backward Fisher-Yates, matching std::shuffle in the cpp tracer.

\(\Sigma_0\) is large because the random partition produces near-equal \(p_{\text{in}}\) and \(p_{\text{out}}\): the two Bernoulli rates have no contrast, the likelihood is at its uniform maximum, and the chain still pays the partition-DL cost. MCMC's job is to push \(\Sigma\) down by re-routing nodes until the within-block pairs are denser than the between-block pairs.

stage 2 · one move at a time

Each visit: pick a vertex, propose a target block, accept or reject.

One sweep visits every vertex once in shuffled order. Each visit runs four steps:

  1. Build the candidate pool: every currently non-empty block, plus one fresh empty slot when the vertex's current block holds more than one member.
  2. Sample a target uniformly from the pool (the \(c \to \infty\) limit of graph-tool's sample_block; the canonical proposal weights candidates by neighbour-block frequencies).
  3. Compute \(\Delta\Sigma\) for the move via virtualMove. PP's likelihood depends on the global aggregates \(E_{\text{in}}\) + \(M_{\text{in}}\), so the kernel falls back to a full PP recompute rather than the analytic subset path DC and NDC use.
  4. Accept with \(\min(1, e^{-\beta\Delta\Sigma})\) at \(\beta = 1\). On accept, commit; on reject, leave the partition unchanged.

Moves with \(\Delta\Sigma \le 0\) always commit (the RNG draw is skipped to keep the stream aligned with the cpp tracer). Moves with \(\Delta\Sigma > 0\) commit with exponentially-decaying probability, which gives the chain room to walk over saddle points. Over enough sweeps the chain samples from the posterior \(P(\boldsymbol{b} \mid \boldsymbol{A}) \propto e^{-\Sigma}\); on the 32-node fixture, 20 sweeps reaches the mode.

A negative \(\Delta\Sigma\) means the move improves \(\Sigma\); a small positive \(\Delta\Sigma\) is gambler-friendly (likely accepted at \(\beta = 1\)); a large positive \(\Delta\Sigma\) is almost always rejected.

why MH instead of greedy

A pure greedy step (always take the best candidate) collapses to a local minimum quickly; \(\Sigma\) plateaus within one sweep at a partition that is roughly right but stuck. The stochastic acceptance trades a small uphill cost for the freedom to walk over saddle points. Graph-tool's multilevel_mcmc_sweep runs single-vertex MH alongside agglomerative merges that propose joining two whole blocks at once; the merges are what give the canonical sampler its fast convergence on large graphs. The walker on this page uses single-vertex moves only: same algorithm, less plumbing.

caveat · canonical cross-check pending

The JS kernel implements the Zhang + Peixoto 2020 microcanonical PP form, with a uniform prior on \(E_{\text{in}}\), partition-DL, and uniform-random target proposal. The kernel diverges from graph-tool's PPBlockState (the Peixoto 2018 degree-corrected PP variant). Byte-equality vs. the cpp tracer holds across the 17-fixture 3-tier stress panel under MT19937. Cross-check vs. graph-tool's PP output is on the follow-up; treat the partitions shown here as the JS-leg mode under the simpler two-rate Bernoulli form.
