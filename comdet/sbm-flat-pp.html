SBM-flat-pp

planted-partition flat stochastic block model, with CC + WCC + CM chain

Per-vertex Metropolis-Hastings over a microcanonical two-rate Bernoulli likelihood, then the three connectivity post-processors enforce structural constraints the SBM does not have. The page walks both halves of the pipeline on the 32-node fixture.

Draft. Two-part walker. The first half steps through the MCMC chain that produces the SBM-Flat-PP mode partition; the second half feeds that partition through the CC, WCC, and CM post-processors and shows what each one does to it. The family-level Bayesian framing, the description-length anatomy, and the cross-algorithm contrasts live on the SBM-flat-best anchor page. The JS PP likelihood is the microcanonical two-rate form (Zhang and Peixoto 2020), distinct from graph-tool's PPBlockState. The chain end-state (PP → CC → WCC → CM) is what the verification harness uses as one of the four base-algorithm chain inputs alongside Leiden-Mod, Leiden-CPM(0.5), and Infomap.

The planted-partition variant collapses the SBM's full per-pair edge-count matrix \(\boldsymbol{e}\) into two scalars: \(E_{\text{in}}\) is the total intra-block edge count (the sum of \(e_{rr}/2\) over all non-empty blocks), and \(E_{\text{out}}\) is the inter-block count (\(E - E_{\text{in}}\)). In plain terms: instead of asking "how do edges distribute across all \(B(B{+}1)/2\) block pairs", the PP model asks only "how do edges distribute between inside-block and outside-block placements". The DC and NDC sisters (flat-dc, flat-ndc) carry the full matrix; PP is the most parsimonious of the three.

The description length the chain minimises is

\(\Sigma_{\text{PP}}(\boldsymbol{b}) = S_{\text{PP}}(E_{\text{in}}, E_{\text{out}}, M_{\text{in}}, M_{\text{out}}) + L_e^{\text{PP}}(E_{\text{in}}, M_{\text{in}}) + L_p(\boldsymbol{b})\),

where the PP likelihood \(S_{\text{PP}}\) is a sum of two log-binomial terms (one for the intra-block placement, one for the inter-block placement), the PP edges DL \(L_e^{\text{PP}}\) prices the single scalar \(E_{\text{in}}\) under a uniform prior, and the partition DL \(L_p\) is identical to the DC and NDC variants. In plain terms: PP pays much less in the edges DL than DC or NDC do, because there is one number to write down instead of a \(B(B{+}1)/2\)-entry matrix; in exchange, the data-fit term carries a strong inductive bias that the partition is assortative (denser inside than between). Two properties. The PP likelihood is global: it depends on the totals \(E_{\text{in}}\) and \(M_{\text{in}}\) only, not on the per-pair entries; this is what makes the model parsimonious but also what prevents a closed-form subset-entropy delta. And the PP form has no nested counterpart in graph-tool, so the nested-best page compares only the nested-DC and nested-NDC variants.

The PP likelihood explicitly is

\(S_{\text{PP}} = \log\!\binom{M_{\text{in}}}{E_{\text{in}}} + \log\!\binom{M_{\text{out}}}{E_{\text{out}}}\),

where \(M_{\text{in}} = \sum_r \binom{n_r}{2}\) is the total count of intra-block pairs (the slots an internal edge can sit in) and \(M_{\text{out}} = \binom{N}{2} - M_{\text{in}}\) is the count of inter-block pairs. In plain terms: each binomial coefficient counts the distinct ways of placing \(E_{\text{in}}\) edges into \(M_{\text{in}}\) intra-block slots (or \(E_{\text{out}}\) into \(M_{\text{out}}\) inter-block slots); the log gives the bits required to specify the placement under a uniform prior. Two properties. The PP likelihood is the microcanonical two-rate model: at the partition that maximises \(p_{\text{in}} = E_{\text{in}}/M_{\text{in}}\) minus \(p_{\text{out}} = E_{\text{out}}/M_{\text{out}}\), the likelihood is at its peak; any partition that flattens the contrast pays more. And the formula is symmetric under the swap "inside" vs. "outside", but the prior over \(E_{\text{in}}\) breaks the symmetry: the edges DL favours the side with fewer slots (typically inside-block, since most graphs have far more inter-block pairs than intra-block pairs at moderate \(B\)).

The PP edges DL is

\(L_e^{\text{PP}} = \log\!\big(\min(E, M_{\text{in}}) + 1\big)\).

In plain terms: \(E_{\text{in}}\) is an integer between 0 and \(\min(E, M_{\text{in}})\), and a uniform prior over this range pays \(\log(\min(E, M_{\text{in}}) + 1)\) nats. Once \(E_{\text{in}}\) is fixed, \(E_{\text{out}} = E - E_{\text{in}}\) follows deterministically. Two properties. The PP edges DL is almost negligible in absolute size on most graphs (a few nats), where the DC/NDC edges DL grows with \(B^2 \log E\); this is the structural source of PP's parsimony. And the term grows logarithmically with \(\min(E, M_{\text{in}})\), so it never dominates the total \(\Sigma\); the data fit and the partition DL are what move under partition changes.

PP likelihood \(S_{\text{PP}} = \log\binom{M_{\text{in}}}{E_{\text{in}}} + \log\binom{M_{\text{out}}}{E_{\text{out}}}\). The microcanonical two-rate Bernoulli (Zhang and Peixoto 2020).
PP edges DL \(L_e^{\text{PP}} = \log(\min(E, M_{\text{in}}) + 1)\). Uniform prior over the single scalar \(E_{\text{in}}\).
partition DL \(L_p = \log(N!) - \sum_r \log(n_r!) + \log\binom{N - 1}{B - 1} + \log N\). Same as in the DC and NDC variants.
\(p_{\text{in}}, p_{\text{out}}\) the two rates: intra-block density \(p_{\text{in}} = E_{\text{in}}/M_{\text{in}}\), inter-block density \(p_{\text{out}} = E_{\text{out}}/M_{\text{out}}\). The PP model wins when \(p_{\text{in}} \gg p_{\text{out}}\).
post-proc chain the three connectivity post-processors run in sequence on PP's mode partition: CC splits each cluster into its connected components; WCC recursively cuts clusters whose mincut sits below \(\log_{10}(n)\); CM re-clusters each split half via Leiden. The chain's job is to patch the connectivity gaps the SBM does not have a prior for.

B (8 nodes, ochre) is two \(K_4\)s bridged by the single edge \((15, 16)\). C (6 nodes, green) is two 3-cycles with no edge between them, both labelled the same in ground-truth. 32 nodes · 52 edges · 4 planted communities + 2 outliers colour = planted ground-truth

What's interesting about this fixture from the PP angle. The fixture is dominantly assortative: A's \(K_5\) is dense (10 internal edges among 5 nodes), B's two \(K_4\)s are dense (6 internal each), and the inter-block edges are sparse. A clean planted-partition split produces \(p_{\text{in}} \approx 0.35\) and \(p_{\text{out}} \approx 0.02\), which is a 17-fold contrast and decisive for the PP likelihood. C is the model's structural challenge: zero edges between two disconnected 3-cycles labelled identically means \(p_{\text{in}}\) is zero on that pair, and PP reads C as two separate blocks. The PP variant is the page in the SBM family that comes closest to the kind of structural reasoning the CC, WCC, and CM post-processors enforce.

Random partition with \(B_0\) blocks; \(\Sigma_0\) on display.

MCMC starts from any valid partition. The chain initialises with \(B_0 = \) 8 blocks: the kernel shuffles the identity permutation under MT19937 and assigns block \(i \bmod B_0\) to vertex \(\sigma(i)\). The shuffle is backward Fisher-Yates, matching the std::shuffle direction in the cpp tracer.

\(\Sigma_0\) is large because the random partition produces near-equal \(p_{\text{in}}\) and \(p_{\text{out}}\): the two Bernoulli rates have no contrast, the likelihood is at its uniform maximum, and the chain still pays the partition DL cost. MCMC's job is to push \(\Sigma\) down by re-routing nodes until the within-block pairs are denser than the between-block pairs.

stage 2 · one move at a time

Each visit: pick a vertex, propose a target block, accept or reject.

One sweep visits every vertex once in shuffled order. Each visit runs four steps:

  1. Build the candidate pool: every currently non-empty block, plus one fresh empty slot when the vertex's current block holds more than one member.
  2. Sample a target uniformly from the pool (the \(c \to \infty\) limit of Peixoto's local-neighbourhood proposal).
  3. Compute \(\Delta\Sigma\) for the move. PP's likelihood depends on the global aggregates \(E_{\text{in}}\), \(M_{\text{in}}\), so the kernel falls back to a full PP recompute rather than the analytic subset path DC and NDC use.
  4. Accept with probability \(\min(1, e^{-\beta\Delta\Sigma})\) at \(\beta = 1\). On accept, commit; on reject, leave the partition unchanged.

The PP variant's lack of a closed-form subset-entropy delta is structural, not an implementation gap. In plain terms: the PP likelihood involves binomial coefficients of the aggregates \(M_{\text{in}}\) and \(E_{\text{in}}\), both of which depend on every block's \(n_r\) sum; moving one vertex shifts both aggregates by a known amount, but the log-binomial does not decompose into a sum of per-block contributions the way DC and NDC do. The kernel computes the full PP likelihood twice (before and after the candidate move) and takes the difference. Two properties. The PP per-visit work is \(O(B_{\text{ne}})\) instead of DC's \(O(\deg(v) + B_{\text{ne}})\); the difference is dominated by the small constant in the binomial computation, so PP is roughly the same wall-clock speed as DC on the comdet fixture. And the PP per-visit \(|\Delta\Sigma|\) values tend to be smaller in absolute size than DC and NDC because the model collapses fine-grained matrix information into two scalars; a move that shifts \(E_{\text{in}}\) by 1 and \(M_{\text{in}}\) by a few has a sub-1-nat effect on the likelihood at most non-trivial partitions.

Moves with \(\Delta\Sigma \le 0\) always commit and the kernel does not draw the uniform random sample (the RNG stream stays aligned with the cpp tracer at exact ties). Moves with \(\Delta\Sigma > 0\) commit with probability exponentially decaying in the cost. Over enough sweeps the chain samples from the posterior \(P(\boldsymbol{b} \mid \boldsymbol{A}) \propto e^{-\Sigma}\); on the 32-node fixture, 20 sweeps reach the mode under most seeds.

stage 5 · why post-process the SBM output

The SBM optimises description length, not connectivity.

The PP likelihood reads "denser inside than outside" through two scalars \(E_{\text{in}}\) and \(E_{\text{out}}\). The aggregates do not record which inside-block pairs are actually adjacent; the two-rate Bernoulli treats every pair inside a block as a slot, occupied with rate \(p_{\text{in}}\). A block whose internal subgraph is two unconnected halves has the same \(p_{\text{in}}\) as a block whose internal subgraph is one well-connected piece of the same size and edge count; PP cannot tell them apart. That is the structural gap.

The connectivity post-processors restore the missing prior. Each one defines a structural test that every output cluster has to clear, and recursively cuts any cluster that fails. The three tests sit on a ladder of strictness:

stage 0 · inputSBM-Flat-PP mode partition. Each block is dense in expectation, no structural check.
stage A · CCcluster must be connected. Split into BFS components, drop singletons.
stage B · WCCcluster mincut must exceed \(\log_{10}(n)\). Recursively cut and repeat.
stage C · CMsame test as WCC, plus Leiden re-cluster on each cut half before recursing.

Two interesting properties. The chain is monotone in strictness: every cluster that passes CM also passes WCC and CC, and every cluster that passes WCC also passes CC; what changes is how many clusters the test rejects and how the cuts compose. And each step writes its own output partition; the chain is not a single composite filter but three labelled passes whose results can be read separately to attribute the patch to a specific test.

On the 32-node fixture the chain has the easiest possible time: every cluster is small enough that the log threshold sits below most realistic mincut values, and Leiden has little room to re-shape a 10-node induced subgraph. The pedagogy stages below are deliberately small so every cut is hand-checkable; the verification harness exercises the same chain on networks up to ~23k nodes where the post-processors do non-trivial work.

stage A · CC pass

Split each cluster into its connected components.

The cheapest of the three. For each cluster in the PP mode partition, take the subgraph induced by that cluster's nodes (drop every edge crossing out to a different cluster), run a breadth-first search, replace the cluster with one output cluster per connected piece. The cost is \(O(|V| + |E|)\) over the whole graph; no parameter to tune; no model to fit. The CC page walks the per-frame mechanics.

On the PP output: the test fires whenever a PP block is internally disconnected, which on the 32-node fixture is the C region case. PP's likelihood happily packs the two disjoint 3-cycles under one label because the two-rate Bernoulli is blind to the missing density. CC's BFS sees the gap immediately and emits two clusters where the SBM emitted one.

Two properties of this step worth noting. CC never merges clusters: if PP under-splits a community (one ground-truth piece labelled as two PP blocks), CC cannot undo it. And CC drops singleton components from the output: a node whose only intra-cluster neighbours sit in different connected pieces ends up unlabelled.

stage B · WCC pass

Each cluster's mincut must clear \(\log_{10}(n)\), or it gets recursively cut.

WCC turns "is the cluster connected" into "is the cluster well-connected". The test runs VieCut's cactus mincut on each survivor of the CC pass and compares the cut value to \(f(n) = \log_{10}(n)\). On pass, the cluster moves to the survivor list. On fail, the cluster is cut at the most-balanced minimum cut and each connected piece of the two halves pushes back into the queue. The recursion terminates because every push-back component is strictly smaller than the popped cluster. The WCC page walks the per-pop mechanics and the threshold-family tradeoffs.

On the PP output, the test fires whenever a cluster is connected but only just: a thin bridge or a pendant pulls the mincut down to 1, which fails the threshold for any cluster of size 10 or larger. On the 32-node fixture the typical PP mode partition contains one large block over A (the \(K_5\) core plus its 7-node periphery). The pendants in A are 1-edge cuts; WCC peels them off one at a time until the threshold drops below 1.

Two properties. WCC's threshold is local to the cluster's size, not the graph's size: a 10-node cluster faces the same bar whether it sits in a 32-node graph or a 32-million-node graph, which is what makes the pass composable on top of any upstream clustering. And the strict-above comparison treats equality as a fail: at \(n = 10\) the threshold lands exactly at 1.000, so a mincut of 1 fails by tolerance band rather than by inequality.

stage C · CM pass

Same threshold as WCC, plus Leiden replay on every failing side.

CM is the heaviest of the three. The threshold check matches WCC's exactly; the difference is what happens after a fail. WCC pushes the two cut halves directly back to the queue. CM instead hands each side to Leiden CPM (resolution \(r = 0.0001\) by default), runs two iterations, and pushes whatever sub-communities Leiden returns. The implication: one failing cluster can spawn many children, not just two, and the lineage tree branches accordingly. The CM page walks the round structure, the lineage tree, and the four cluster-fate labels.

On the PP output: CM and WCC produce the same partition when Leiden at \(r = 0.0001\) returns one community on every cut side it sees. That is the default case on the 32-node fixture, because Leiden's CPM at near-zero resolution prefers a single community on any small dense subgraph. Raising the resolution past about \(r = 0.1\) makes Leiden find multiple sub-communities on the 11-node residual of A, and the CM and WCC outputs diverge.

Two properties. CM's re-cluster step is local: Leiden runs on the induced subgraph of one cut half, not the whole graph, so the inner optimisation has no notion of the rest of the partition. And the inner Leiden RNG is re-seeded to 0 at every call, so two re-cluster runs on identical induced subgraphs produce identical sub-partitions regardless of where they sit in the pop sequence; this is what makes the chain deterministic given the input partition.

what the chain buys on this fixture

The chain's win is mostly in the CC step. CC catches the disconnected-C case, which is PP's structural blindspot; the NMI and ARI both jump at that step. WCC peels A's pendants; the NMI shifts by a smaller amount because the peeled nodes drop out of the comparison rather than getting re-labelled. CM matches WCC here because Leiden CPM at \(r = 0.0001\) returns one community on every side, so the re-cluster step is a no-op. On larger graphs where Leiden has room to find sub-communities, CM and WCC diverge.

stage 10 · why the chain order is CC → WCC → CM

The three tests are not commutative.

The pipeline order is CC first, then WCC, then CM. Each step's invariants depend on the previous step's invariants holding. Reversing any pair breaks something.

chain orderwhat breaks
CC → WCC → CM the shipped order. CC produces connected clusters; WCC runs mincut on connected inputs (mincut is undefined on a disconnected graph; running it returns 0 with an arbitrary bipartition); CM runs Leiden on connected, mincut-cleared inputs.
WCC → CC WCC's mincut would return 0 with an arbitrary bipartition on any disconnected input cluster. The "cut" would peel one disjoint piece off and treat the other as a normal cluster; the bipartition is meaningless. WCC's own implementation runs CC as its pre-pass for exactly this reason; reversing the order strips the safety net.
CM → WCC Leiden runs on the SBM output before any connectivity filter. Leiden's CPM at low resolution can absorb a thin-bridge community back into a larger one, which undoes the structural information WCC's threshold relies on. The post-proc gain shrinks; on benchmarks the inversion measurably loses NMI versus the shipped order.
CC → CM → WCC CM's Leiden replay can produce sub-communities with low mincut that the subsequent WCC would then shred. The shipped order folds the WCC test into the CM loop itself (CM checks the threshold per pop, then re-clusters on fail), so the test runs at the right granularity.

The shipped order is the only one that respects all three tests' preconditions. In plain terms: CC's job is to feed WCC well-defined inputs; WCC's job is to fail well-defined clusters and let CM's Leiden replay choose what to do with the failing sides; CM's job is to run that replay under the same threshold WCC enforces, so the chain's output is consistent at every step.

stage 11 · sister variants do not chain through CC/WCC/CM

SBM-flat-best, flat-dc, flat-ndc emit raw partitions; this page is the post-processed leg.

The flat SBM family runs three variants under one MCMC chain (DC, NDC, PP) and reports the lowest-\(\Sigma\) partition on the flat-best anchor page. None of the three sister variants pipes its output through CC, WCC, or CM by default. The SBM-Flat-PP variant is paired with the chain in the verification harness because PP's structural blindspot (it cannot see internal connectivity) is the cleanest case for the post-processors to patch.

variantraw partitionpost-proc chain
flat-best lowest-\(\Sigma\) of DC, NDC, PP not in the harness; the anchor page focuses on Bayes-factor model selection across the three variants.
flat-dc degree-corrected sparse entropy + degree DL not in the harness; DC's data-fit term absorbs degree variance under the existing prior, so the post-proc gain is smaller in expectation.
flat-ndc non-degree-corrected sparse entropy not in the harness; NDC's failure mode is hub-peeling rather than connectivity, which is a different patch.
flat-pp (this page) microcanonical two-rate Bernoulli shipped. PP → CC → WCC → CM is one of the four base-algorithm chain inputs in the verification harness; the others are Leiden-Mod, Leiden-CPM(0.5), and Infomap.

The reason PP is the SBM variant that pairs with the chain is structural, not historical. PP's likelihood depends on global aggregates only, so a partition that lines up assortative density wins regardless of whether the inside-block subgraph is one piece or many. DC and NDC carry the per-pair edge matrix; a partition that under-connects an inside block pays in the data-fit term, which gives those variants a partial structural prior the chain duplicates. PP has no such partial prior, so the chain's job is unambiguous on PP output and the patch is cleaner to attribute.
