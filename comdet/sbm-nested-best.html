SBM-nested-best

nested (hierarchical) stochastic block model, lowest description length

Two nested SBM variants (DC and NDC) run from one shared seeded init on the 32-node fixture; pairwise \(\Delta\Sigma\) ranked under Peixoto's Bayes-factor reading.

Draft, level-0 walker only. Anchor page for the nested SBM family. The kernel composes the full multi-level \(e_{rs}\) propagation end-to-end and the cpp tracer plus JS replay leg exercise the hierarchy under self-RNG (306/306 cells PASS on the 3-tier stress panel); this page surfaces only the level-0 comparison. The planted-partition (PP) variant has no nested counterpart in graph-tool and is omitted. Verdict labels follow Peixoto 2017 §VII.A's Bayes-factor reading (\(|\Delta\Sigma| < 5\) nats inconclusive, \(5\) to \(10\) suggestive, beyond \(10\) decisive).

The nested stochastic block model stacks flat blockmodels into a hierarchy. Level 0 is the input graph plus its block partition, exactly as in the flat SBM family. Level 1 is the block-multigraph obtained by collapsing level 0's blocks into super-vertices: the vertices of the level-1 graph are the non-empty level-0 blocks, the edges carry the level-0 \(e_{rs}\) counts as weights, and self-loops on the level-1 graph carry \(e_{rr}/2\) per the doubled-diagonal convention. Level 1 has its own block partition. The construction repeats at level 2, 3, and so on, until the top level has one block or two.

The hierarchy buys two things. First, an asymptotic resolution-limit improvement. The flat SBM under the uniform-edge-count prior has \(B_{\max} \propto \sqrt{N}\) (Peixoto 2017 §VI, Eq 27); on a graph the size of this comdet fixture, \(\sqrt{32} \approx 5.7\), so the flat model can already represent the 4-community ground-truth cleanly. On large sparse graphs the flat-SBM resolution limit becomes a real obstacle. The nested prior replaces the quadratic-in-\(B\) penalty with a stack of priors at every level, and the asymptotic limit lifts to \(B_{\max} \propto N / \log N\). Second, the hierarchy makes the partition structurally interpretable: the higher levels are coarser groupings of lower-level blocks, so the inference returns not just a single partition but a tree of partitions at multiple scales.

The objective is still the description length, but summed across levels:

\(\Sigma_{\text{nested}}(\boldsymbol{b}^{(0)}, \boldsymbol{b}^{(1)}, \ldots, \boldsymbol{b}^{(L-1)}) = \sum_{l = 0}^{L - 1} \Sigma_l(\boldsymbol{b}^{(l)} ; G^{(l)})\),

where \(G^{(0)}\) is the input graph and \(G^{(l)}\) for \(l > 0\) is the block-multigraph from level \(l - 1\). In plain terms: pay the flat-SBM cost at every level, where each level's "graph" is the collapsed version from below. Two properties worth keeping. The total decomposes per level, so a per-level MCMC move only changes the level it touches (plus, via the rebuild path, the upper levels' graphs); the chain can be run as a round-robin over levels with deterministic upper-level rebuilds. And the resolution-limit improvement is asymptotic: on small graphs the level-1+ contributions are tiny in absolute nats, and the flat and nested mode partitions usually coincide at level 0.

Two principled variants for the nested family. The degree-corrected (DC) variant pays the degree DL at level 0 and uses NDC sparse entropy at every upper level (the level-\(l > 0\) graphs are weighted multigraphs with no separate degree sequence, so degree correction is a level-0-only concept). The non-degree-corrected (NDC) variant uses NDC entropy at every level. The PP variant has no nested form in graph-tool: PP collapses the per-pair matrix into two scalars at level 0, which leaves no \(e_{rs}\) for the level-1 graph to inherit; the nested construction breaks down on the first rebuild.

DC degree-corrected level-0 sparse entropy plus uniform degree DL; NDC sparse entropy at every level above. Survives heavy-tailed degree distributions on the input graph.
NDC non-degree-corrected sparse entropy at every level. The block-multigraph view does not depend on per-node degrees at any level.
level-\(l\) graph the multigraph where vertices are the non-empty level-\((l-1)\) blocks (renumbered \(0 \ldots B_{\text{ne}} - 1\)), edges carry the level-\((l-1)\) \(e_{rs}\) counts, and self-loops carry \(e_{rr}/2\).
\(\Sigma_{\text{nested}}\) sum of per-level description lengths. Lower is better. Self-consistent across levels (no inter-level coupling term).

On this fixture the asymptotic resolution-limit benefit of the nested form is mostly cosmetic. \(\sqrt{32} \approx 5.7\), so the flat SBM has roughly six representable blocks before the partition DL term overwhelms the data-fit gains. The 4-community ground-truth fits inside that budget. The nested formulation walks the same trajectory on this small fixture and produces a structurally interpretable two-level partition where the flat model produces one.

stage 1 · the level stack

One graph per level, one block partition per level.

The nested kernel maintains a list of flat block states, one per level. Level 0 wraps the input graph; level \(l > 0\) wraps the block-multigraph obtained from level \(l - 1\). Each state is a fully self-contained flat block model with its own entropy formula, its own partition, and its own MCMC sweep.

level 0 input graph (\(N_0 = 32\), \(E_0 = 52\)) with the user-supplied entropy mode (DC or NDC) walked one MH move at a time below
level 1 non-empty level-0 blocks become vertices; level-0 \(e_{rs}\) counts become weighted edges; self-loops carry \(e_{rr}/2\). NDC entropy at this and every higher level. rebuilt after each level-0 sweep
level 2 same construction over level-1 blocks. rebuilt after each level-1 sweep
level L-1 top level. Typically one or two blocks. no rebuild above; chain ends here

The level-1 graph is a weighted multigraph, not a simple graph. A single edge in the level-1 graph between vertices \(r_1\) and \(r_2\) carries the weight \(e^{(0)}_{r_1, r_2}\) (the number of level-0 edges from block \(r_1\) to block \(r_2\)); self-loops on the level-1 graph at vertex \(r\) carry the weight \(e^{(0)}_{rr} / 2\). In plain terms: every edge in the level-0 graph is preserved as a weight contribution in the level-1 graph, but the structure is compressed to one vertex per non-empty level-0 block. Two properties. The total weight of the level-1 graph equals the total weight of the level-0 graph (no edges are lost in the collapse), so \(E^{(l)}\) is conserved across the level transition. And the level-1 NDC entropy reads the level-0 partition coarsely: blocks within a level-1 group are super-blocks of level-0 blocks, with the same hierarchical interpretation as in any other hierarchical clustering scheme.

The level-1+ entropy formula is always NDC. The reason is structural: the level-1 graph is a multigraph over level-0 blocks, and the "degree sequence" of a level-1 vertex is the level-0 block's total edge weight \(e^{(0)}_r\), which is part of the level-0 model rather than an independent quantity at level 1. Degree correction at the higher levels would double-count. Graph-tool's canonical NestedBlockState makes the same choice (every level above 0 is NDC).

The level transitions follow the doubled-diagonal convention from level 0. A level-0 self-loop at vertex \(v\) contributes \(2 w\) to \(e^{(0)}_{b_v, b_v}\); the level-1 graph then sees a self-loop at the super-vertex with weight \(e^{(0)}_{b_v, b_v} / 2 = w\). In plain terms: the convention preserves the total internal-edge count exactly through every level transition. Two properties. The convention is what allows the same flat-BlockState code to handle both the level-0 graph and the level-1+ multigraph without separate code paths. And the diagonal halving on the rebuild is the only place where the convention is visible; everywhere else the level-1+ states behave as ordinary flat block models.

stage 2 · per-level description-length terms

The nested \(\Sigma\) is a sum of per-level flat \(\Sigma_l\).

The nested description length is

\(\Sigma_{\text{nested}} = \sum_{l = 0}^{L - 1} \Big[ S_l(\boldsymbol{e}^{(l)}, \boldsymbol{b}^{(l)}) + L_e(B^{(l)}, E^{(l)}) + L_p^{(l)}(\boldsymbol{b}^{(l)}) + [l = 0]\, L_d \Big]\),

where \([l = 0]\) is the Iverson bracket. In plain terms, every level pays the same three or four terms a flat SBM pays, computed on that level's graph and that level's partition. The degree DL only fires at level 0 because higher levels work on multigraphs with no separate degree sequence. Two properties. The decomposition is fully additive across levels, with no cross-level coupling term, which is what makes per-level MCMC well-defined; graph-tool's canonical adds a small \(\Lambda_{\text{rec dx}}\) coupling between levels that JS does not port (the pages' walker is byte-equal to the cpp tracer, which mirrors the JS-side self-consistent sum; this is the sanctioned divergence). And the absolute \(\Sigma_{\text{nested}}\) is always larger than the corresponding flat \(\Sigma_0\) on a non-trivial hierarchy, because the level-1+ terms are positive (they pay extra DL for the hierarchy itself), but the gain from the resolution-limit improvement on large \(B\) is asymptotically much larger.

termformulaactive at levelplain-English read
sparse entropy level 0: DC or NDC per user. level \(l > 0\): NDC always. every level data-fit cost at this level given the level's partition
edges DL \(L_e^{(l)} = \log\!\binom{B^{(l)}(B^{(l)}{+}1)/2 + E^{(l)} - 1}{E^{(l)}}\) every level cost of writing the level's \(\boldsymbol{e}^{(l)}\) matrix once \(B^{(l)}\) is known
partition DL \(L_p^{(l)} = \log(N^{(l)}!) - \sum_r \log(n_r^{(l)}!) + \log\!\binom{N^{(l)} - 1}{B^{(l)} - 1} + \log N^{(l)}\) every level cost of writing the level's labelling
degree DL (uniform) \(L_d = \sum_r \log\!\binom{n_r + e_r - 1}{e_r}\) level 0 only, DC variant only cost of per-node degrees given block sums at level 0

The level-1+ edges DL is computed on the multigraph total weight. In plain terms: the level-1 graph has weighted edges, and the edges DL counts the integer compositions of the total weight \(E^{(1)} = \sum_{r_1, r_2} e^{(0)}_{r_1, r_2} / 2\) over the level-1 cell count. The kernel uses the raw edge-weight sum (\(E^{(l)} = \sum_e w_e\)), not the stub-pair sum, so the formula matches Peixoto Eq 23 at every level. Two properties. The level-1 edges DL is typically much smaller in nats than the level-0 edges DL because the level-1 cell count drops as the partition coarsens. And the term is what penalises overly fine hierarchies: a 4-level partition of a 32-node graph would pay edges DL at all four levels, but the level-3 graph is so small that the contribution shrinks below 1 nat.

The level-1+ partition DL similarly costs the labelling at each level. The size of the level-\(l\) "graph" \(N^{(l)} = B^{(l-1)}_{\text{ne}}\), so the partition DL at level \(l\) scales with \(\log B^{(l-1)}_{\text{ne}}!\). In plain terms: every level pays for its labels, and the higher levels pay less in absolute nats because the graph has fewer vertices. Two properties. The cascade of partition DLs is the structural cost of the hierarchy; it is non-zero even if every level above 0 has trivial structure. And the level-0 partition DL is unchanged from the flat-SBM formula; the higher levels are pure additions.

stage 3 · round-robin MCMC across levels

Each sweep visits every level in turn.

The nested MCMC chain is a round-robin over levels. One outer iteration runs one sweep at every level in order; the upper levels rebuild their graphs after each lower-level sweep.

The level-0 sweep is the standard MH sweep: shuffled visit order, per-vertex uniform proposal, single-vertex acceptance ratio. Each accepted move updates the level-0 \(\boldsymbol{e}^{(0)}\) matrix in place. After the sweep completes, the level-1 graph is rebuilt from the updated \(\boldsymbol{e}^{(0)}\): vertices are the non-empty level-0 blocks renumbered \(0 \ldots B^{(0)}_{\text{ne}} - 1\), edges carry the updated \(e^{(0)}_{rs}\) counts, self-loops carry \(e^{(0)}_{rr}/2\). The level-1 state is re-initialised with the parent's level-1 membership remapped to the new vertex ids. The level-1 sweep then runs an MH sweep on this rebuilt graph; the cycle repeats up to level \(L - 1\).

The rebuild is deterministic given the level-0 \(\boldsymbol{e}\) matrix. In plain terms: the only stochastic step in the nested chain is the per-level MH sweep; the level transitions are pure aggregations. Two properties. The rebuild is O(\(N^{(l)}\)) work per level transition, dominated by walking the level-\((l-1)\) edge list and binning into the level-\(l\) edge map; the total per-iteration cost is O(\(N + \sum_l N^{(l)}\)) which on graphs with rapidly shrinking levels reduces to O(\(N\)). And the rebuild preserves the doubled-diagonal convention: self-loops on the level-\(l\) graph carry the halved \(e^{(l-1)}_{rr}/2\) so that the level-\(l\) BlockState's internal doubling on load reproduces the original level-\((l-1)\) diagonal count.

The level-0 walker on the sister nested-dc and nested-ndc pages animates only the level-0 sweep. The kernel composes the full multi-level chain, and the cpp tracer plus JS replay leg exercise the full round-robin under self-RNG, but surfacing the upper levels in the page UI is on the follow-up. The walker's level-0 trajectory is identical to the corresponding flat walker; the difference is purely in the entropy computation (the nested-best chain evaluates \(\Sigma_{\text{nested}}\), the flat-best chain evaluates \(\Sigma_{\text{flat}}\)).

the resolution-limit lift is visible asymptotically, not here

The asymptotic claim \(B_{\max} \propto N / \log N\) for nested vs. \(\sqrt{N}\) for flat applies when the optimal block count grows with \(N\). On the comdet fixture (\(N = 32\)) both bounds are above the 4-community ground-truth, so the flat and nested mode partitions usually coincide at level 0. On a graph with \(N = 10^4\) and a planted block count of \(100\), the flat model is at \(\sqrt{N} \approx 100\) (right at the limit, often falling short); the nested model is at \(N/\log N \approx 1086\) (comfortable headroom). The page is the structural reference for the algorithm; the resolution-limit benefit shows up on real-world networks of the kind the gallery's stress panel covers.

stage 6 · what the hierarchy is for

Three uses the flat SBM cannot offer.

The hierarchy is more than a resolution-limit fix. Three practical uses are worth naming.

First, multi-scale interpretation. The flat SBM returns a partition. The nested SBM returns a tree of partitions: every level-1 block is a super-block of level-0 blocks; every level-2 block is a super-super-block. The hierarchy is what allows reading the partition at multiple scales without re-running the inference. On the comdet fixture the level-1 partition often groups A and D into one super-block, capturing the cross-edges between them; on a real-world network this might correspond to grouping departments within a faculty, or municipalities within a region.

Second, automatic model-complexity control on large graphs. The flat SBM's partition DL grows as \(B(B{+}1)/2\) edges DL terms plus the \(\log\binom{N - 1}{B - 1}\) sub-term, both of which scale roughly quadratically in \(B\). On a graph with \(N = 10^5\), a flat partition with \(B = 300\) pays around \(10^4\) nats in partition + edges DL alone, which is enough to keep the optimum at much smaller \(B\). The nested partition pays a tower of smaller DLs at every level, summing to a much smaller total for the same effective block count.

Third, faster MCMC mixing on assortative networks. The level-1+ MCMC moves can shift super-blocks of vertices in one step, which is structurally similar to graph-tool's agglomerative merge proposals. The page walker only animates level 0, but the round-robin chain explores partitions the level-0-only chain reaches only through long sequences of single-vertex moves. On large graphs this gap is order-of-magnitude in sweeps to convergence.

contrast · nested SBM vs. other hierarchical clustering

Hierarchical agglomerative clustering (single-link, average-link, Ward's) produces a dendrogram by greedily merging clusters bottom-up; the levels of the dendrogram have no inherent stopping criterion, and the choice of cut depth is left to the user. The nested SBM differs in two ways. First, the hierarchy is inferred jointly with the partitions at every level, not constructed sequentially: a move at level 2 can rearrange level-0 blocks indirectly through the rebuild path. Second, every level has its own description length, and the total \(\Sigma_{\text{nested}}\) is the criterion for picking the depth: the chain runs to a fixed depth (typically \(\lceil \log_2(N) \rceil\)) and the upper levels with trivial partitions contribute zero or near-zero DL. The choice of depth is automatic in the sense that adding levels above the natural top costs nothing.
