SBM-nested-best

nested (hierarchical) stochastic block model, lowest description length

Two nested SBM variants (DC and NDC) run from one shared seeded init on the 32-node fixture; pairwise \(\Delta\Sigma\) ranked under Peixoto's Bayes-factor reading.

Draft, level-0 walker only. Anchor page for the nested SBM family. The kernel composes the full multi-level \(e_{rs}\) propagation end-to-end and the cpp tracer plus JS replay leg exercise the hierarchy under self-RNG (306/306 cells PASS on the 3-tier stress panel); this page surfaces only the level-0 comparison. The planted-partition (PP) variant has no nested counterpart in graph-tool and is omitted. Verdict labels follow Peixoto 2017 §VII.A's Bayes-factor reading (\(|\Delta\Sigma| < 5\) nats inconclusive, \(5\) to \(10\) suggestive, beyond \(10\) decisive).

The nested stochastic block model stacks flat blockmodels into a hierarchy. Level 0 is the input graph plus its block partition, exactly as in the flat SBM family. Level 1 is the block-multigraph obtained by collapsing level 0's blocks into super-vertices: the vertices of the level-1 graph are the non-empty level-0 blocks, the edges carry the level-0 \(e_{rs}\) counts as weights, and self-loops on the level-1 graph carry \(e_{rr}/2\) per the doubled-diagonal convention. Level 1 has its own block partition. The construction repeats at level 2, 3, and so on, until the top level has one block or two.

The hierarchy buys two things. First, an asymptotic resolution-limit improvement. The flat SBM under the uniform-edge-count prior has \(B_{\max} \propto \sqrt{N}\) (Peixoto 2017 §VI, Eq 27); the nested prior lifts the asymptotic limit to \(B_{\max} \propto N / \log N\). Second, the hierarchy makes the partition structurally interpretable: the higher levels are coarser groupings of lower-level blocks, so the inference returns not just a single partition but a tree of partitions at multiple scales.

The objective is the description length summed across levels:

\(\Sigma_{\text{nested}}(\boldsymbol{b}^{(0)}, \ldots, \boldsymbol{b}^{(L - 1)}) = \sum_{l = 0}^{L - 1} \Sigma_l(\boldsymbol{b}^{(l)} ; G^{(l)})\),

where \(G^{(0)}\) is the input graph and \(G^{(l)}\) for \(l > 0\) is the block-multigraph from level \(l - 1\). In plain terms: pay the flat-SBM cost at every level, where each level's "graph" is the collapsed version from below. Two properties. The total decomposes per level, so a per-level MCMC move only changes the level it touches plus, via the rebuild path, the upper levels' graphs; the chain runs as a round-robin over levels with deterministic upper-level rebuilds. And the resolution-limit improvement is asymptotic: on small graphs the level-1+ contributions are tiny in absolute nats, and the flat and nested mode partitions usually coincide at level 0.

Two variants for the nested family. The degree-corrected (DC) variant pays the degree DL at level 0 and uses NDC sparse entropy at every upper level. The non-degree-corrected (NDC) variant uses NDC entropy at every level. The PP variant has no nested form in graph-tool: PP collapses the per-pair matrix into two scalars at level 0, which leaves no \(e_{rs}\) for the level-1 graph to inherit; the nested construction breaks down on the first rebuild.

DC degree-corrected level-0 sparse entropy plus uniform degree DL; NDC sparse entropy at every level above. Survives heavy-tailed degree distributions on the input graph.
NDC non-degree-corrected sparse entropy at every level. The block-multigraph view does not depend on per-node degrees at any level.
level-\(l\) graph the multigraph where vertices are the non-empty level-\((l-1)\) blocks (renumbered \(0 \ldots B_{\text{ne}} - 1\)), edges carry the level-\((l-1)\) \(e_{rs}\) counts, and self-loops carry \(e_{rr}/2\).
\(\Sigma_{\text{nested}}\) sum of per-level description lengths. Lower is better. Self-consistent across levels (no inter-level coupling term).

stage 1 · why a hierarchy at all

The flat resolution limit, and what the hierarchy does about it.

The flat SBM under the uniform-edge-count prior places the partition DL and the edges DL on the same scale as the data-fit term. The two DL terms grow with \(B\); on a sparse graph the trade-off saturates around \(B \le \sqrt{2 E}\), and partitions with more blocks than that pay more in DL than they save in data fit. Peixoto 2017 §VI traces this through Eq 27 and gives the asymptotic bound \(B_{\max}^{\text{flat}} \propto \sqrt{N}\) on sparse graphs.

The nested prior replaces the single quadratic-in-\(B\) penalty with a stack of priors at every level, and the prior at level \(l > 0\) acts on a graph whose vertex count \(N^{(l)}\) is the level-\((l-1)\) non-empty block count. Each upper level pays a partition DL that grows with \(B^{(l)}\) and an edges DL that grows with the level-\(l\) cell count, but both are computed on a much smaller "graph" than the input. The cumulative penalty grows asymptotically as \(N / \log N\), which is what lifts the upper bound:

\(B_{\max}^{\text{flat}} \propto \sqrt{N}, \qquad B_{\max}^{\text{nested}} \propto N / \log N\).

In plain terms: doubling \(N\) lets the flat SBM represent \(\sqrt{2} \approx 1.41\) times as many blocks, where the nested SBM grows by a factor close to 2. Two properties. The lift is asymptotic; on small graphs both bounds are comfortable and the modes coincide. And the shape of the bound matches the way real-world communities scale: empirical block counts in social networks and biological pathways grow roughly linearly with \(N\), which the flat model cannot follow.

\(B_{\max}\) vs. \(N\) for flat (\(\propto \sqrt{N}\), blue) and nested (\(\propto N / \log N\), ochre) log-log axes

The curves cross at small \(N\) (where the prefactors dominate) and the nested curve overtakes by an order of magnitude past \(N \approx 10^3\). The page fixture sits at the left edge of the chart; on graphs the stress panel covers (\(N\) up to \(\sim 30\)K) the gap is already large.

stage 2 · one graph per level

Level 0 is the input; level \(l\) is the collapsed block-multigraph from level \(l - 1\).

The nested kernel maintains a list of flat block states, one per level. Level 0 wraps the input graph; level \(l > 0\) wraps the block-multigraph obtained from level \(l - 1\). Each state is a self-contained flat block model with its own entropy formula, its own partition, and its own MCMC sweep.

The level-1 graph is a weighted multigraph, not a simple graph. A single edge in the level-1 graph between vertices \(r_1\) and \(r_2\) carries the weight \(e^{(0)}_{r_1, r_2}\); self-loops carry \(e^{(0)}_{rr} / 2\). In plain terms: every edge in the level-0 graph is preserved as a weight contribution in the level-1 graph, but the structure is compressed to one vertex per non-empty level-0 block. Two properties. The total weight of the level-1 graph equals the total weight of the level-0 graph, so \(E^{(l)}\) is conserved across the level transition. And the level-1 NDC entropy reads the level-0 partition coarsely: blocks within a level-1 group are super-blocks of level-0 blocks.

The level-1+ entropy formula is always NDC. The level-1 graph is a multigraph over level-0 blocks, and the "degree sequence" of a level-1 vertex is the level-0 block's total edge weight \(e^{(0)}_r\), which is part of the level-0 model rather than an independent quantity at level 1. Degree correction at the higher levels would double-count. Graph-tool's canonical NestedBlockState follows the same convention.

stage 3 · the nested description length

\(\Sigma_{\text{nested}}\) decomposes into per-level flat \(\Sigma_l\).

The nested description length is

\(\Sigma_{\text{nested}} = \sum_{l = 0}^{L - 1} \Big[ S_l(\boldsymbol{e}^{(l)}, \boldsymbol{b}^{(l)}) + L_e(B^{(l)}, E^{(l)}) + L_p^{(l)}(\boldsymbol{b}^{(l)}) + [l = 0]\, L_d \Big]\),

where \([l = 0]\) is the Iverson bracket. In plain terms, every level pays the same three or four terms a flat SBM pays, computed on that level's graph and that level's partition. The degree DL only fires at level 0 because higher levels work on multigraphs with no separate degree sequence. Two properties. The decomposition is fully additive across levels with no cross-level coupling term, which is what makes per-level MCMC well-defined; graph-tool's canonical adds a small \(\Lambda_{\text{rec dx}}\) coupling between levels that JS does not port. And the absolute \(\Sigma_{\text{nested}}\) is always larger than the corresponding flat \(\Sigma_0\) on a non-trivial hierarchy, because the level-1+ terms are positive (they pay extra DL for the hierarchy itself), but the gain from the resolution-limit improvement on large \(B\) is asymptotically much larger.

The level-0 contribution dominates on a small fixture. Level 1 and higher add small positive amounts; the cascade is the structural cost the model pays for having a hierarchy at all. The flat \(\Sigma\) (level-0 only) is always lower in absolute nats; the lift is asymptotic, not on \(N = 32\).

stage 4 · init the nested partition

Random level-0 labelling; the upper levels copy the level-0 block ids verbatim.

The chain initialises with a seeded random level-0 partition over \(B_0\) blocks. The level-\(l > 0\) initial partitions are degenerate: every level-\(l\) vertex sits in its own level-\(l\) block. In plain terms: the hierarchy starts as a flat partition at level 0 plus a singleton partition at every level above. Two properties. The initial \(\Sigma_{\text{nested}}\) is high because the level-1+ partition DL is paying for as many blocks as there are level-0 blocks. And the upper-level chains rapidly merge the singleton level-\(l\) blocks; within a few outer iterations the upper levels typically settle into a single block, and the level-1+ contributions shrink toward log\(N^{(l)}\) per level.

The shuffle is the backward Fisher-Yates from the SBM utility module, matching the std::shuffle direction in the cpp tracer; the per-vertex assignment is \(b^{(0)}_{\sigma(i)} = i \bmod B_0\) for the shuffled index \(\sigma\). The level-1 init assigns every level-1 vertex to its own block; the same on every higher level.

stage 5 · round-robin level traversal

One outer iteration sweeps every level in order.

The nested MCMC chain runs a round-robin over levels. One outer iteration runs one MH sweep at every level in order; the upper levels rebuild their graphs after each lower-level sweep.

The level-\(l\) sweep is a standard single-vertex MH sweep on the level-\(l\) graph. Each accepted move updates \(\boldsymbol{e}^{(l)}\) in place. After the sweep completes, the level-\((l+1)\) graph is rebuilt deterministically from the updated \(\boldsymbol{e}^{(l)}\), and the level-\((l+1)\) partition is re-initialised from the parent's level-\((l+1)\) membership. In plain terms: the only stochastic step in the chain is the per-level sweep; the level transitions are pure aggregations. Two properties. The rebuild preserves the doubled-diagonal convention, so the level-\((l+1)\) BlockState reads the parent's diagonals the way Peixoto's eterm formula expects. And the per-iteration cost is \(O(N + \sum_l N^{(l)})\), which on graphs with rapidly shrinking levels is dominated by the level-0 work.

stage 6 · one MH visit at level 0

Pick a vertex, propose a target block, accept or reject.

One sweep visits every level-0 vertex once in shuffled order. Each visit runs four steps: build the candidate pool (every currently non-empty level-0 block, plus one fresh slot when the vertex's current block has more than one member); sample a target uniformly; compute \(\Delta\Sigma_0\) via the subset-entropy delta on the level-0 entropy; accept with probability \(\min(1, e^{-\beta\Delta\Sigma_0})\) at \(\beta = 1\). The walker below replays one chain visit at a time.

The walker drives the DC chain; the NDC chain runs from the same seeded init in parallel and is summarised on stage 12. In plain terms: the level-0 acceptance ratio uses only the level-0 entropy, not the level-1+ contributions. Two properties. The single-level acceptance is what makes the chain factorise across levels; if every level-0 move had to include the upper-level rebuild costs, each visit would be \(O(N \log N)\). And the round-robin pattern still samples the joint posterior asymptotically: per-level MH moves are valid Gibbs-style updates of the level's conditional distribution given the other levels.

stage 7 · level 0 collapses into level 1

Non-empty level-0 blocks become level-1 vertices; \(e^{(0)}_{rs}\) becomes weight.

After the level-0 sweep finishes, the kernel walks the updated level-0 \(\boldsymbol{e}^{(0)}\) matrix and builds the level-1 graph. Vertices are the non-empty level-0 blocks renumbered \(0 \ldots B^{(0)}_{\text{ne}} - 1\); for every off-diagonal cell \(e^{(0)}_{rs}\) the rebuild emits a weighted edge between the corresponding super-vertices; for every diagonal cell \(e^{(0)}_{rr}\) it emits a self-loop with weight \(e^{(0)}_{rr} / 2\).

level 0 · coloured by block
level 1 · super-vertex per non-empty block

The rebuild is deterministic given \(\boldsymbol{e}^{(0)}\): no RNG is drawn, the same level-0 \(\boldsymbol{e}\) always produces the same level-1 graph. In plain terms: the level transition is a pure aggregation, not a stochastic step. Two properties. The doubled-diagonal halving on the rebuild is what allows the same flat-BlockState code to handle both the level-0 simple graph and the level-1+ multigraph without separate code paths; the level-\(l\) BlockState's internal doubling on load reproduces the original level-\((l-1)\) diagonal count. And the rebuild remaps level-0 block ids 0..N to compact level-1 vertex ids 0..\(B^{(0)}_{\text{ne}} - 1\), so the level-1 graph never has empty vertex slots.

stage 8 · one MH visit at level 1

Same sweep, but each "vertex" is a level-0 super-block.

The level-1 sweep is structurally identical to the level-0 sweep: shuffled visit order over level-1 vertices, uniform proposal over non-empty level-1 blocks, MH acceptance at \(\beta = 1\). The difference is that every "vertex" the sweep visits is a super-block of level-0 vertices; assigning it to a level-1 block effectively groups every level-0 vertex inside it into the corresponding super-super-block.

The proposal at level 1 reads as a super-block reassignment. In plain terms: moving level-1 vertex \(r\) to level-1 block \(s\) takes every level-0 vertex with \(b^{(0)} = r\) and reads them under the level-1 partition as part of super-block \(s\) instead of \(r\). The level-0 partition is unchanged; only the level-1 labelling shifts. Two properties. The level-1 sweep has fewer vertices than the level-0 sweep (\(N^{(1)} = B^{(0)}_{\text{ne}}\)), so individual moves at level 1 are coarser; an accepted level-1 move corresponds to a whole-block reassignment at level 0. And the level-1 acceptance is decided by the level-1 entropy alone; level-0 moves do not appear in the per-visit \(\Delta\Sigma_1\).

stage 9 · level 1 → level 2 rebuild and sweep

Repeat the construction one floor up.

The cycle repeats. After the level-1 sweep, the kernel rebuilds the level-2 graph from \(\boldsymbol{e}^{(1)}\); a level-2 sweep then runs an MH chain over level-2 vertices. The level-2 graph is typically much smaller (\(N^{(2)} = B^{(1)}_{\text{ne}}\)), so the level-2 sweep is fast and the level-2 partition often settles into one or two blocks within a few visits.

The construction terminates when the top level holds one block or two. In plain terms: the chain runs to a fixed depth chosen by the page (typically \(\lceil \log_2 N \rceil\)), and the top levels with trivial partitions contribute zero or near-zero DL to \(\Sigma_{\text{nested}}\). Two properties. The depth is automatic in the sense that adding levels above the natural top costs nothing once the partitions saturate to one block per level. And the level-\(L - 1\) MH sweep never moves anything once the partition is a single block; the kernel still draws RNG for the sweep, which is the only level-budget impact on the total runtime.

stage 10 · block deletions percolate upward

An emptied level-0 block disappears from the level-1 graph on the next rebuild.

When an MH sweep at level 0 empties a block (every vertex moves out of it during the sweep), the level-0 partition's non-empty count \(B^{(0)}_{\text{ne}}\) drops by one. The next rebuild walks the updated \(\boldsymbol{e}^{(0)}\) and emits one fewer level-1 vertex. The level-1 partition is re-initialised from the parent's remapped membership, so the level-1 partition typically inherits one fewer block as well. The cascade can propagate further up the stack if level 1 then empties a block during its sweep.

The merge cascade gives the chain access to coarser partitions without explicit agglomerative moves. In plain terms: graph-tool's canonical pipeline runs explicit merge proposals at every level; the JS port uses single-vertex MH only, and reaches the same coarse partitions through the cascade. Two properties. The cascade is purely deterministic on the rebuild path; the only stochastic decisions are the per-level MH accepts. And the lift in convergence speed comes from the fact that one level-1 move corresponds to a whole-block relabelling at level 0, which the level-0 chain would need many single-vertex moves to reach.

stage 11 · the chain converges when total \(\Sigma_{\text{nested}}\) is stable

The round-robin chain runs for \(K\) outer iterations (the page uses \(K = 20\)). The chain has converged when the total \(\Sigma_{\text{nested}}\) stops drifting between iterations; on a fixture this size that happens within 10 to 15 iterations. stage 14 · what the hierarchy buys vs. flat at this fixture's \(\gamma\)

\(B_{\max}^{\text{flat}} \approx 5.7\), \(B_{\max}^{\text{nested}} \approx 9.2\), planted \(B^* = 4\).

The flat flat-best page walks the same fixture without a hierarchy. On this fixture both bounds (flat \(\sqrt{N} \approx 5.7\), nested \(N/\log N \approx 9.2\)) are above the planted block count \(B^* = 4\); the flat model already fits comfortably, the nested model fits with headroom, and the two pages typically settle on the same level-0 mode partition. The asymptotic gap shows up on graphs where the optimum block count grows past the flat bound.

\(B_{\max}\) for flat (blue) and nested (ochre) at \(N = 32, 10^3, 10^4, 10^5\) red bar = planted \(B^* = 4\) at \(N = 32\)

The gap widens with \(N\). At \(N = 10^3\) the flat bound is at 32 and the nested bound is at 145; at \(N = 10^5\) the flat bound is at 316 and the nested bound is past 8000. In plain terms: on the kind of empirical networks the stress panel covers, the nested form represents an order of magnitude more blocks than the flat form before the priors saturate. Two properties. The CPM alternative (Leiden-CPM page) is resolution-limit-free at every \(\gamma\), but requires picking \(\gamma\) by hand; the nested SBM replaces \(\gamma\) with a fixed prior and recovers the trade-off automatically. And on this fixture the flat and nested level-0 mode partitions usually coincide; the lift is visible in the upper-level structural interpretation, not in the level-0 partition itself.

stage 15 · three practical uses

What the hierarchy is for beyond the resolution-limit lift.

The hierarchy is more than a resolution-limit fix. Three uses below.

three uses · coarsen, drill down, multi-scale read each panel shows a snippet of the same hierarchy used differently

coarsen

The level-1 partition reduces 32 vertices to a handful of super-blocks. A coarser read of the same structure when the level-0 partition is too fine.

drill down

Start at the top level (one or two super-blocks), then read the level-0 substructure inside one super-block. A localised view of one region of the network.

multi-scale

Read every level at once and label each leaf by the path from root to leaf. The tree itself becomes part of the output.

The level-1+ MCMC moves also speed up convergence on assortative networks: one level-1 move corresponds to a whole-block relabelling at level 0, which the single-vertex level-0 chain reaches only through long sequences of moves. In plain terms: the upper-level chains substitute for the agglomerative-merge proposals graph-tool's canonical pipeline runs explicitly. Two properties. The convergence benefit is structural; on large graphs the gap reaches order-of-magnitude in sweeps. And the multi-scale read is the use the JS-port viz check pipeline exercises: the cpp tracer dumps every level's partition + entropy at every sweep, and the JS replay leg matches all levels byte-for-byte.

stage 16 · failure modes

Where the nested form does not help (or breaks).

Two structural failures. The PP variant has no nested counterpart in graph-tool: PP collapses the per-pair \(\boldsymbol{e}\) matrix into two scalars at level 0, which leaves no level-1 graph to inherit. In plain terms: the level-1 rebuild requires the full \(\boldsymbol{e}^{(0)}_{rs}\) per-pair count to act as edge weights at level 1; PP's two-rate aggregation throws that information away, and the nested construction breaks down on the first rebuild. The page omits a PP nested chain.

left: PP at level 0 (\(\boldsymbol{e}\) collapsed to two scalars) · right: rebuild target (no per-pair counts available) red box = rebuild fails

The second failure is the connectivity blindspot the nested form inherits from the flat SBM. The model has no prior on connectivity, so a disconnected community (like C's two 3-cycles) reads as one block at level 0 just as it does in the flat model. The hierarchy does not fix this; the upper levels carry the same blindspot upward. In plain terms: a level-0 block whose induced subgraph has multiple components stays one block at every level above, unless an MH move at level 0 happens to split the components. The post-processing pages (CC, WCC, CM) patch this gap on the flat-pp output; the nested form's level-0 partition feeds the same post-processors.

caveat · level-0 walker only

The kernel runs the full multi-level chain end-to-end and the cpp tracer matches it byte-for-byte across the 17-fixture 3-tier stress panel under self-RNG (306/306 cells PASS); the live walker on stage 6 animates only the level-0 sweep, and the level-1 walker on stage 8 mounts off the post-sweep snapshot rather than driving a full level-1 chain. Cross-check against graph-tool's minimize_nested_blockmodel_dl is on the follow-up; the canonical pipeline uses pcg64 RNG, dense entropy, and multilevel agglomerative merges, all sanctioned divergences from the JS-cpp byte-equal pair.
