SBM-nested-dc

nested (hierarchical) degree-corrected stochastic block model

Stacks flat blockmodels into a hierarchy. Level 0 uses DC entropy and pays the degree DL; every level above uses NDC entropy on the collapsed block-multigraph. The page walks level 0 and shows the upper-level rebuild, priors, and hierarchy readout as static panels.

Draft, level-0 walker only. Walker for the DC variant of the nested SBM. The family-level framing, the hierarchical construction at every depth, the resolution-limit lift, and the cross-algorithm contrasts live on the SBM-nested-best anchor page. The kernel runs the full multi-level chain end-to-end and the cpp tracer plus JS replay leg exercise the hierarchy under self-RNG (306/306 cells PASS on the 3-tier stress panel). The page animates only the level-0 trajectory; the upper levels are shown through static rebuild, NDC-sweep, and hierarchy-readout panels.

The nested degree-corrected SBM is the hierarchical lift of the flat-dc variant. Each component of that statement gets its own stage below.

Level 0 uses the DC entropy form, with the per-node degree sequence conditioned on as auxiliary data and the uniform degree DL term active. Every level above 0 uses NDC entropy on the collapsed block-multigraph. At the bottom of the hierarchy the model prices the per-node degrees through \(L_d\); at every level above, the "vertices" are level-0 blocks and their "degrees" are the per-block edge totals, which are already part of the level-0 entropy and not independent quantities at the upper level. Splitting the levels this way lets the same flat-BlockState code handle every level under one entropy framework.

level 0 the input graph. DC entropy plus uniform degree DL. The walker on this page animates this level.
level \(l > 0\) the multigraph on the non-empty level-\((l-1)\) blocks. NDC entropy, no degree DL.
\(\Sigma_{\text{nested-DC}}\) sum of per-level \(\Sigma\). Lower is better. Self-consistent, no inter-level coupling.
round-robin sweep one outer iteration runs one MH sweep at every level in order, with deterministic upper-level rebuilds between sweeps.
degree correction (DC) conditioning the likelihood on the observed degree sequence \(\boldsymbol{k}\) so that the partition does not have to explain degree heterogeneity through block membership.

Start at level 0: the chain assigns input vertices to blocks, counts edges between those blocks, and scores the result with BlockState(G, { mode: "dc" }). Degree correction conditions on the observed \(k_i\), so hubs do not have to become singleton communities merely to explain degree. After that, the hierarchy collapses non-empty blocks into super-vertices with buildLevelGraph(...); every upper level fits an NDC block model because the super-vertex degrees are inherited edge totals rather than new node data.

The nested score is the sum of level.entropy() across the stack. One round-robin iteration sweeps a level, rebuilds the next graph, and then sweeps that graph, keeping each acceptance ratio local. The output is read bottom-up: level-0 blocks are communities, while upper blocks group communities into coarser structure.

One feature of this fixture matters most for the DC variant. A's hub (node 0) has degree 9; A's periphery leaves have degree 2 or 3. stage 1 · why nested + DC

Two model features, two distinct problems they address.

The nested variant and the degree-correction modifier solve different problems. The hierarchy addresses the resolution-limit problem at large \(N\); degree correction addresses the degree-heterogeneity problem at any \(N\). Both are active on this page and both apply independently.

two features, two failure modes they prevent left: hierarchy (resolution-limit lift) · right: DC (degree heterogeneity)

The flat SBM under the uniform-edge-count prior has \(B_{\max} \propto \sqrt{N}\) (Peixoto 2017 Eq 27). Above this bound, the partition DL term overwhelms the data-fit gains and the chain stops splitting blocks further. On a graph with \(N = 10^5\) and a planted block count of \(300\), the flat model cannot represent the planted partition because \(\sqrt{N} \approx 316\) sits at the limit. The nested prior replaces the quadratic-in-\(B\) penalty with a stack of priors at every level; the asymptotic bound lifts to \(B_{\max} \propto N / \log N\).

In plain terms: the hierarchy opens up representational headroom the flat prior cannot allocate. Two properties. The lift is asymptotic, so on small graphs the nested mode partition usually coincides with the flat mode partition at level 0. And the lift costs per-level DL terms at every higher level, positive but small relative to the headroom gain at large \(N\).

Degree correction addresses a different failure. Under NDC the data-fit term \(e_r \log n_r\) treats every vertex in a block as exchangeable in degree; a degree-9 hub and a degree-2 leaf in the same block pay a penalty proportional to the squared spread of their degrees. Under DC the model conditions on the observed degree sequence \(\boldsymbol{k}\), so the partition can group vertices by their connection neighbourhoods regardless of degree. In words: NDC asks the partition to account for why some vertices have many neighbours and others few; DC takes the degrees as given and asks only how the connections distribute across blocks. Two properties. DC is the only flat variant that pays the uniform degree DL \(L_d\), the price of conditioning on \(\boldsymbol{k}\). And the gap between DC and NDC widens with the degree heterogeneity of the graph: on a regular graph the two are equivalent; on a power-law graph DC pays much less.

stage 1.5 · level-0 block model

The bottom level turns vertices into block-edge counts.

The level-0 model is the only place where the observed vertices appear directly. Given a partition \(\boldsymbol{b}^{(0)}\), the code counts how many edge endpoints fall between every pair of blocks and evaluates the flat-DC description length on those sufficient statistics.

Each node receives a block label, and the graph is summarised by block sizes \(n_r\), block degrees \(e_r\), and block-pair counts \(e_{rs}\). The Bayesian score compares partitions by how compactly they encode the same edges, so a useful block assignment makes the edge-count matrix concentrated. In the walker, BlockState.entropy() reads cached counts and local proposals use virtualMove(v, s) to recompute only the affected delta.

level-0 accounting · \(B^{(0)}\) = · · \(\Sigma^{(0)}_{\text{DC}}\) = · nats partition → \(e^{(0)}_{rs}\) matrix → DL terms

Notice the separation of roles. The partition \(\boldsymbol{b}^{(0)}\) is the latent object being inferred. The degrees \(\boldsymbol{k}\) are observed auxiliary data under DC. The matrix \(\boldsymbol{e}^{(0)}\) is the sufficient statistic that lets a proposed move be scored without re-reading the whole graph.

stage 2 · nested DC description length

The objective the chain minimises, written out per level.

The description length the nested-DC chain minimises is

\(\Sigma_{\text{nested-DC}}(\boldsymbol{b}^{(0)}, \ldots, \boldsymbol{b}^{(L-1)}) = \Sigma^{(0)}_{\text{DC}}(\boldsymbol{b}^{(0)} ; G^{(0)}) + \sum_{l = 1}^{L - 1} \Sigma^{(l)}_{\text{NDC}}(\boldsymbol{b}^{(l)} ; G^{(l)})\),

where \(\Sigma^{(0)}_{\text{DC}}\) is the full flat-DC \(\Sigma\) on the input graph and each \(\Sigma^{(l)}_{\text{NDC}}\) for \(l > 0\) is the flat-NDC \(\Sigma\) on the level-\(l\) block-multigraph. In words: pay the DC cost at level 0, pay the NDC cost at every level above. Two properties. The decomposition is additive across levels with no inter-level coupling term, which is what keeps per-level MH well-defined and matches the JS-side self-consistent form (a deliberate divergence from graph-tool's NestedBlockState entropy, which adds a small \(L_{\text{rec dx}}\) coupling). And on any non-trivial hierarchy the absolute \(\Sigma_{\text{nested-DC}}\) exceeds the corresponding flat-DC \(\Sigma_0\) because the level-1+ terms are positive; the resolution-limit lift outpaces this overhead at large \(N\).

1. score the input graph Use DC because raw vertices have observed degrees and hubs. levels[0].state.entropy()
2. collapse the partition The next graph's vertices are non-empty level-0 blocks. buildLevelGraph(G0, state0, b1)
3. score upper graphs Use NDC because their "degrees" are inherited block-edge totals. BlockState(Gl, { mode: "ndc" })
4. add the code lengths A shorter total code means the hierarchy explains the graph more parsimoniously. \(\Sigma_{\text{nested}} = \sum_l \Sigma_l\)

Each per-level \(\Sigma^{(l)}\) decomposes into the standard three or four DL terms on that level's graph and partition.

per-level DL terms · active at level level 0 pays four terms · level \(l > 0\) pays three

The level-0 sparse entropy \(S^{(0)}_{\text{DC}}\) reads

\(S^{(0)}_{\text{DC}} = \sum_r \log(e^{(0)}_r!) - \sum_{r < s} \log(e^{(0)}_{rs}!) - \sum_r \Big[ \log\!\big((e^{(0)}_{rr}/2)!\big) + (e^{(0)}_{rr}/2)\log 2 \Big]\),

the same form as on the flat-dc page. In words: the first sum counts the orderings of the half-stubs at every block, the second discounts the redundancy in pairing them across off-diagonal block pairs, the third does the same inside diagonal pairs. Two properties. The formula depends on the partition only through the entries of \(\boldsymbol{e}^{(0)}\); the per-node degrees enter through the constraint \(\sum_s e^{(0)}_{rs} = e^{(0)}_r\), not as free parameters. And its value at \(\boldsymbol{b}^{(0)}\) equals \(-\log\) of the number of simple graphs with the observed \(\boldsymbol{e}^{(0)}\) and \(\boldsymbol{k}\), the microcanonical likelihood under Peixoto Eq 43.

The level-1+ sparse entropy \(S^{(l)}_{\text{NDC}}\) is the NDC analogue,

\(S^{(l)}_{\text{NDC}} = \sum_r e^{(l)}_r \log(n^{(l)}_r) - \sum_{r < s} \log(e^{(l)}_{rs}!) - \sum_r \Big[ \log\!\big((e^{(l)}_{rr}/2)!\big) + (e^{(l)}_{rr}/2)\log 2 \Big]\),

with the vertex factor replaced by \(e^{(l)}_r \log n^{(l)}_r\) because at level \(l > 0\) the "vertices" of the level-\(l\) graph have no separate degree sequence; their degree is the level-\((l-1)\) block sum, already part of the level-\((l-1)\) model. In words: the level-1+ entropy treats the multigraph as a weighted graph with no per-vertex degree distribution to condition on. Two properties. NDC at every upper level is structurally forced, not a modelling choice: degree correction at level \(l > 0\) would double-count the level-\((l-1)\) degree sums. And the level-1+ contribution is typically much smaller in nats than the level-0 contribution because the level-1+ graph has many fewer vertices than the input graph.

stage 3 · random init

Random level-0 partition with \(B_0\) blocks; \(\Sigma_0\) on display.

MCMC starts from any valid partition. The level-0 chain initialises with \(B_0 = \) 8 blocks: the kernel shuffles the identity permutation under MT19937 and assigns block \(i \bmod B_0\) to vertex \(\sigma(i)\). The shuffle is backward Fisher-Yates, matching the std::shuffle direction in the cpp tracer. The upper-level partitions inherit from this seeded init by way of the deterministic level-\((l-1) \to l\) rebuild path.

re-runs the chain · init + 20 sweeps

The random init has the property that the chain reaches the level-0 mode partition from any starting point under enough sweeps. In words: the MH chain is ergodic over the partition space at \(\beta = 1\), and the random init gives a baseline that does not bias the trajectory. Two properties. At \(\beta = 1\) the chain samples the posterior \(P(\boldsymbol{b}^{(0)} \mid \boldsymbol{A})\) marginal at level 0, so multiple chains from different seeds trace out different posterior samples. And the canonical graph-tool pipeline uses a different init strategy (\(B = 1\) bottom-up plus agglomerative merges) that reaches the same posterior region by a different path; the comdet pages use random init for pedagogy.

stage 4 · round-robin across levels

One outer iteration touches every level in order.

The nested MCMC chain is a round-robin over levels. One outer iteration runs one sweep at every level in turn; the upper levels rebuild their graphs after each lower-level sweep. The page walker animates the level-0 sweep only; the upper-level sweeps and the rebuild path run inside the kernel.

round-robin schedule · one outer iteration blue = DC sweep at level 0 · ochre = NDC sweep at level \(l > 0\)

The level-0 sweep is the standard MH sweep at the DC entropy form. The rebuild that follows is deterministic given the level-0 \(\boldsymbol{e}^{(0)}\) matrix: vertices of the level-1 graph are the non-empty level-0 blocks renumbered \(0 \ldots B^{(0)}_{\text{ne}} - 1\), edges carry the level-0 \(e^{(0)}_{rs}\) counts as weights, self-loops carry \(e^{(0)}_{rr}/2\). The level-1 state is re-initialised with the parent's level-1 membership remapped to the new vertex ids. The level-1 sweep then runs an MH sweep on the rebuilt graph under NDC entropy, and the cycle repeats up to level \(L - 1\). In words: the only stochastic step in the nested chain is the per-level MH sweep; the level transitions are pure aggregations. Two properties. The round-robin guarantees the chain samples the joint posterior asymptotically because the per-level MH moves are valid Gibbs-style updates of the level's conditional posterior given the other levels. And the per-iteration cost is \(O(N + \sum_l N^{(l)})\), which on graphs with rapidly shrinking levels reduces to \(O(N)\); the rebuild dominates only when the level-0 partition is very fine.

stage 5 · level-0 MH walker

Each visit: pick a vertex, propose a target block, accept or reject.

One sweep visits every level-0 vertex once in shuffled order. Each visit runs four steps:

  1. Build the candidate pool: every currently non-empty block at level 0, plus one fresh empty slot when the vertex's current block holds more than one member.
  2. Sample a target uniformly from the pool (the \(c \to \infty\) limit of Peixoto's local-neighbourhood proposal).
  3. Compute \(\Delta\Sigma\) for the move via the subset-entropy delta on the level-0 DC entropy.
  4. Accept with probability \(\min(1, e^{-\beta\Delta\Sigma})\) at \(\beta = 1\). On accept, commit; on reject, leave the partition unchanged.

The \(\Delta\Sigma\) computed at each visit is the level-0 delta only. The upper-level contributions to \(\Sigma_{\text{nested-DC}}\) are deterministic given the level-0 partition, but they do not enter the per-visit acceptance ratio. In words: the level-0 sweep optimises the level-0 likelihood under the level-0 prior, and the upper levels see the level-0 mode partition after the sweep and adjust their own partitions during the next round-robin pass. Two properties. The single-level acceptance ratio is what lets the chain factorise across levels; folding upper-level rebuild costs into the per-visit ratio would require an \(O(N \log N)\) recompute per visit. And the round-robin pattern restores asymptotic correctness of the joint posterior sampling under the factorised acceptance ratio.

Moves with \(\Delta\Sigma \le 0\) always commit, and the kernel does not draw the uniform random sample (the RNG stream stays aligned with the cpp tracer at exact ties). Moves with \(\Delta\Sigma > 0\) commit with probability exponentially decaying in the cost.

stage 6 · level-0 → level-1 rebuild

Collapse blocks into vertices; \(e_{rs}\) counts become edge weights.

After the level-0 sweep settles, the kernel rebuilds the level-1 graph from the level-0 mode partition. The rebuild walks the level-0 edge list and bins each edge into a level-1 edge between the source and target block ids; level-0 self-loops contribute to a level-1 self-loop on the same block id. The level-1 graph is a weighted multigraph with one vertex per non-empty level-0 block.

The rebuild reads every current label \(b_v^{(0)}\), ignores empty block ids, bins each input edge \((u,v)\) into the pair \((b_u^{(0)}, b_v^{(0)})\), compacts the non-empty ids to \(0, \ldots, B_{\text{ne}}^{(0)} - 1\), and creates \(G^{(1)}\) with one super-vertex per surviving level-0 block. Weighted level-1 edges are exactly the binned \(e_{rs}^{(0)}\) counts.

level-0 partition (left) · level-1 block-multigraph (right) ·

The rebuild preserves the doubled-diagonal convention from level 0. A level-0 self-loop at vertex \(v\) contributes \(2w\) to \(e^{(0)}_{b_v, b_v}\); the level-1 graph then carries a self-loop at the super-vertex with weight \(e^{(0)}_{b_v, b_v} / 2 = w\). In words: the convention preserves the total internal-edge count exactly through every level transition. Two properties. The convention lets the same flat-BlockState code handle both the level-0 graph and the level-1+ multigraph without separate code paths. And the diagonal halving on the rebuild is the only place the convention is visible; everywhere else the level-1+ states behave as ordinary flat block models.

The rebuild is deterministic. The only stochastic input to the level-1 state is the inherited level-1 membership remapping; given the level-0 mode partition, the level-1 graph is determined.

stage 7 · level-1 NDC sweep

Same MH walker, NDC entropy, smaller graph.

The level-1 sweep is structurally identical to the level-0 sweep: shuffled visit order, uniform proposal over the level-1 non-empty blocks (plus optional fresh slot), accept-reject under \(\exp(-\beta \Delta\Sigma)\). The only difference is the entropy form: level 1 uses NDC, with no degree DL. The level-1 graph is a weighted multigraph, so the entropy uses edge weights rather than unit counts; the BlockState code path is the same.

At level 1 the "vertices" are whole level-0 blocks, and an edge weight records how many original edges crossed that block pair. The upper state is another BlockState, but it is built with mode: "ndc" on the rebuilt graph: a super-vertex degree is \(e_r^{(0)}\), already encoded by the lower level, so treating it as a fresh degree sequence would charge it twice.

level-1 state · \(B^{(1)}\) = · · \(\Sigma^{(1)}_{\text{NDC}}\) = · nats colour = level-1 block id

The level-1 NDC entropy looks at the level-1 graph and asks the same question DC asked at level 0, minus the degree conditioning. The "vertices" of the level-1 graph are level-0 blocks, and their "degrees" are the level-0 block edge sums \(e^{(0)}_r\), which are not free parameters in the level-1 model. In words: the level-1 chain decides which level-0 blocks to group together into super-blocks from the level-1 \(e_{rs}\) concentration, not from per-vertex degree heterogeneity at the upper level. Two properties. The level-1 acceptance ratio uses the level-1 \(\Delta\Sigma\) only, so a single level-1 move costs \(O(\deg^{(1)}(v) + B^{(1)}_{\text{ne}})\) at the upper level, much cheaper than a level-0 move. And the level-1 mode partition on the comdet fixture is small (typically two or three super-blocks); the level-2 graph collapses further and contributes only a fraction of a nat to \(\Sigma_{\text{nested-DC}}\).

stage 8 · where degree correction matters

DC carries weight at level 0, very little above it.

Degree correction prices the per-node degree variance through the data-fit term plus the uniform degree DL. The cost depends on how spread the degree distribution is inside each block. At level 0 the input graph has a heavy-tailed degree distribution: A's hub at degree 9, A's periphery at degree 2 or 3, the rest of the graph at degree 2 to 4. At every level above, the "vertices" are aggregated blocks whose weighted degree is the level-\((l-1)\) block sum, and the distribution is much narrower because aggregation averages out the per-vertex variance.

weighted-degree variance per level · lower = less degree heterogeneity levels read left to right

Putting degree correction at every level would double-count the spread the level-0 model already prices. In words: DC matters most at the input level where the raw degree distribution lives; at every level above the spread has been absorbed into the level-0 \(e^{(0)}\) matrix and the NDC entropy works with what remains. Two properties. DC at level 0 only is structurally forced by the level-0-only degree sequence, not a modelling preference. And on real-world graphs with heavy tails the level-0 DC vs. level-0 NDC gap can reach hundreds of nats, while the level-1+ DC vs. NDC gap typically sits below 10 nats because aggregation has already smoothed the distribution.

stage 10.5 · hierarchy interpretation

Read the output bottom-up: communities, then groups of communities.

The nested model does not return one partition and stop. It returns a sequence of partitions. Level 0 groups vertices into communities; level 1 groups those communities into super-communities; higher levels keep compressing until the remaining graph is too small to justify more structure.

hierarchy readout · current seed ·

Interpret level-0 blocks as the finest communities the model is willing to pay for. The middle levels describe relations among those communities: which level-0 groups share unusually many edges. Once a level has one or two blocks, the hierarchy has mostly spent its explanatory budget.

stage 11 · nested-DC vs flat-DC

What the hierarchy adds, on this fixture.

On the comdet fixture the level-0 mode partitions of nested-DC and flat-DC usually coincide. The difference is the reported \(\Sigma\): the nested chain pays \(\Sigma_{\text{nested-DC}} = \Sigma^{(0)}_{\text{DC}} + \sum_{l \ge 1} \Sigma^{(l)}_{\text{NDC}}\), the flat chain pays \(\Sigma^{(0)}_{\text{DC}}\) only. The level-1+ overhead on this fixture is a handful of nats; on a graph the size of the gallery's stress panel it grows to tens or hundreds, and the resolution-limit gain at large \(B\) outpaces it.

same fixture, same seed · nested-DC (level 0) vs flat-DC absolute Σ values are model-class commensurable per Peixoto Eq 43

On a small fixture the hierarchy leaves the level-0 mode unchanged and pays its way through interpretability rather than through resolution-limit relief. In words: on this graph the nested chain returns a tree of partitions whose top levels are tiny, and the bottom of the tree matches what the flat chain returns. Two properties. The per-level mode partitions are consistent: the level-1 mode partition at this scale typically holds each level-0 block in its own super-block, with maybe one merge of A and D. And the equilibration traces line up between flat-DC and nested-DC at level 0; the divergence is in absolute \(\Sigma\), not in the mode the chain reaches.

stage 13 · DC failure mode

Over-correction at sparse upper levels.

Degree correction at level 0 is the right choice when the level-0 graph has a heavy-tailed degree distribution. NDC at every level above is correct in expectation, with one corner case: when the level-0 partition collapses into a fine set of blocks at large \(N\), the level-1 multigraph can pick up its own heavy tail (a level-0 block with many edges to many other level-0 blocks becomes a high-weighted-degree level-1 vertex). On a graph where this happens, the level-1 NDC entropy pays a spread penalty that DC at level 1 would have absorbed; the page does not animate that scale.

On the 32-node fixture this corner case does not appear: the level-1 graph has at most 4 to 6 vertices and a narrow weighted-degree distribution. The failure mode is asymptotic, and on small graphs the choice of NDC at every upper level is harmless. Two properties. The canonical graph-tool implementation makes the same choice (NDC at every level above 0), so the comdet page mirrors the canonical convention rather than a port-specific simplification. And the corner case is best handled by tuning the depth of the hierarchy: when the level-1 graph still shows heavy tails, the partition is too fine at level 0 and the chain has not converged to the parsimonious depth.
