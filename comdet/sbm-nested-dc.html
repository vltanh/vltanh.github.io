SBM-nested-dc

nested (hierarchical) degree-corrected stochastic block model

Stacks flat blockmodels into a hierarchy; level 0 uses DC entropy, every level above uses NDC. The page walks level 0.

Draft, level-0 walker only. Walker for the DC variant of the nested SBM. The family-level Bayesian framing, the hierarchical construction, the round-robin MCMC, and the cross-algorithm contrasts live on the SBM-nested-best anchor page; this page focuses on the level-0 DC walker and how it composes into the hierarchy. The kernel runs the full multi-level chain end-to-end and the cpp tracer plus JS replay leg exercise the hierarchy under self-RNG (306/306 cells PASS on the 3-tier stress panel); the page animates only the level-0 trajectory.

The nested degree-corrected SBM is the hierarchical lift of the flat-dc variant. Level 0 uses the DC entropy form, with the per-node degree sequence conditioned and the uniform degree DL term active. Every level above 0 uses NDC entropy on the collapsed block-multigraph. In plain terms: at the bottom of the hierarchy the model knows the per-node degrees and reads them as auxiliary data; at every level above, the "vertices" are level-0 blocks whose "degrees" are the per-block edge totals, which are part of the level-0 entropy rather than independent quantities at the upper level. The split is what allows the same flat-BlockState code to handle every level under one entropy framework.

The description length the chain minimises is

\(\Sigma_{\text{nested-DC}} = \Sigma^{(0)}_{\text{DC}} + \sum_{l = 1}^{L - 1} \Sigma^{(l)}_{\text{NDC}}\),

where \(\Sigma^{(0)}_{\text{DC}}\) is the full flat-DC \(\Sigma\) on the input graph (sparse entropy DC + edges DL + partition DL + degree DL) and each \(\Sigma^{(l)}_{\text{NDC}}\) for \(l > 0\) is the flat-NDC \(\Sigma\) on the level-\(l\) block-multigraph (sparse entropy NDC + edges DL + partition DL; no degree DL at upper levels). In plain terms: pay the DC cost at level 0, pay the NDC cost at every level above. Two properties. The decomposition is fully additive across levels with no inter-level coupling term; this is the JS-side self-consistent form that diverges (intentionally) from graph-tool's NestedBlockState entropy which adds a small Lrecdx coupling. And the level-0 DC entropy is what the level-0 walker on this page minimises directly; the upper-level contributions are computed by the kernel after the round-robin chain converges but are not animated on this page.

The level-0 walker is structurally identical to the flat-dc walker. The difference is in the reported \(\Sigma\): the nested page computes the multi-level total, the flat page computes only \(\Sigma^{(0)}\). On the 32-node fixture the level-0 mode partitions typically coincide; the asymptotic benefit of the hierarchy applies to graphs much larger than this fixture.

On the 32-node fixture the resolution-limit benefit of the nested form is mostly cosmetic. \(\sqrt{32} \approx 5.7\), so the flat SBM can already represent the 4-community ground-truth comfortably. The hierarchy starts paying for itself when the optimal block count exceeds \(\sqrt{N}\), which on graphs of the kind the gallery's stress panel covers requires \(N\) at least in the high thousands. The DC variant here mirrors the flat-dc page's handling of A's hub: degree correction absorbs the gap between node 0 (degree 9) and its periphery leaves, so A reads as cohesive.

stage 1 · random init

Random partition with \(B_0\) blocks; \(\Sigma_0\) on display.

MCMC starts from any valid partition. The level-0 chain initialises with \(B_0 = \) 8 blocks: the kernel shuffles the identity permutation under MT19937 and assigns block \(i \bmod B_0\) to vertex \(\sigma(i)\). The shuffle is backward Fisher-Yates, matching the std::shuffle direction in the cpp tracer.

\(\Sigma_0\) is the level-0 DC entropy at the random init; the kernel also computes the level-1+ contributions in the background but the walker traces level 0 only. MCMC's job at level 0 is to push the level-0 \(\Sigma\) down by re-routing nodes into blocks where their edge neighbourhoods cohere; the upper levels then absorb the residual structure on the rebuild.

stage 2 · one move at a time

Each visit: pick a vertex, propose a target block, accept or reject.

One sweep visits every level-0 vertex once in shuffled order. Each visit runs four steps:

  1. Build the candidate pool: every currently non-empty block at level 0, plus one fresh empty slot when the vertex's current block holds more than one member.
  2. Sample a target uniformly from the pool (the \(c \to \infty\) limit of Peixoto's local-neighbourhood proposal).
  3. Compute \(\Delta\Sigma\) for the move via the subset-entropy delta on the level-0 DC entropy.
  4. Accept with probability \(\min(1, e^{-\beta\Delta\Sigma})\) at \(\beta = 1\). On accept, commit; on reject, leave the partition unchanged.

The \(\Delta\Sigma\) computed at each visit is the level-0 delta only. The upper-level contributions to \(\Sigma_{\text{nested-DC}}\) are deterministic given the level-0 partition (the upper-level rebuild path takes the updated \(e^{(0)}_{rs}\) matrix and produces the level-1 graph), but they are not part of the per-visit acceptance ratio. In plain terms: the level-0 sweep optimises the level-0 likelihood under the level-0 prior; the upper levels see the level-0 mode partition after the sweep and adjust their own partitions during the next round-robin pass. Two properties. The single-level acceptance ratio is what makes the chain factorise across levels; if the per-visit acceptance included the upper-level rebuild costs, each level-0 move would require a full upper-level recompute (O(\(N \log N\)) per visit). And the round-robin pattern guarantees the chain still samples the joint posterior asymptotically; the per-level MH moves are valid Gibbs-style updates of the level's conditional posterior given the other levels.

Moves with \(\Delta\Sigma \le 0\) always commit and the kernel does not draw the uniform random sample (the RNG stream stays aligned with the cpp tracer at exact ties). Moves with \(\Delta\Sigma > 0\) commit with probability exponentially decaying in the cost.

how the hierarchy composes

Level 0's mode partition collapses into the level-1 graph: vertices are the non-empty level-0 blocks renumbered \(0 \ldots B_{\text{ne}} - 1\), edges carry the level-0 \(e_{rs}\) counts as weights, and self-loops carry \(e_{rr}/2\) per the doubled-diagonal convention. Level 1 is itself a flat NDC BlockState over that smaller multigraph. The nested total \(\Sigma_{\text{nested-DC}} = \Sigma^{(0)}_{\text{DC}} + \sum_{l \ge 1} \Sigma^{(l)}_{\text{NDC}}\) is self-consistent across levels. The walker on this page animates level 0; the full multi-level \(e_{rs}\) propagation runs end-to-end in the kernel and the cpp tracer matches it byte-for-byte under the 3-tier stress panel.

A's \(K_5\) almost always anchors a level-0 block: the internal edges are dense, so peeling A's hub away from its periphery pays a steep sparse-entropy cost that DC absorbs through the model. B's bridge often stays bridged; C's two 3-cycles split into two blocks (no edges between them); D is seed-dependent. Under nested, the upper levels would package A and D into one super-block when the cross-edges between them are dense relative to other inter-block pairs, but level 0 alone reads the same as the flat-dc page on this fixture.

contrast against the NDC sister page

The nested-ndc page walks the same fixture with NDC entropy at every level (including level 0). On A, the level-0 NDC entropy pays the same hub-vs-leaf penalty as the flat-ndc page, and the chain often peels node 0 into its own block at level 0. The nested-DC vs. nested-NDC \(\Delta\Sigma\) on this fixture is in the same ballpark as the flat-DC vs. flat-NDC gap, plus or minus a few nats for the level-1+ contributions. The nested-best page surfaces the comparison directly.
