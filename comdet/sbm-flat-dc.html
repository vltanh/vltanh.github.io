SBM-flat-dc

degree-corrected flat stochastic block model

Per-vertex Metropolis-Hastings over the microcanonical posterior under a degree-conditional prior. The variant that survives heavy-tailed degree distributions.

Draft. Walker for the DC variant of the flat SBM. The family-level Bayesian framing and the cross-algorithm contrasts live on the SBM-flat-best anchor page; this page walks the DC-specific entropy form one substep at a time on the 32-node fixture, then contrasts DC against its NDC sister. Byte-equal to the cpp tracer under matched MT19937 across the 17-fixture 3-tier stress panel.

The degree-corrected flat SBM conditions the likelihood on the observed degree sequence \(\boldsymbol{k}\). In plain terms: per-node degrees are treated as known auxiliary data, so the partition does not have to explain degree heterogeneity through block membership. A degree-9 hub and a degree-2 leaf can share a block as long as their connection neighbourhoods agree at the block level. The non-degree-corrected sister (flat-ndc) drops this conditioning; the planted-partition sister (flat-pp) collapses the matrix into two scalars. The anchor page (flat-best) ranks all three by total \(\Sigma\) on the same fixture.

The description length the chain minimises is

\(\Sigma_{\text{DC}}(\boldsymbol{b}) = S_{\text{DC}}(\boldsymbol{e}, \boldsymbol{b}) + L_e(B, E) + L_p(\boldsymbol{b}) + L_d(\boldsymbol{e}, \boldsymbol{b})\),

four terms in nats. \(S_{\text{DC}}\) is the DC sparse entropy (data fit under degree conditioning); \(L_e\) is the cost of writing the edge-count matrix; \(L_p\) is the cost of writing the labelling; \(L_d\) is the uniform degree DL that prices the per-block degree sums. The DC variant is the only flat variant that pays \(L_d\), which is the price for conditioning on \(\boldsymbol{k}\).

Two properties. Every term is the log of a counting problem on the graph or the partition, so the four are commensurable inside DC and their sum is the negative log of the joint microcanonical posterior. \(S_{\text{DC}}\) is exact and lgamma-based, not a sparse-Stirling approximation, so DC's per-partition ranking is the principled MAP under the chosen prior; absolute \(\Sigma\) is not directly comparable across DC, NDC, and PP, and the flat-best page handles cross-variant ranking via pairwise gaps.

edges DL \(L_e = \log\binom{B(B+1)/2 + E - 1}{E}\). Cost of writing \(\boldsymbol{e}\) given \(B\). Grows with \(B\), penalises over-splitting.
partition DL \(L_p = \log(N!) - \sum_r \log(n_r!) + \log\binom{N - 1}{B - 1} + \log N\). Cost of the labelling under a hierarchical prior.

stage 1 · why degree correction

The hub-vs-leaf gap, and what NDC does with it.

Region A's degrees range from 9 at the hub (node 0) to 2 at the outer periphery leaves. The plot below shows the per-node degree across all 32 nodes, coloured by ground-truth. Node 0 sits well above the rest of A. Under an exchangeable-within-block likelihood (NDC), every internal stub of node 0 costs \(\log n_A\) nats. For \(n_A = 12\), node 0's stubs alone cost \(9 \log 12 \approx 22.4\) nats, most of A's data-fit budget spent on a single vertex.

degree of every node · coloured by planted block hub = node 0 (degree 9)

Under NDC, two cheap moves lower the hub's contribution: group node 0 with other high-degree nodes (rare on this fixture), or split it into a singleton block where \(n_r = 1\) and every stub costs \(\log 1 = 0\) nats. The singleton route pays an extra block in the partition DL and a larger \(\boldsymbol{e}\) matrix in the edges DL, but on this fixture that trade favours splitting. The map below highlights the nodes whose ground-truth label NDC most often splits across blocks. DC's degree conditioning folds the hub-leaf gap into the model itself, and A stays one community.

NDC's expected hub-peel pattern on the fixture red ring = often peeled by NDC; blue fill = ground-truth

Two properties. The cost gap NDC pays at the hub is \(9 \log 12 - \log(9!) \approx 9.6\) nats, and replicated across any hub-anchored community that gap drives the bulk of the DC-vs-NDC \(\Delta\Sigma\). The gap also grows with both the hub's degree and the block size; on this fixture only A carries the pattern visibly, but on heavy-tailed empirical graphs (citation, web, biological) every community is a hub-leaf mixture and the gap accumulates across hundreds of blocks. The full per-variant ranking lives on the flat-best page.

stage 2 · the four-term description length

What DC pays for, term by term.

DC's total description length decomposes into four terms. Three are shared with NDC (the partition DL is shared with PP as well); the highlighted two are DC-specific. The shared partition DL pays for writing the labelling under a hierarchical prior; the edges DL pays for writing the full \(\boldsymbol{e}\) matrix once \(B\) is known; the sparse entropy pays for the per-stub block assignment given the matrix. DC adds the uniform degree DL, which prices the per-node degree sequence given the block-level totals, and DC replaces NDC's vertex factor \(e_r \log n_r\) with \(\log(e_r!)\) inside the sparse entropy.

termformulaplain-English read
sparse entropy (DC vertex factor) \(S_{\text{DC}} = \sum_r \log(e_r!) - \sum_{r < s} \log(e_{rs}!) - \sum_r \log\!\big((e_{rr}/2)!\big) - (e_{rr}/2)\log 2\) orderings of the \(e_r\) half-stubs at every block, discounted by the redundancy from pairing them inside and across pairs
edges DL \(L_e = \log\!\binom{B(B{+}1)/2 + E - 1}{E}\) cost of writing the full \(\boldsymbol{e}\) matrix once \(B\) is known
partition DL \(L_p = \log(N!) - \sum_r \log(n_r!) + \log\!\binom{N - 1}{B - 1} + \log N\) cost of writing which node went into which block
degree DL (uniform) \(L_d = \sum_r \log\!\binom{n_r + e_r - 1}{e_r}\) cost of writing the per-node degrees given block sums

The DC-only terms together encode the model's stance on degrees. The vertex factor \(\log(e_r!)\) prices the orderings of the half-stubs inside a block when the per-node degrees are already given; it replaces NDC's \(e_r \log n_r\) which prices the per-stub block lookup under uniform-within-block placement. The uniform degree DL pays the constant cost (over all block-level degree compositions) of locating the per-node degrees inside the block-level totals.

stage 3 · random init

Random partition with \(B_0\) blocks; \(\Sigma_0\) on display.

MCMC starts from any valid partition. The chain initialises with \(B_0 = \) 8 blocks: the kernel shuffles the identity permutation under MT19937 and assigns block \(i \bmod B_0\) to vertex \(\sigma(i)\). The shuffle is backward Fisher-Yates, matching the std::shuffle direction in the cpp tracer. Rerolling the seed redraws this partition and re-runs the entire chain on the new init; every downstream stage refreshes from the same run.

re-runs the chain · init + 20 sweeps

\(\Sigma_0\) is large because the random partition is uninformative. Edges cross block boundaries roughly uniformly, the sparse-entropy term gets no concentration discount, and the chain still pays the full edges DL plus partition DL plus degree DL. The chain lowers \(\Sigma\) by re-routing nodes into blocks where their edge neighbourhoods cohere. At \(\beta = 1\) the chain samples the posterior \(P(\boldsymbol{b} \mid \boldsymbol{A})\), so multiple chains from different inits trace out different posterior samples; the mode is one of several quantities of interest.

stage 4 · MH step 1 · build the candidate pool

For one vertex, which target blocks are on the table.

An MH visit runs in four substages, this stage being the first. The walker on stage 7 below runs all four in sequence on every vertex of every sweep; the next four stages take one substage at a time on a single visit so the structure of each step is visible. The example visit shown here is the first MH visit of sweep 1 at seed 7 (vertex ·, current block ·).

The candidate pool is the set of target blocks the vertex can propose to. Every currently non-empty block is included, and when the vertex's current block holds more than one member, one fresh empty slot is added; the fresh slot is what lets the chain change \(B\). The c→∞ limit of Peixoto's local-neighbourhood proposal then samples one target uniformly from the pool.

Two properties. The proposal is symmetric in the Hastings sense, so the acceptance ratio reduces to \(e^{-\beta \Delta\Sigma}\) with no correction term. Convergence is slower than under the canonical \(c = 1\) proposal, but the trajectories are easier to read because the bias toward a vertex's neighbour blocks is absent.

candidate pool for vertex · · current block highlighted mint = pool members; ring = current block

The fresh-block proposal is the c→∞ analogue of Peixoto's "open a new block" move. When the vertex is the only member of its block (a singleton) and the chain proposes a fresh block, the move is structurally a no-op: the singleton becomes a singleton elsewhere with the same \(n_r\), the same \(e_r\), the same \(e_{rs}\). The kernel detects this case and short-circuits to \(\Delta\Sigma = 0\), which keeps the RNG draw count aligned with the cpp tracer at exact ties.

stage 5 · MH step 2 · entropy delta with DC terms

What changes in \(\Sigma\) when one vertex moves from \(r\) to \(s\).

Computing \(\Delta\Sigma\) from scratch on every proposal would be quadratic in the partition size and prohibitive past a few thousand vertices. The kernel uses the closed-form subset-entropy delta instead: moving vertex \(v\) from block \(r\) to block \(s\) changes only the entropy terms that involve row \(r\), column \(r\), row \(s\), or column \(s\) of the \(\boldsymbol{e}\) matrix, plus the per-block sums \(n_r\), \(n_s\), \(e_r\), \(e_s\). The kernel maintains a running tracker of these quantities and computes the before-after difference directly, never recomputing the full entropy. Per visit the cost is \(O(\deg(v) + B_{\text{ne}})\) on the comdet fixture.

The four-row \(\Delta\) is shown below, with the DC-specific rows highlighted. On most visits the sparse-entropy delta dominates the total; the degree DL contributes single-digit nats per move because it only changes when the block-level sums \(n_r\), \(e_r\), \(n_s\), \(e_s\) shift. Both DC-only deltas are sensitive to operand order, so the kernel walks the sorted neighbour list in a fixed order to keep the floating-point sum deterministic. Reordering the operands would introduce sub-ulp drift that on long chains would surface as different tie-break decisions.

stage 6 · MH step 3 · the acceptance ratio

How \(\Delta\Sigma\) maps to a probability of moving.

Once \(\Delta\Sigma\) is known, the chain accepts the proposed move with probability

\(\alpha(\boldsymbol{b} \to \boldsymbol{b}') = \min\!\big(1, \, e^{-\beta \Delta\Sigma}\big)\),

where \(\beta\) is the inverse temperature. The page runs at \(\beta = 1\). In plain terms: any move that lowers \(\Sigma\) is accepted with probability 1; any move that raises \(\Sigma\) is accepted with probability decaying exponentially in the cost.

Two properties. At \(\beta = 1\) the chain's stationary distribution is \(P(\boldsymbol{b} \mid \boldsymbol{A}) \propto e^{-\Sigma(\boldsymbol{b})}\), so long enough runs sample the posterior, not just the mode. \(\beta = 0\) reduces the chain to a uniform random walk, \(\beta \to \infty\) reduces it to pure greedy descent; the kernel exposes \(\beta\) as a parameter, and the page is fixed at 1.

\(\alpha(\Delta\Sigma) = \min(1, e^{-\Delta\Sigma})\) at \(\beta = 1\) mint dot = the example visit (\(\Delta\Sigma\) = ·)

Two thresholds matter for tie-break. At \(\Delta\Sigma \le 0\) the kernel accepts without drawing the uniform random sample (the RNG stream stays aligned with the cpp tracer at exact ties); the curve is flat at 1 for the entire negative half-line. At \(\Delta\Sigma > 0\) the kernel draws \(u \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 1)\) and accepts iff \(u < e^{-\Delta\Sigma}\); the strict less-than is what makes the boundary deterministic at exact equality. why MH instead of greedy

A pure greedy step (always take the best candidate) collapses to a local minimum within one sweep, at a partition that is roughly right but cannot escape. The stochastic acceptance pays a small uphill cost in exchange for the option to cross saddle points. Graph-tool's canonical multilevel sweep runs single-vertex MH alongside agglomerative merges that propose joining two whole blocks at once; the merges give the canonical sampler its fast convergence on large graphs. The walker on this page uses single-vertex moves only.

NMI is the normalised mutual information between found and planted labellings; ARI is the adjusted Rand index. Both are 1 at exact match (modulo label permutation) and 0 at random; ARI can go negative on worse-than-random partitions. On this fixture the two outliers and C's disconnected components are the predictable disagreements; the gap to perfect match is small in nats but visible in both metrics. The NMI calculation here uses the natural-log variant for commensurability with the entropy units used elsewhere on the page.

stage 12 · DC's failure mode

When degree correction over-corrects.

Conditioning on the observed degree sequence makes degree heterogeneity invisible to the partition prior. On graphs where degree variation carries cluster signal (a community of hubs adjacent to a community of leaves, with the hub-leaf split itself being the planted structure), DC folds that signal into the model and the partition no longer carries it. The NDC variant treats degree variation as data and recovers the split. The contrast is the mirror of stage 11's: on heavy-tailed graphs DC produces lower \(\Sigma\), on near-uniform-degree graphs with degree-coded clusters DC under-fits and NDC takes over.

The comdet fixture does not exercise this failure cleanly; every planted community on it is degree-heterogeneous, which favours DC. The illustration below is a sketch instead of a fixture run: a 24-node toy with two degree-coded clusters (a 12-hub set with internal degree 4, a 12-leaf set with internal degree 1, the only inter-block edges being random hub-leaf pairings). DC labels the toy as one block of 24; NDC splits it into the two planted clusters.

illustrative toy · degree-coded two-cluster graph DC: one block of 24 · NDC: 12 + 12

Two properties. The over-correction tracks per-block degree variance, not the absolute degree level: a community where every node has degree exactly \(k\) next to another where every node has degree exactly \(k'\) is the worst case for DC, regardless of the gap \(|k - k'|\). The standard recipe is what the flat-best page implements: run both DC and NDC, compare \(\Sigma\), and read the lower as the better-fitting prior. The two variants are alternative explanations of the same data under the Bayesian framing, and model selection is the mechanism that picks between them.

where DC fits

Empirical networks with heavy-tailed degree distributions, especially where the planted or hypothesised structure spans hubs and leaves inside one cluster. Web graphs, citation graphs, biological interaction graphs, social media graphs. On these DC's \(\Sigma\) sits well below NDC's at any partition with non-singleton communities containing hubs. The hub-peel signature on the NDC sister page is the diagnostic.
