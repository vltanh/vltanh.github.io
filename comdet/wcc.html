WCC

well-connected components · Park 2025 / Vu-Le 2026, threshold per Park 2024

A size-scaled connectedness check on top of CC. Clusters with a thin edge bridge pass CC and still look suspect; WCC rejects them with a log-scaled mincut threshold and recursively peels the cut until each survivor clears the bar.

Draft. Under-construction trace for the WCC post-processor on the shared 32-node fixture. The page keeps under-construction status until reviewed; read the walker as an explanatory trace of the mincut-threshold recursion.

CC asks whether a cluster is connected. WCC asks whether it is well-connected. The binary check becomes a sliding threshold scaled to the cluster's size: the mincut of cluster \(C\) has to strictly exceed \(f(|C|)\). If yes, keep. If no, cut at the most balanced minimum cut and recurse on each connected side. CM sits one step heavier: same recursion, with a base-algorithm replay on each cut half.

The first move is still CC's residual-component pre-pass: drop cross-label edges, split disconnected labels into connected pieces, and discard singletons. From there WCC drains a FIFO queue. Each pop calls VieCut, reads back a cut value \(c\) and a most-balanced bipartition, compares \(c\) with \(f(n)\), then either keeps the cluster or pushes connected pieces from the two cut sides back onto the queue.

On the shared fixture, A fails because a one-edge pendant cut is below \(\log_{10}(12)\). B and D pass because their sizes put the default threshold below 1. The threshold is global policy, so sparse planted clusters, paths, and stars can be dissolved even when they are semantically one community. Exact ties fail too: the predicate is strict, with a \(10^{-9}\) tolerance band on the conservative side.

roadmap · algorithm contract

Think of WCC as a queue of candidate clusters.

WCC does not invent communities from scratch. It audits an existing partition. The audit has one invariant: every item in the queue is connected, has more than one node, and still needs a well-connectedness decision.

Step 1. Start with a labelled graph from a base method: planted labels on this page, or Leiden, Infomap, or SBM in a chained run. The original graph stays fixed; the membership vector is the object being audited. Step 2. Run the connected-component pre-pass so every candidate is connected before mincut is asked anything.

Step 3. Pop one connected candidate, compute its minimum cut, and compare the cut to the size-scaled bar. A pass becomes final. A fail is cut into smaller connected queue items. Step 4. At the end, relabel survivors in pop order. Singletons and peeled-off size-1 fragments remain unlabelled as \(-1\).

queue invariant

Every queued item is connected, has more than one node, and has not yet cleared the well-connectedness predicate. That invariant is what lets the recursion stay iterative: no backtracking is needed, because every failed pop only creates strictly smaller connected candidates.

stage 1 · input

The base clustering on the 32-node fixture.

Four planted communities labelled A, B, C, D plus two unlabelled outliers (30, 31). A is a 12-node \(K_5\) core with seven periphery nodes attached. B is two \(K_4\)'s joined by one bridge edge. C is two disjoint 3-cycles sharing one label. D is a 4-node path. Shared with the CC and CM pages.

stage 2 · the threshold

One inequality, evaluated on each cluster.

The well-connectedness test sits on a single inequality:

\[ \text{mincut}(C) \;>\; f(|C|) \]

In words: the smallest edge cut of cluster \(C\) must strictly exceed the threshold evaluated at the cluster's own size \(|C|\). Two interesting properties: the verdict reads only the cluster's internal structure, so the same cluster lifted into a larger graph gets the same answer; and the comparison is strict, so a cluster whose mincut exactly meets the threshold fails, not passes.

stage 3 · why \(\log_{10}(n)\)

The threshold scales with cluster size, slowly.

Park 2024 argues the bar from a tail bound on stochastic block models: a planted community drawn from a block has, with high probability, no cut smaller than \(\Theta(\log n)\). A constant bound like "mincut at least 2" catches the bridge case at small \(n\) and then stops being informative once clusters reach the hundreds. Logs grow slower than any polynomial, so the bar stays mild even at \(|V| = 10^6\), where \(\log_{10}(n) = 6\). The default constants are \(C = 1, x = 10\), giving \(f(n) = \log_{10}(n)\).

stage 5 · connected-component pre-pass

Drop inter-cluster edges, split disconnected clusters, seed the queue.

Same first step as CC: bucket nodes by label, drop every edge whose endpoints carry different labels, breadth-first-search each induced subgraph, drop components of size 1. The surviving multi-node components become the initial queue for the cut loop. C's two 3-cycles split into C\(_1\) and C\(_2\) here; the cut loop never sees a single \(n = 6\) cluster.

Why split first, then test well-connectedness. Mincut is undefined on a disconnected graph: the cut value is trivially 0 and the bipartition is arbitrary. The pre-pass is the cheap shortcut, and it costs one breadth-first search per cluster.

Step 1. Bucket nodes by input label. Step 2. Keep only residual edges where membership[u] === membership[v]. Step 3. Run BFS inside each bucket so every disconnected label becomes one candidate per connected piece. Step 4. Drop components of size one. Step 5. Push every remaining component into the FIFO queue.

stage 6 · per-pop mechanics

Five phases inside every queue pop.

Once the queue is seeded, the cut loop drains it FIFO. Each pop runs the same five phases.

Step 1. Pull the head of the queue: cur = queue.shift(). Recursion is represented as a work list, not as a call stack. Step 2. Induce the subgraph on the candidate's nodes, because WCC judges only internal cohesion. Step 3. Hand that induced graph to VieCut and read back the minimum cut value \(c\) plus the most-balanced bipartition among tied minimum cuts.

Step 4. Evaluate \(f(n)\) on this candidate's size, not on the whole graph size. The implementation precomputes \(C / \ln(x)\), so a log-family threshold costs one log call and one multiply per pop. Step 5. Compare under the strict-above rule, with a \(10^{-9}\) tolerance band that treats near-ties as failures.

pass and fail branches

If \(c\) is strictly above \(f(n)\), the candidate is well-connected under the selected criterion; it joins the survivor list and receives an output id later in survivor order. If \(c\) is at or below \(f(n)\), the mincut is evidence that the cluster is held together too weakly, so each cut side is split into connected components and every size-greater-than-1 component is pushed back, in-side first and then out-side. 

queue = connected_components(residual_graph).filter(size > 1)
survivors = []

while queue is not empty:
    C = queue.pop_front()
    S, T, c = most_balanced_mincut(G induced by C)
    t = f(size(C))

    if c > t and abs(c - t) > 1e-9:
        survivors.push(C)
    else:
        for side in [S, T]:
            for H in connected_components(G induced by side):
                if size(H) > 1:
                    queue.push_back(H)

Three interesting properties of this routine. Survivors are immutable: once a cluster passes, it leaves the queue and never returns. Push-back components are strictly smaller than the popped cluster, so the recursion terminates in at most \(n\) pops on a worst-case 1-cut chain. Survivors carry no input label; output ids run \(0, 1, 2, \dots\) in survivor pop order.

stage 7 · recursive cut walker

Mincut, threshold check, repeat.

The walker advances one queue pop at a time. The picker re-runs the kernel on the new criterion and re-mounts the walker with the same input partition. Default is \(f(n) = \log_{10}(n)\), the Park 2024 default.

Read the canvas as the queue's live partition, not as a new base clustering. After a failed pop, one coloured chunk is replaced by the connected components pushed back from the two cut sides. The phase strip follows the implementation order from the previous section: pull, mincut, threshold, verdict, action. The gauge is the verdict: the integer cut value sits left or right of the selected threshold, with exact log ties forced to fail.

The queue and survivor panels make the recursion explicit. New components appear at the queue tail, survivors leave the queue forever, and no backtracking is needed because each pushed item is smaller than the failed parent.

stage 8 · cluster A, initial state

The only cluster that fails on first sight.

A enters the cut loop at \(n = 12\): the \(K_5\) core on \(\{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}\) plus four 1-edge pendants (5, 6, 7, 11) and three 2-edge periphery nodes (8, 9, 10). VieCut returns a mincut of 1 (any of the four 1-edge pendants). Among the four tied 1-cuts, all peel a single node, so the most-balanced rule picks one of them; the choice is a VieCut-internal tie-break. Threshold at \(n = 12\) is \(\log_{10}(12) \approx 1.079\). The verdict is fail: 1 is not strictly above 1.079.

The cut is local: remove one pendant edge and one node separates, so a single weak appendage is enough to make the whole 12-node cluster have mincut 1. The bar is just above 1, so the strict test \(1 > 1.079\) is false. Failing does not delete the rest of A; the one-node cut side is filtered, and the connected 11-node side becomes the first recursive work item.

stage 9 · A pop 1

Peel one pendant. Push the 11-node side back to the queue.

VieCut returns the bipartition (1 pendant, 11 rest). The 1-side fails the size-greater-than-1 push-back gate and vanishes. The 11-side joins the queue tail. The peeled pendant becomes one of nodes 5, 6, 7, 11; which one depends on VieCut's tie-break.

stage 10 · A pop 2

Same shape, smaller cluster. \(n = 11\), cut 1, threshold 1.041.

The 11-node residual still has 1-edge pendants in it, so the mincut is still 1. Threshold at \(n = 11\) is \(\log_{10}(11) \approx 1.041\). Fail again. Another pendant peels; the 10-node side returns to the queue.

stage 11 · A pop 3

Threshold lands on the integer. The epsilon band catches it.

At \(n = 10\), \(\log_{10}(n) = 1.000\) exactly. Mincut is still 1. Under the strict-above rule, 1 is not greater than 1, so the verdict is fail; the \(10^{-9}\) tolerance band confirms it (the gap is zero, well inside the band). A third pendant peels. The 9-node side returns to the queue.

stage 12 · A pop 4

The 9-node residual clears the bar.

At \(n = 9\), threshold drops to \(\log_{10}(9) \approx 0.954\). Mincut is still 1. The verdict is pass: 1 strictly exceeds 0.954. The 9-node side joins the survivor list. Binary-verified, the surviving set is \(\{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 10, 11\}\): the \(K_5\) core, the three 2-edge periphery nodes, and one 1-edge pendant that the peel sequence happened to leave behind. The three peeled pendants are some permutation of \(\{5, 6, 7\}\); peel order varies with VieCut tie-breaks, but the survivor set is stable.

The four-pop sequence is one threshold crossing. At \(n = 12\) and \(n = 11\), the log bar is above 1, so each pop fails and peels a size-1 side. At \(n = 10\), the bar ties the cut exactly and the strict comparison still fails. At \(n = 9\), the bar finally drops below 1, so the same mincut value passes.

stage 13 · cluster B

One bridge edge, passes on first sight.

B is two \(K_4\)'s joined by the edge (15, 16). Mincut is 1 (the bridge). Threshold at \(n = 8\) is \(\log_{10}(8) \approx 0.903\). \(1 > 0.903\), so B passes. One pop, no peeling. The single-bridge cluster of size 8 counts as well-connected by the log family. Doubling the coefficient to \(2 \log_{10}(n)\) raises the bar at \(n = 8\) to 1.806 and flips B's verdict to fail; the picker in stage 7 exposes that flip.

stage 14 · clusters C, D

The disconnected one was already split. The path-shaped one passes.

C entered the queue as two separate 3-cycles via the connected-component pre-pass. Each has mincut 2 and threshold 0.477 at \(n = 3\); both pass on first sight. D is a 4-node path with mincut 1; threshold at \(n = 4\) is 0.602, so D passes. Three small clusters, three single-pop survivors.

stage 15 · what falls off

Size-1 components are dropped at every push-back gate.

The push-back loop walks each side's connected components and pushes only the size-greater-than-1 ones. Size-1 fragments are filtered, both in the initial connected-component pre-pass and inside the cut loop. On this fixture, three peeled pendants from A and the two outliers (30, 31) all fall through this gate. The output has no entries for them; downstream tools read them as unlabelled.

stage 17 · siblings in the family

WCC sits between CC and CM in cost and reach.

CC stops after the pre-pass. Every connected component of the residual graph is a survivor; no threshold, no recursion. CM goes one step further than WCC: each failing cluster gets cut, and each side is re-clustered by the base algorithm (Leiden, in the shipped binary) before any survivors are accepted. WCC splits only; CM splits and re-clusters. On the fixture under \(\log_{10}\): CC keeps all five connected pieces (A intact at 12 nodes, C split into two cycles, B and D intact); WCC peels A to 9 nodes; CM peels A and then re-clusters the 9-node residual into whatever the inner Leiden call returns.

stage 18 · chain composition

WCC is post-processing; the base clustering feeds it.

In the chained verification matrix, WCC sits downstream of four bases: Leiden modularity, Leiden CPM at resolution 0.5, Infomap, and SBM-Flat-PP. Each base produces an input partition; WCC reads that partition and emits a survivor partition. The stress grid injects nine seeds into the tracer to exercise equal-cut paths; the shipped executable itself starts at fixed seed 0.

stage 19 · failure mode

Sparse-by-design clusters fragment.

Tree-shaped clusters (a star, a path, any cluster whose mincut is structurally 1) fail every non-trivial log or sqrt threshold and break down to singletons or pairs. The output drops them entirely. Vu-Le 2026 reports this failure mode on benchmarks with sparse planted communities; the picker in stage 7 reproduces it by turning the bar up. The trade-off is global: the threshold applies to every cluster regardless of provenance, so a planted community that happens to look tree-like is misread the same way an over-merged cluster is.
