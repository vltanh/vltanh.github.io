SBM-flat-ndc

non-degree-corrected flat stochastic block model

Posit an exchangeable-within-block generative model; minimise the description length \(\Sigma\) by single-vertex Metropolis-Hastings.

Draft. Walker over a JS port of graph-tool's flat BlockState at deg_corr=False. Sparse-entropy formulas verbatim from graph_blockmodel_entropy.hh; DL priors from Peixoto's review (10.1002/9781119483298.ch11) Eq 17 + 23. Proposal is uniform over current blocks (the \(c \to \infty\) limit of graph-tool's sample_block). Byte-equal to the cpp tracer under matched MT19937 across the 17-fixture 3-tier stress panel; cross-check vs. canonical graph-tool output is on the follow-up.

Community detection as Bayesian inference: posit a generative model where each node carries a block label \(b_i \in \{1,\dots,B\}\), the model freezes the count of edges between every pair of blocks \(e_{rs}\), and the actual graph is a uniform draw from the simple graphs that meet those constraints. The inference problem is the inverse: given the observed graph, find the partition that compresses it the most.

The microcanonical formulation gives a clean target: the partition that minimises the description length \(\Sigma = -\log P(\boldsymbol{A}, \boldsymbol{e}, \boldsymbol{b})\). Each candidate partition carries a cost in nats, split between data fit (sparse entropy of \(\boldsymbol{e}\)) and model complexity (writing down \(B\), \(\boldsymbol{b}\), \(\boldsymbol{e}\)). A single-block partition fits the data perfectly but pays no model-cost discount; an \(N\)-singleton partition spends its whole budget on writing down which node is where. The minimum sits in the middle.

The NDC variant does not condition on the observed degree sequence. The model treats every node in a block as exchangeable, so degree heterogeneity ends up in the data-fit term rather than the model. On networks with heavy-tailed degrees, NDC tends to split hubs and leaves into separate blocks even when their connection neighbourhoods coincide. The sister DC page handles the same fixture under a degree-conditional prior. The walker pushes \(\Sigma\) down by single-vertex MH moves: pick a vertex, sample a target block uniformly, compute \(\Delta\Sigma\), accept with \(\min(1, e^{-\beta\Delta\Sigma})\) at \(\beta = 1\). Iterate until \(\Sigma\) stops dropping.

stage 1 · random init

Random partition with \(B_0\) blocks; \(\Sigma_0\) on display.

MCMC starts from any valid partition. The chain initialises with \(B_0 = \) 8 blocks: the kernel shuffles the identity permutation under MT19937 and assigns block \(i \bmod B_0\) to vertex \(\sigma(i)\). The shuffle is backward Fisher-Yates, matching std::shuffle in the cpp tracer.

\(\Sigma_0\) is large because the random partition is uninformative: edges cross block boundaries roughly uniformly, the sparse-entropy term gets no concentration discount, and the chain still pays the full edges-DL + partition-DL cost. MCMC's job is to push \(\Sigma\) down by re-routing nodes.

stage 2 · one move at a time

Each visit: pick a vertex, propose a target block, accept or reject.

One sweep visits every vertex once in shuffled order. Each visit runs four steps:

  1. Build the candidate pool: every currently non-empty block, plus one fresh empty slot when the vertex's current block holds more than one member.
  2. Sample a target uniformly from the pool (the \(c \to \infty\) limit of graph-tool's sample_block; the canonical proposal weights candidates by neighbour-block frequencies).
  3. Compute \(\Delta\Sigma\) for the move via virtualMove: apply the move on a transient state, recompute the subset entropy that touches the two blocks, revert.
  4. Accept with \(\min(1, e^{-\beta\Delta\Sigma})\) at \(\beta = 1\). On accept, commit; on reject, leave the partition unchanged.

Moves with \(\Delta\Sigma \le 0\) always commit (the RNG draw is skipped to keep the stream aligned with the cpp tracer). Moves with \(\Delta\Sigma > 0\) commit with exponentially-decaying probability, which gives the chain room to walk over saddle points. Over enough sweeps the chain samples from the posterior \(P(\boldsymbol{b} \mid \boldsymbol{A}) \propto e^{-\Sigma}\); on the 32-node fixture, 20 sweeps reaches the mode.

A negative \(\Delta\Sigma\) means the move improves \(\Sigma\); a small positive \(\Delta\Sigma\) is gambler-friendly (likely accepted at \(\beta = 1\)); a large positive \(\Delta\Sigma\) is almost always rejected.

why MH instead of greedy

A pure greedy step (always take the best candidate) collapses to a local minimum quickly; \(\Sigma\) plateaus within one sweep at a partition that is roughly right but stuck. The stochastic acceptance trades a small uphill cost for the freedom to walk over saddle points. Graph-tool's multilevel_mcmc_sweep runs single-vertex MH alongside agglomerative merges that propose joining two whole blocks at once; the merges are what give the canonical sampler its fast convergence on large graphs. The walker on this page uses single-vertex moves only: same algorithm, less plumbing.

stage 3 · equilibration

The trace plots \(\Sigma\) (blue, left axis) and the number of non-empty blocks \(B\) (ochre, right axis) after each sweep. The chain has equilibrated when both stop drifting. Early sweeps see big drops in \(\Sigma\) as obvious moves commit. Later sweeps oscillate as the chain explores partitions of similar quality near the posterior mode.

A still-falling trace at the right edge means the chain has not equilibrated and the sweep count is too low. On this fixture 20 sweeps usually settle the chain; rerolling the seed shows the equilibrium \(\Sigma\) is roughly stable but the equilibrium \(B\) wobbles by 1–2. Under NDC the wobble is wider than under DC because the model is more sensitive to hub-placement choices.
