SBM-nested-ndc

nested (hierarchical) non-degree-corrected stochastic block model

Hierarchy without per-level degree priors. Cleaner regularisation on small homogeneous fixtures; weaker on heavy-tailed degree sequences. The page walks every level in turn.

Draft, level-0 walker animated. Walker for the NDC variant of the nested SBM. The family-level Bayesian framing, the hierarchical construction, and the cross-algorithm contrasts live on the SBM-nested-best anchor page; this page is the lecture-style step-by-step for the NDC nested family. The kernel runs the full multi-level chain end-to-end and the cpp tracer plus JS replay leg exercise the hierarchy under self-RNG (306/306 cells PASS on the 3-tier stress panel). The page animates the level-0 trajectory directly and sketches the upper levels schematically.

The nested non-degree-corrected SBM applies NDC entropy at every level of a level stack. Level 0 is the input graph plus its block partition. Level 1 is the block-multigraph obtained by collapsing level 0's blocks into super-vertices. Levels above repeat the same construction until the top has one or two blocks. The hierarchy returns a tree of partitions at multiple scales instead of a single flat partition.

The description length the chain minimises is

\(\Sigma_{\text{nested-NDC}} = \sum_{l = 0}^{L - 1} \Sigma^{(l)}_{\text{NDC}}\),

where each \(\Sigma^{(l)}_{\text{NDC}}\) is the flat-NDC \(\Sigma\) on the level-\(l\) graph: sparse entropy NDC plus edges DL plus partition DL, with no degree DL at any level. In plain terms: pay the NDC cost at every level. Two properties. The level-0 NDC entropy carries the hub-leaf penalty the flat-ndc walker exposes, and the nested form does not modify that penalty, so the level-0 mode partition under nested-NDC matches the flat-ndc mode partition on the comdet fixture under most seeds. And the absence of the degree DL term everywhere simplifies the kernel's bookkeeping compared to nested-DC: both variants run the same round-robin MCMC but differ only in the entropy form at level 0.

level 0 NDC entropy on the input graph. The level-0 vertex factor is \(e_r \log n_r\).
level \(l > 0\) NDC entropy on the block-multigraph from level \(l - 1\). Identical to the upper-level construction in nested-DC.
\(\Sigma_{\text{nested-NDC}}\) sum of per-level NDC \(\Sigma\). Lower is better; no inter-level coupling term.
no degree DL anywhere NDC at every level skips the degree DL term that nested-DC pays at level 0.

On the 32-node fixture the asymptotic resolution-limit benefit of the nested form is mostly cosmetic. \(\sqrt{32} \approx 5.7\), so the flat SBM can already represent the 4-community ground-truth comfortably. The hierarchy starts paying for itself when the optimal block count exceeds \(\sqrt{N}\). What's interesting about this fixture for nested-NDC is the level-0 hub at node 0 (degree 9 inside a 12-member block): under NDC the chain often peels node 0 into its own level-0 block, and the level-1 graph then carries the peeled hub as a singleton super-vertex that may or may not re-merge upstairs.

stage 1 · why nested, why NDC

Hierarchy without per-level degree priors.

Two design choices stack here. The nested half says the partition should be a tree, not a list: every level-\(l\) block is a super-block of level-\((l-1)\) blocks, and the inference returns the whole tree at once. The NDC half says no level should condition on the degree sequence: every node in a block is treated as exchangeable, and degree heterogeneity surfaces through the data fit.

Why nested-NDC and not just nested-DC for everything. On graphs with roughly homogeneous within-block degrees, the degree prior in nested-DC pays a per-node bookkeeping cost that the model does not get back in data-fit improvement; the extra parameter slot regularises against noise that is not actually there. NDC is the cleaner choice when the within-block degree distribution is narrow. Two properties of this regime. The NDC sparse entropy reads block membership through the \(e_r \log n_r\) vertex factor, which is invariant under node relabelling inside a block; on homogeneous fixtures this matches what the data actually says. And the NDC chain pays no degree DL anywhere, so the absolute \(\Sigma_{\text{nested-NDC}}\) is always smaller than the corresponding \(\Sigma_{\text{nested-DC}}\) in raw nats. Smaller raw \(\Sigma\) values do not by themselves mean the model wins; the comparison goes through the Bayes-factor reading on the anchor nested-best page.

Why nested-NDC and not flat-NDC. The hierarchy buys a structural interpretation that the flat model cannot offer: even when the level-0 mode partition coincides with the flat-ndc mode partition, the level-1+ partition reads the cross-block edge pattern at a coarser scale, grouping blocks that interact more than expected under the level-0 \(e_{rs}\) baseline. On the comdet fixture the level-1 partition often groups A and D into one super-block (they share cross-edges through nodes 27, 28); flat-ndc has no place to express that observation.

stage 2 · description length

Sum of per-level NDC terms, no degree DL anywhere.

The objective the chain minimises decomposes per level. At level \(l\) the kernel evaluates the flat-NDC description length on the level-\(l\) graph and the level-\(l\) partition. The total is the sum.

\(\Sigma_{\text{nested-NDC}}(\boldsymbol{b}^{(0)}, \ldots, \boldsymbol{b}^{(L-1)}) = \sum_{l = 0}^{L - 1} \Big[ S^{(l)}_{\text{NDC}} + L_e^{(l)} + L_p^{(l)} \Big]\).

In plain terms: every level pays three terms, the same three the flat-NDC chain pays at level 0, computed on that level's graph and that level's partition. Two properties. The decomposition is fully additive across levels (no inter-level coupling term in this JS-side formulation; graph-tool's canonical adds a small \(\Lambda_{\text{rec dx}}\) coupling that the JS port intentionally omits, byte-equal to the cpp tracer). And the absolute \(\Sigma_{\text{nested-NDC}}\) is always larger than the corresponding flat \(\Sigma^{(0)}_{\text{NDC}}\) on a non-trivial hierarchy, because the level-1+ terms are positive (they pay extra DL for the hierarchy itself).

The two properties of the NDC sparse entropy worth keeping. The vertex factor \(e_r \log n_r\) charges \(\log n_r\) per internal stub: an internal edge incident to a degree-9 hub in a 12-member block pays \(9 \log 12 \approx 22.4\) nats, where the same edge incident to a degree-2 leaf pays \(2 \log 12 \approx 5\) nats. The factor is what makes NDC sensitive to hub-leaf splits. And the formula falls out of the Poisson stochastic block model in the dense-graph limit, which is why NDC is the form pre-2011 literature uses; DC is the post-2011 correction (Karrer and Newman 2011) for heavy-tailed degrees, and nested-DC carries it at level 0 only.

stage 3 · init

Random level-0 partition with \(B_0\) blocks; \(\Sigma_0\) on display.

MCMC starts from any valid partition. The level-0 chain initialises with \(B_0 = \) 8 blocks: the kernel shuffles the identity permutation under MT19937 and assigns block \(i \bmod B_0\) to vertex \(\sigma(i)\). The shuffle is backward Fisher-Yates, matching the std::shuffle direction in the cpp tracer.

re-runs the chain · init + 20 sweeps

\(\Sigma_0\) is large because the random partition is uninformative: edges cross block boundaries roughly uniformly, the sparse-entropy term gets no concentration discount, and the chain pays the full edges DL + partition DL at every level. Under NDC the level-0 chain has a strong incentive to either group hubs with other hubs or peel them into singletons, both of which lower the \(e_r \log n_r\) penalty; the upper levels then read the level-0 mode partition and adjust their own partitions during the next round-robin pass.

stage 4 · round-robin init

Level-by-level pass; every level gets a starting partition before the MH chain runs.

The nested kernel maintains a list of flat block states, one per level. Before the MH chain starts, the level stack is initialised top-down: level 0 from the random partition above, level \(l > 0\) from a one-block-per-vertex partition on the level-\(l\) graph (the level-\(l\) graph itself is built from the level-\((l-1)\) partition that the lower level just finished initialising).

round-robin init · one starting partition per level depth fixed at \(L = \lceil \log_2 N \rceil = 5\)
level 0 input graph (\(N_0 = 32\), \(E_0 = 52\)); random partition with \(B_0 = 8\) blocks walked one MH move at a time below
level 1 block-multigraph from level 0 (\(N_1 = B_0\)); each level-1 vertex starts in its own block (\(B_1 = N_1\)) collapses during sweep 1 at level 1
level 2 block-multigraph from level 1; each vertex in its own block rebuilt after each level-1 sweep
level 3 same, smaller rebuilt after each level-2 sweep
level 4 (top) typically one or two blocks; chain ends here no rebuild above

Two properties of the round-robin init. Every level above 0 starts in the most permissive state (one block per vertex), so the MH sweep at that level has the maximum freedom to find structure; the alternative (one super-block containing everything) starts from the most restrictive state and tends to lock in trivial partitions for long stretches. And the depth \(L\) is fixed at \(\lceil \log_2 N \rceil\) (5 levels for \(N = 32\)); levels with trivial structure pay near-zero DL because their partition collapses to one block on the first sweep, so over-allocating depth is cheap.

stage 5 · level-0 MH sweep

Each visit: pick a vertex, propose a target block, accept or reject.

The level-0 sweep is identical to the flat-NDC walker. One sweep visits every level-0 vertex once in shuffled order. Each visit runs four steps:

  1. Build the candidate pool: every currently non-empty block at level 0, plus one fresh empty slot when the vertex's current block holds more than one member.
  2. Sample a target uniformly from the pool (the \(c \to \infty\) limit of Peixoto's local-neighbourhood proposal).
  3. Compute \(\Delta\Sigma\) for the move via the subset-entropy delta on the level-0 NDC entropy.
  4. Accept with probability \(\min(1, e^{-\beta\Delta\Sigma})\) at \(\beta = 1\). On accept, commit; on reject, leave the partition unchanged.

The \(\Delta\Sigma\) at each visit is the level-0 NDC delta only. The upper-level contributions to \(\Sigma_{\text{nested-NDC}}\) are deterministic given the level-0 partition, so they do not enter the per-visit acceptance ratio; the round-robin pass picks them up between sweeps. The NDC delta has the same closed-form structure as DC's, but the vertex factor is \(e_r \log n_r\) instead of \(\log(e_r!)\); the per-visit \(|\Delta\Sigma|\) values tend to be larger in absolute size than DC's because the NDC vertex factor is sensitive to per-1 changes in \(n_r\).

stage 6 · level-0 to level-1 rebuild

Collapse the level-0 mode into the level-1 graph.

After the level-0 sweep completes, the level-1 graph is rebuilt from the updated level-0 \(e^{(0)}\) matrix. The rebuild is deterministic: the only stochastic step in the nested chain is the per-level MH sweep; the level transitions are pure aggregations.

Two properties of the rebuild. The total weight of the level-1 graph equals the total weight of the level-0 graph (no edges are lost in the collapse), so \(E^{(l)}\) is conserved across the level transition; this is what allows the same flat-BlockState code to evaluate \(\Sigma^{(l)}\) without level-specific arithmetic. And the rebuild is \(O(N^{(l)})\) work per level transition, dominated by walking the level-\((l-1)\) edge list and binning into the level-\(l\) edge map; the total per-iteration cost is \(O(N + \sum_l N^{(l)})\) which on graphs with rapidly shrinking levels reduces to \(O(N)\).

The level-1 partition is preserved across the rebuild: every level-1 vertex (which is a non-empty level-0 block) keeps the level-1 block label it had before the rebuild. When a level-0 block became empty during the level-0 sweep, the level-1 vertex that represented it is dropped and the remaining vertices are renumbered \(0 \ldots B^{(0)}_{\text{ne}} - 1\). The level-1 partition vector is permuted to match the new numbering.

stage 7 · level-1 MH sweep

Same MH move, smaller graph, no degree prior to track.

The level-1 sweep runs the same MH move as level 0, on the level-1 graph: shuffled visit order, per-vertex uniform proposal over the level-1 candidate pool, single-vertex acceptance ratio at \(\beta = 1\). The \(\Delta\Sigma\) is the level-1 NDC delta.

The "without DC" qualifier at this level is structural, not a choice. The level-1 graph is a weighted multigraph over level-0 blocks; the "degree" of a level-1 vertex is the level-0 block's total edge weight \(e^{(0)}_r\), which is part of the level-0 model rather than an independent quantity at level 1. Degree correction at the higher levels would double-count: the per-node degree variation it tries to fit was already absorbed by the level-0 entropy. Graph-tool's canonical NestedBlockState makes the same choice; every level above 0 is NDC in both nested-DC and nested-NDC.

Two properties of the level-1 sweep. The per-visit cost is \(O(\deg^{(1)}(v) + B^{(1)}_{\text{ne}})\), same shape as the level-0 sweep but with the smaller \(N^{(1)} = B^{(0)}_{\text{ne}}\) (typically a small handful of vertices for a fixture this size). And the level-1 mode partition is what gives the hierarchy its multi-scale interpretation: a non-singleton level-1 block groups level-0 blocks that interact more than the per-pair baseline expects, which on the comdet fixture often pairs A and D into one super-block.

Two properties of the cascade. The level sizes decay geometrically when the partition coarsens by a constant factor at each step; on the comdet fixture they decay faster than that because the level-1 graph already has only 5 to 7 vertices and most of them merge during the level-1 sweep. And the top level is always trivial: when \(N^{(l)} \le 2\) the only valid partitions are one block or two blocks, the entropy is bounded above by a constant, and the chain just confirms that adding more depth pays nothing.

stage 9 · multi-level merge

One outer iteration = one full pass top to bottom.

The complete round-robin pass is one outer iteration of the nested MCMC chain: one MH sweep at every level in order, with deterministic rebuilds between sweeps. The chain runs for a fixed number of outer iterations (20 on this page) and the per-iteration cost is dominated by the level-0 sweep.

Two properties of the round-robin. The cycle is a valid Gibbs-style update of each level's conditional posterior given the other levels, so the chain samples the joint posterior asymptotically; the per-level MH sweeps are inner Metropolis-Hastings moves on each conditional. And the upper-level moves can shift super-blocks of level-0 vertices in one step: when a level-1 move merges two level-1 blocks, every level-0 block underneath one of them is relabelled to the other's super-block id, which on the next level-0 sweep is visible as a different starting partition. The flat chain reaches the same partitions only through long sequences of single-vertex moves.

Why the chain does not run greedy merges between level-0 blocks directly. Graph-tool's canonical multilevel sampler does propose agglomerative merges as part of the proposal mix; the JS port omits this for the byte-equal pair and matches the cpp tracer under single-vertex MH only. The agglomerative merges live in the canonical pipeline and are noted as the principal divergence from the JS-side mode on the technical companion.

A still-falling trace at the right edge means the chain has not equilibrated and the sweep count is too low. On this fixture 20 sweeps usually settle the chain. Under NDC the trace is rougher than under DC because the model is more sensitive to hub placement: the chain may switch between "hub in its own block" and "hub in A" several times before settling, and each switch shifts \(\Sigma_0\) by tens of nats. stage 12 · NDC degree-pathology across levels

Base level groups by degree if hubs present; coarser levels less affected.

The signature failure mode of flat NDC is hub-leaf splitting: in a block with mixed degrees, the model prefers to peel the high-degree node into a singleton block where its internal-stub cost drops from \(e_r \log n_r\) to \(e_r \log 1 = 0\). The same pathology shows up at every level of nested-NDC, but the higher levels are less exposed because the per-level "degree" is the block-summed edge weight, which is less heterogeneous than per-node degrees.

At level 0 the cost gap between the hub (22.4 nats) and the leaves (5 nats) is 17.4 nats; the chain has a strong incentive to peel the hub into its own block to save those 17.4 nats per internal stub it carries. At level 1 the cost gap between the most-connected super-vertex (around 8 nats) and the least-connected (around 3 nats) is 5 nats; the incentive is real but much smaller, and the rest of the per-level DL terms (edges DL + partition DL) often outweigh the saving. Two properties. The pathology attenuates with depth because per-level edge-weight aggregation flattens the per-vertex heterogeneity. And the level-0 pathology is the main signal NDC users care about when picking between nested-NDC and nested-DC; the upper levels behave similarly under both variants and rarely change the comparison.

On A, nested-DC absorbs node 0's degree into the model and reads A as cohesive; nested-NDC pays the hub-leaf penalty and tends to peel the hub. The nested-DC vs. nested-NDC \(\Delta\Sigma\) on this fixture is in the same ballpark as the flat-DC vs. flat-NDC gap, plus or minus a few nats for the level-1+ contributions. Under Peixoto 2017 §VII.A's Bayes-factor reading, a \(\Delta\Sigma\) above 10 nats is decisive; the gap on this fixture is consistently in that range in favour of nested-DC, which is the expected ordering on any graph with heavy-tailed degrees.

Two properties of the comparison. The two chains share the level-1+ construction exactly: the level-1 graph is identical given the same level-0 partition, the level-1 NDC entropy is the same formula, the level-1 sweep is the same MH move; so the divergence in \(\Sigma_{\text{nested-DC}}\) vs. \(\Sigma_{\text{nested-NDC}}\) is concentrated at level 0. And the comparison generalises to any nested SBM: every nested variant in graph-tool is NDC above level 0, so the only choice that ever matters is the level-0 entropy form. The nested-best page surfaces the \(\Delta\Sigma\) directly under shared seeded init.

Small fixtures, narrow within-block degree distributions, regularisation favours fewer parameters.

Two properties of the "NDC wins" regimes. None of them apply to the comdet fixture: the within-block degree variance at A is large (hub at degree 9, leaves at degree 2), \(N = 32\) is small but the degree DL at A's block is only around 6 nats (smaller than the 17.4-nat-per-stub hub-leaf gap), and the level-1+ prior on this fixture is structurally trivial (the level-1 graph is too small to add real regularisation). And the Bayes-factor reading is symmetric: a \(\Delta\Sigma\) of 5 to 10 nats in favour of NDC under Peixoto's reading is "suggestive but inconclusive" exactly as a 5 to 10 nat gap in favour of DC would be; treating raw \(\Sigma\) values across variants as commensurable is a category error the anchor nested-best page guards against.

caveat · level-0 walker only

This page animates only the level-0 MCMC walker. The kernel composes the full NestedBlockState across all levels end-to-end and the cpp tracer matches it byte-for-byte; surfacing upper-level walkers in the page UI is on the follow-up. Cross-check vs. graph-tool's nested output via minimize_nested_blockmodel_dl is on the same follow-up; the canonical pipeline uses pcg64 RNG, dense entropy, and multilevel agglomerative merges, all sanctioned divergences from the JS-cpp byte-equal pair.
