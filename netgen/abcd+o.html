ABCD+o

ABCD with a cluster_id=1 outlier block. Outliers have only external stubs and cannot connect to each other; real clusters inherit the ABCD recipe verbatim.

Plain ABCD treats unclustered input nodes as 1-node singletons that get filtered out of com.csv at the end. That is fine when the background is noise, but when the "unclustered" set is meaningful (bots, sensors, drive-by users) the silent filter loses information. ABCD+o promotes the background into a first-class block with ID 1 and two extra constraints baked into the sampler.

First: outliers have \(d^{\text{int}}_i = 0\). Every one of their stubs is external. You do not get to argue. The ABCD recipe applied inside an outlier "cluster" would scatter edges across the background, recreating the community the model is trying to deny.

Second: outliers cannot pair with each other. The external configuration model is constrained so every outlier stub pairs with a non-outlier. Outlier-outlier edges that existed in the input are dropped at profile time because the sampler cannot reproduce them.

profiling · degree sequence

Drop the outlier-outlier edges first; read what remains as each node's degree.

ABCD+o is the one generator in the repo that flips drop_outlier_outlier_edges to true by default. The reason is an admissibility match: the sampler refuses OO edges, so counting them at profile time would hand the sampler a stub budget it cannot satisfy, and the realised \(\xi\) would drift. Dropping OO at profile time means the degree sequence the sampler sees is the sequence it can actually fulfil.

On our synthetic, the edge \((19, 20)\) is the only OO edge. Dropping it reduces \(d_{19}\) and \(d_{20}\) by one each. A real input like dnc drops more aggressively: outliers there often connect mostly to other outliers, so a handful of outliers end up with degree 0 after the drop and get silently dropped from the edge list.

profiling · cluster sizes + n_outliers

Two files, one mega-cluster.

Profile emits cluster_sizes.csv with the real clusters only, plus a separate n_outliers.txt holding the integer outlier count. Stage 2 concatenates the two: the outlier count is prepended to the size list so the sampler sees one flat list where entry 1 is the outlier mega-cluster and entries 2 onward are the real clusters, sorted size desc. After prepend, cluster_id=1 is always the outliers.

On our synthetic:

  • cluster_sizes.csv (real clusters, sorted size desc): [8, 6, 4]
  • n_outliers.txt: 2
  • What the sampler sees after prepend: [2, 8, 6, 4]; cluster_id=1 is the outlier mega.

On dnc the mega-cluster is size 355 (the full outlier pile) and the real list is [52, 50, 36, 33, 33, ...], so the sampler sees [355, 52, 50, 36, 33, 33, ...] with 88 entries total. The mega-cluster towers over every real cluster.

profiling · the mixing scalar

Still one number: \(\xi\), computed after drop_oo.

Same formula as ABCD, different denominator. Let \(E^+\) be the edge count after dropping OO edges, and \(E^+_{\text{out}}\) the count of edges in \(E^+\) that leave the home cluster (with outliers pseudo-clustered as singletons at profile time). Then

\[ \xi \;=\; \frac{E^+_{\text{out}}}{E^+}. \]

At profile time outliers are treated as singletons (not yet the mega-cluster); the mega-cluster idea kicks in only when stage 2 prepends the count. So an outlier-to-real edge still counts as inter here and lands in the numerator. The effect of the prepend on \(\xi\) is already baked into the number the sampler receives; dropping OO edges and leaving CO (clustered-outlier) edges in is the version of \(\xi\) the sampler can actually reproduce.

why drop_oo is on by default here (and only here)

The ABCD+o sampler refuses outlier-outlier edges by construction. If the profile counted them in the degree sequence, the sampler would be asked to produce degrees it cannot fulfil, and in practice it drops stubs until admissibility holds. That silent drop shows up in the output as a depressed \(\xi\) even when the target \(\xi\) was correctly passed. Profiling with drop_oo=true means the sampler's budget matches its capability, and the realised \(\xi\) tracks the target. Every other generator in the repo leaves drop_oo at false.

generation · the \(\xi\) split with outlier pinning

Outliers are pinned: every stub is external, regardless of \(\xi\).

For non-outliers the ABCD rule applies: \(d^{\text{ext}}_i = \mathrm{round}(\xi \cdot d_i)\), \(d^{\text{int}}_i = d_i - d^{\text{ext}}_i\). For outliers (nodes in cluster_id=1) the sampler overrides:

\[ d^{\text{ext}}_i \;=\; d_i, \qquad d^{\text{int}}_i \;=\; 0. \]

Slide \(\xi\) below and watch the non-outlier bars repartition while nodes 19 and 20 stay red for any value. They are locked. The only exception is exact \(\xi = 0\), where the ABCD rule would send everything internal; for consistency the viz still shows outliers as all-external even at \(\xi = 0\).

generation · configuration model with OO exclusion

Internal pairing per real cluster. External pairing on the global pool, minus outlier-outlier.

Three passes. Internal: for each real cluster (cluster_id ≥ 2), shuffle the \(d^{\text{int}}_i\) stubs of its members and pair adjacent. The outlier mega-cluster (cluster_id=1) sits this step out, because its members have \(d^{\text{int}} = 0\). External: shuffle the global pool of external stubs and pair adjacent, but with a rejection rule: any pair where both endpoints are outliers is dropped and re-attempted. Rewire: self-loops and parallels get swapped out the same way plain ABCD does.

rejection vs constraint

The sampler enforces OO exclusion by rejection inside the external pairing loop: draw two stubs at random from the pool; if both belong to outliers, put them back and redraw. The effect is the same as drawing from a restricted distribution where OO pairs have probability zero. On inputs with a large outlier share (e.g. dnc where 355 of 906 nodes are outliers and each contributes many external stubs), the rejection rate can climb; the sampler handles this cleanly but runtime grows roughly linearly with the outlier-stub fraction.

generation · the warning branch

At low \(\xi\), the outliers accidentally look like a community.

There are no outlier-outlier edges in the output, but modularity-style detectors do not care about edges directly; they care about shared neighbours. Two outliers that connect to the same three non-outliers look similar to a detector, even when they share no edge. At low \(\xi\) with a large outlier mega-cluster, the non-outlier side of the external pool is small, and many non-outliers end up with several outlier neighbours, so outlier pairs share a lot of neighbours by coincidence.

The Julia sampler detects the pattern and writes "outlier nodes form a community" to stderr. The Python wrapper branches on that substring:

  • Warning fires: keep cluster_id=1 rows in com.csv. The outliers appear as a real community in the output. Whatever downstream tool reads com.csv will see a big lump at ID 1.
  • Warning silent: strip cluster_id=1 rows from com.csv. The outliers still appear in edge.csv (they have edges to real clusters) but carry no cluster label. This is the intended "outliers are outliers" path.

Slide \(\xi\) below 0.10 to simulate the warning firing. In our shipped dnc run the warning is silent; cluster_id=1 gets stripped and com.csv has 551 rows, the clustered 551 out of 906 total.

caveat: the heuristic is deterministic but not documented

The branch affects com.csv's semantics, and it triggers on a stderr substring. Any downstream tool that reads com.csv without also reading the wrapper's log file cannot tell whether an outlier "got lifted" into cluster_id=1 or was silently dropped. Plot the warning status prominently next to any visualisation of ABCD+o output.

caveat 1: drop_oo can delete nodes

On dnc, 233 of 906 nodes vanish from edge.csv. They were all outliers whose only edges were to other outliers; drop_oo zeros their degrees and the sampler has nothing to pair. If your downstream pipeline expects one row per input node in the edge list, ABCD+o silently breaks that invariant on outlier-heavy inputs.

caveat 2: cluster_id=1 is not always there

The semantics of com.csv depend on whether the "outlier nodes form a community" heuristic fired. On dnc it did not, so com.csv has 551 rows and no cluster_id=1. On inputs with low \(\xi\) and a dense outlier block, the warning fires, cluster_id=1 rows appear, and any code that joined on cluster_id will see a new community with 355 members. Always read run.log alongside com.csv.
