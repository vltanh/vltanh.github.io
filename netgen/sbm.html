netgen · sbm

SBM

Keep each node's degree, keep the block-to-block edge counts, roll the dice on everything else. The degree-corrected stochastic block model in its purest form.

You have a real network. You want a synthetic twin. The stochastic block model says: two things about G matter enough to freeze, and everything else is accident.

The two things it freezes are (1) which block each node belongs to, and (2) how many edges cross each pair of blocks. The degree-corrected variant adds a third: each node's degree is preserved so that hubs stay hubs. The micro-canonical flavour makes those constraints exact on the sampled multigraph, not just expected.

After the sampler runs, a quick cleanup: drop self-loops and parallel edges. That cleanup is where the model pays its honesty tax.

stage 0 · the question

If you knew only the block counts and the degrees, could you rebuild the graph?

A social network has two people in cluster C1 who are especially well-connected: call them 1 and 2. You and I know they are friends. Does the SBM know? Only if the edge (1, 2) shows up in the block matrix as one of the twelve intra-C1 edges. The SBM cannot tell which specific pairs use up that count. It just knows cluster C1 owes twelve intra-edges somewhere.

So the sampler scatters those edges over all possible intra-C1 pairs. Some pairs end up connected, some do not. The degree constraint pulls things back toward the hubs, but it does not pin down the pairs.

stage 2 · profile

Extract the block matrix.

The profile is a 4×4 matrix: three real clusters plus a combined outlier block (the default combined outlier mode folds 19 and 20 into one pseudo-block). Off-diagonal entries count inter-block edges once. Diagonal entries count intra-block edges twice: that matches graph-tool's undirected convention, where each intra-block edge is two half-edges both landing in the same block.

the idea

Two distinct graphs with the same block-matrix and the same degree sequence are equally likely under SBM. The model cannot tell them apart, which means the sample spreads probability over both.

stage 3 · sample

The sampler places edges per block-pair, allowing collisions.

Micro-SBM fires edges into each block-pair cell until its count is met. Because the constraint is on counts, not distinct pairs, the sampler is free to place two edges between the same two nodes (a parallel edge) or an edge from a node to itself (a self-loop). These do not violate the constraint and they do not get re-rolled.

The red-dashed edges below are the self-loops and parallels the micro-SBM emitted on this run. They are about to die in stage 4.

stage 4 · dedup

Drop the red-dashed edges. Watch the degree sequence break.

Flip the toggle to see what gt.remove_parallel_edges and gt.remove_self_loops do. The graph becomes a simple graph. Each dropped edge removes one unit of degree from its endpoints; on a dense block, a hub loses several.

On this 20-node synthetic the dedup bill is small. On the real dnc graph shipped in the repo it is 29% of edges, because the block matrix had many duplicate-heavy cells.

honesty

The pre-dedup multigraph matches the profile exactly: degree sequence, block matrix, every count. The post-dedup simple graph does not. Every SBM guarantee you read in papers applies to the multigraph; the simple-graph output is an upper bound on what the profile asked for.
