nPSO

Nonuniform Popularity-Similarity Optimisation. Embeds nodes on a hyperbolic disk and connects pairs that lie close in the geometry; a temperature knob trades clustering coefficient against randomness.

The other six generators in this project produce nearly tree-like graphs. Low triangle density. Clustering coefficient around 0.01 to 0.05, where your real social graph might be 0.3 or higher. That is a property of the degree-corrected SBM and configuration-model families: they do not target triangles.

nPSO is the one exception. It gets triangles by embedding nodes in a hyperbolic disk and connecting nodes that are geometrically close. A temperature knob $$T \in (0, 1)$$ controls how strictly "close" is enforced: small $$T$$ means only very close pairs connect (high clustering), large $$T$$ means random-ish (low clustering). Matching an empirical clustering coefficient means searching for the right $$T$$.

stage 1 · the disk

Hubs at the center, leaves at the rim, angular sectors by cluster.

Each node gets two coordinates. The radial position is the popularity: hubs sit near the center, low-degree nodes near the rim. The angular position is the similarity: nodes in the same community sit in the same angular sector.

For this small 20-node demo the formulas used are:

$$ r_i \;=\; r_{\min} + (r_{\max} - r_{\min}) \cdot \frac{\ln(\mathrm{rank}_i + 1)}{\ln N} \qquad \theta_i \;=\; \theta_{C(i)} + \delta_i $$

where \(\mathrm{rank}_i\) is the node's degree rank (0 for the biggest hub, \(N{-}1\) for the lowest-degree leaf), \(\theta_{C(i)}\) is the angular centre of node \(i\)'s cluster sector, and \(\delta_i\) spreads nodes evenly within that sector.

reading the disk · the dashed circles mark popularity levels (degree rank tiers). The angular sectors are cluster-coloured faintly at the rim. Drag a node to see the snap-back to its polar home; hover for stats.
stage 2 · temperature

Drag $$T$$. Watch edges form or dissolve.

For each pair of nodes, nPSO checks their hyperbolic distance against a cutoff scaled by $$T$$. Low $$T$$: only the closest pairs connect, which produces many triangles because triangles require three pairwise-close nodes. High $$T$$: the cutoff stretches and random-looking pairs connect, triangles become scarce.

monotonic in T

The clustering coefficient is a monotonically decreasing function of $$T$$ on $$(0, 1)$$. That is what makes the search a one-dimensional root-finding problem: bracket from above and below, bisect until you hit the target.

stage 3 · the secant search

Bracket, bisect, and (where possible) take secant steps.

The wrapper starts with $$[T_{\min}, T_{\max}] = [0, 1]$$ and bisects. Once both endpoints have residuals with opposite signs it switches to the secant formula $$T_{\text{sec}} = T_{\min} - f_{\min} \cdot (T_{\max} - T_{\min}) / (f_{\max} - f_{\min})$$. A 5% margin guard falls back to midpoint if the secant lands too close to an endpoint. Stops when the residual drops below 0.005 or the step stalls below 0.0001 or after 100 iters.

honest failure is possible

nPSO's achievable clustering coefficient on a given input depends on $$(N, m, \gamma, c)$$. If the target is above that range (say, a very highly-clustered real graph), the search exhausts 100 iters without converging and returns the best-so-far. On the shipped dnc example the target is 0.548 and the best achievable was around 0.099. The output is the best nPSO could do, but it is nowhere near the target. Always check the achieved ccoeff in the run log.
