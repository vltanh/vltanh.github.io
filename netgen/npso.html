nPSO

Nonuniform Popularity-Similarity Optimisation. Embeds nodes on a hyperbolic disk and connects pairs that lie close in the geometry; a temperature knob trades clustering coefficient against randomness.

The other six generators in this project produce nearly tree-like graphs. Low triangle density. Clustering coefficient around 0.01 to 0.05, where your real social graph might be 0.3 or higher. That is a property of the degree-corrected SBM and configuration-model families: they do not target triangles.

nPSO is the one exception. It gets triangles by embedding nodes in a hyperbolic disk and connecting nodes that are geometrically close. A temperature knob $$T \in (0, 1)$$ controls how strictly "close" is enforced: small $$T$$ means only very close pairs connect (high clustering), large $$T$$ means random-ish (low clustering). Matching an empirical clustering coefficient means searching for the right $$T$$.

stage 1 · profile

Fit every scalar the generator needs.

Profile reads the reference network + clustering and distils them into a small, explicit contract. Stage 2 gets only those derived values; it never re-reads the edgelist. The default model is nPSO2, so alongside the scalars profile also emits a weight vector \(\rho_k = \text{size}_k / N\), one entry per mixture component.

stage 2 · the disk

Embed every node on the Poincaré disk.

Each node gets two polar coordinates. The radial position encodes popularity: hubs sit near the centre, low-degree nodes drift to the rim. The angular position encodes similarity: nodes in the same component share an angular neighbourhood.

Let \(t_i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) be the node's arrival time in the growth process (biggest hub = earliest arrival), and let \(\beta = 1/(\gamma - 1)\). Under the popularity-fading rule (Papadopoulos et al. 2012; Muscoloni & Cannistraci 2018), the radial coordinate after all \(N\) arrivals is

\[ r_i \;=\; 2\beta \, \ln(t_i) \;+\; 2(1-\beta) \, \ln(N). \]

Default model is nPSO2: the angular coordinate is sampled from a Gaussian mixture with \(C\) equidistant means \(\mu_k = 2\pi k / C\), common width \(\sigma = 2\pi / (6C)\), and the profile-supplied weights \(\rho_k = \text{size}_k / N\):

\[ \theta_i \;\sim\; \sum_{k=1}^{C} \rho_k \, \mathcal{N}(\mu_k, \sigma^2) \pmod{2\pi}. \]

Each node is assigned to the component \(C(i) = \arg\min_k d_\mathrm{ang}(\theta_i, \mu_k)\). Under outlier-mode singleton (the default), each input outlier rides on its own tiny-weight component with \(\rho = 1/N\), so it lands near a dedicated \(\mu_k\) rather than in an anonymous gap. The outer heatmap strip reflects this: the three bright lobes are the real clusters, the two dim lobes are singleton outliers.

stage 3 · temperature

Drag $$T$$. Watch edges form or dissolve.

For each pair of nodes, nPSO checks their hyperbolic distance against a cutoff scaled by $$T$$. Low $$T$$: only the closest pairs connect, which produces many triangles because triangles require three pairwise-close nodes. High $$T$$: the cutoff stretches and random-looking pairs connect, triangles become scarce.

monotonic in T

The clustering coefficient is a monotonically decreasing function of $$T$$ on $$(0, 1)$$. That is what makes the search a one-dimensional root-finding problem: bracket from above and below, bisect until you hit the target.

stage 4 · the secant search

Bracket, bisect, and (where possible) take secant steps.

The wrapper starts with $$[T_{\min}, T_{\max}] = [0, 1]$$ and bisects. Because $$cc(T)$$ is monotonically decreasing, the bracket update is the opposite of the textbook rule: an iterate with $$cc > \mathrm{target}$$ means $$T$$ is too small, so $$T_{\min}$$ is raised and the next sample moves right; an iterate with $$cc < \mathrm{target}$$ overshoots, so $$T_{\max}$$ is lowered and the next sample moves left. Once both endpoints have residuals with opposite signs it switches to the secant formula $$T_{\text{sec}} = T_{\min} - f_{\min} \cdot (T_{\max} - T_{\min}) / (f_{\max} - f_{\min})$$. A 5% margin guard falls back to midpoint if the secant lands too close to an endpoint. Stops when the residual drops below 0.005 or the step stalls below 0.0001 or after 100 iters.

honest failure is possible

nPSO's achievable clustering coefficient on a given input depends on $$(N, m, \gamma, c)$$. If the target is above that range (say, a very highly-clustered real graph), the search exhausts 100 iters without converging and returns the best-so-far. On the shipped dnc example the target is 0.548 and the best achievable was around 0.099. The output is the best nPSO could do, but it is nowhere near the target. Always check the achieved ccoeff in the run log.
