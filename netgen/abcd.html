ABCD

A faster, more tractable alternative to LFR. Takes the degree sequence and cluster sizes as given; a single global mixing parameter $$\xi$$ sets the proportion of edges that cross cluster boundaries.

Imagine each node has a bundle of friendship stubs. One for each edge they owe to someone. A single knob $$\xi \in [0, 1]$$ decides the fraction of each node's stubs that leave the cluster. Inside each cluster, shuffle the internal stubs and pair them at random. Globally across clusters, do the same for the externals. Fix collisions by rewiring.

That is the whole model. The beauty is that ABCD takes your empirical degree sequence and your cluster sizes as is: no power-law fit, no resampling. The output's degrees are yours, up to the small perturbation the rewire introduces.

stage 1 · input + summary

ABCD only keeps three numbers.

The profile step reads your input and throws away the edge list. What remains: the degree sequence, the cluster-size list, and the scalar $$\xi$$. Everything else the sampler makes up on the fly.

stage 2 · the ξ split

Drag $$\xi$$. Watch each node's bundle repartition live.

For each node, ABCD computes $$d_i^{\text{ext}} = \text{round}(\xi \cdot d_i)$$ and $$d_i^{\text{int}} = d_i - d_i^{\text{ext}}$$. The internal stubs stay in the cluster; the external stubs go to the global pool. Because the split is per-node, the global external fraction is exactly $$\xi$$ in expectation, and concentrates around it as N grows.

the rounding rule matters

A node with degree 2 and $$\xi = 0.333$$ rounds to $$d^{\text{ext}} = 1$$. A node with degree 5 rounds to $$d^{\text{ext}} = 2$$. Low-degree nodes cross more than their share; hubs cross less. The per-node rounding is the reason ABCD's realised ξ can drift a tiny bit from the target.

stage 3 · configuration model

Shuffle the stubs and pair them.

Each cluster runs a configuration model on its internal half-edges: flatten the stubs into one list, shuffle, pair adjacent. Globally, the external half-edges do the same. That is it. The only deviation from pure random is the constraint that a node cannot connect to more neighbours than its cluster has members, which ABCD enforces at assignment time via $$d_i \leq s_{C(i)} - 1$$.

identity is not preserved

ABCD's sampler receives your degree sequence, your cluster sizes, and $\xi$ as anonymous TSVs. It reassigns nodes to clusters under $d_i \leq s_{C(i)} - 1$ and emits output IDs that match its own integer indexing, not yours. Node "1" in the output is not node 1 in your input. What is preserved: the multiset of degrees (up to rewire drift) and the multiset of cluster sizes. LFR goes further and resamples even those.

stage 4 · rewire

Fix self-loops and duplicates.

The configuration model is happy to pair a stub with another stub from the same node (a self-loop) or re-pair a pair (a parallel edge). ABCD rewires these: swap endpoints with another random pair until the collision is gone. On this small synthetic the rewire is trivial; on larger graphs it typically handles a few percent of edges.
