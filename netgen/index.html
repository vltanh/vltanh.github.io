project · network-generation

netgen

Seven synthetic-network generators, walked stage by stage. Same small input in every page, so what each generator preserves and what it throws away becomes visible side by side.

The question: given a real network G, what is a synthetic twin of it? There is no single answer. Each generator freezes a different set of things about G and rolls the dice on the rest. Pick the generator by picking what matters.

Each page below is a vertical scroll. Start with the question the generator is trying to answer, meet the same 20-node example, walk through the stages with one interactive widget per stage, end with an honest note about what gets kept and what drifts.

SBM family

Block-model samplers. These three keep your node IDs and your cluster assignment through the pipeline, and (try to) match the block-pair edge matrix.

sbm
The cookie cutter. Keep the block matrix and the degrees, roll the dice on everything else. Expect a 29% edge loss to self-loop and parallel-edge dedup on dense inputs.

Configuration-model family

Stub-pairing samplers. One knob controls how many edges leave the cluster. ABCD keeps your degrees; LFR fits a power law and resamples.

abcd
Friendship stubs, one knob $$\xi$$ for the internal/external split. Takes your empirical degree sequence as is; the modern benchmark of choice.
interactive · ξ slider
profile extractsdegreecluster sizesξ
abcd+o
ABCD with an explicit outlier block. Outliers have only external stubs and cannot pair with each other. A warning-branch flips com.csv semantics at low ξ.

lfr
Fit two power laws, resample both, throw the original away. The incumbent benchmark; fresh node IDs with no correspondence to the input.

Geometric

The only generator here that aims for clustering coefficient. Nodes on a hyperbolic disk, one temperature knob, a search for the right T.

npso
Hyperbolic-disk embedding. Low T gives many triangles; high T gives a random-looking graph. A secant search finds the T that matches a target clustering coefficient, when one exists.
