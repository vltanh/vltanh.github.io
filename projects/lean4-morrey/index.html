Morrey's problem in Lean 4

Lean 4 formalizations of three papers on Morrey's problem, whether rank-one convexity implies quasiconvexity, and a proof that it does for isotropic, positively homogeneous energies on 2 × 2 matrices.

An elastic material settles where its energy is least. Describe a deformation of a sheet by its gradient, the \(2 \times 2\) matrix that says how each small square of the sheet is stretched, and the material by an energy \(F\) of that matrix. In 1952 Charles Morrey found the condition on \(F\) under which the total energy behaves well, quasiconvexity: no wiggle of a sheet held at its edge lowers its energy. It ranges over infinitely many wiggles and is hard to check. Rank-one convexity asks only that no layering of two deformations lowers the energy, and it can be checked one line at a time. Quasiconvexity implies it, and Morrey asked whether the converse holds.

For maps with three or more components it does not, by an example of Vladimír Šverák from 1992, and two preprints of 2026 claim the same for two components and three or more variables. For maps of the plane to itself, \(2 \times 2\) matrices, the question is open. A positive answer there would prove Iwaniec’s conjecture of 1982 in complex analysis, as Astala, Iwaniec, Prause and Saksman showed in 2012.

This project formalizes three papers on the problem in Lean 4: Šverák’s, a claimed proof of the \(2 \times 2\) case by Pablo Pedregal, and a preprint of Gabriele Cassese. It also proves a theorem of its own on the \(2 \times 2\) case, for isotropic, homogeneous energies.

The papers

  • Šverák (1992). The formalization proves every step of his example, a quartic polynomial on \(3 \times 2\) matrices, including the steps left to the reader. It found no false statement.
  • Pedregal. In his preprint (arXiv:1905.06571v5), the formalization refutes Proposition 7.1, Corollary 7.2 and the summary of Step 2 of the proof by an explicit counterexample, checked in Lean, and proves the rest of Sections 3 to 7 but one claim. It states the main theorems without proof, so the claim is neither proved nor refuted.
  • Cassese (2026). The preprint (arXiv:2608.03488v3) claims a negative answer for maps from \(\mathbb{R}^m\) to \(\mathbb{R}^2\) with \(m \ge 3\). The repository audits it with a Lean formalization that covers every result the preprint proves, with the results it cites from probability and harmonic analysis formalized in three packages, lean4-banach-probability, lean4-harmonic-analysis and lean4-calculus-of-variations. It found no false statement, and filled the gaps it came across.

A theorem for isotropic, homogeneous energies

An energy \(F\) is isotropic if \(F(QAR) = F(A)\) for all rotations \(Q\) and \(R\), and positively \(p\)-homogeneous if \(F(\lambda A) = \lambda^p F(A)\) for \(\lambda > 0\).

Theorem A. Let \(p > 0\). Every isotropic, positively \(p\)-homogeneous, rank-one convex energy on \(2 \times 2\) matrices is quasiconvex.

For \(p \ge 2\), an energy with the three properties that is never negative is even polyconvex, a convex function of the matrix and its determinant, and so quasiconvex by the classical argument. This answers Question 1.7 of André Guerra and Jan Kristensen (2022). For \(p > 2\) an energy that is negative somewhere is not polyconvex. Among such energies are the Burkholder function, whose quasiconvexity implies Iwaniec’s conjecture, and the energies \(f_\gamma\) of Dacorogna and Marcellini with \(1 < \lvert\gamma\rvert \le 2/\sqrt3\), whose quasiconvexity Dacorogna’s book left open. By Theorem A these \(f_\gamma\) are quasiconvex, as a 2026 preprint of Bruno and Pasqualotto also claims. The explainer has more on these energies and the results for each range of \(p\).

The proof uses one result from outside, the quasiconvexity of the truncated determinant, claimed as Theorem 1 of the preprint of Agazzi et al. on Iwaniec’s conjecture and audited with a Lean formalization, lean4-iwaniec. A paper in the repository writes up the proof.

Verification

Lean’s kernel checks every proof, with Lean and Mathlib v4.36.0-rc2, and an audit script checks that every declaration, in the packages too, depends only on Lean’s three standard axioms. Comparator, the checker of the Palomar registry, accepts the Solution of each paper and of Theorem A against its Challenge, under Lean’s kernel and the independent NanoDa kernel. The four entries are packaged for Palomar but not yet submitted. Independent AI agents checked the statements and the proofs against the papers. No person has reviewed the proofs or the paper.

What’s next

Theorem A needs both symmetries, and the general \(2 \times 2\) case, still open, remains the target.
