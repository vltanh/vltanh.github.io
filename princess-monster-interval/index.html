Princess and Monster

A princess and a monster are in a dark corridor, the stretch of line from −1 to 1. Neither can see the other, and neither can move faster than one unit of length per unit of time. The monster wants to bump into the princess as soon as he can; she wants to stay hidden as long as she can. If both play as well as possible, how long does it take on average?

Nobody knows exactly. It is between and , and here you can play both sides with the two plans that show it. In every picture the corridor runs across and time runs upward.

Hide from the monster

You are the princess. Drag the blue dot to where you want to start, press Start, and steer. Your trail is drawn as you go; moving at full speed draws a line at 45°, and you can't go faster.

The monster follows one path picked at random from his plan, from either end. You won't see it until he finds you.

Before the round, drag your dot along the bottom edge. During the round, move the pointer left or right over the board, or hold ← and →. Space starts a round.

What to look for

One round is luck: some of his paths find you at once, some late. What counts is your average over his whole plan, shown after each round, and no path averages more than against it. The best paths step out a little, run in towards the centre, and leave it just before time 1.

3

The monster's plan

A monster with one fixed path is easy to dodge, so he rolls dice over many paths. Six times in ten he simply sweeps from one end to the other. The rest of the time he turns back somewhere in the middle, so that waiting near an end isn't safe either.

Every path also has a mirror image that starts from the other side, used half the time. The picture shows one side only.

Faint lines are all of his paths, darker for the likelier ones. Pick a kind of path on the right to see only those.

    The princess's plan

    She rolls dice too, over escape routes that never cross. Her plan is the same on both sides of the centre, so the picture shows one side: across is her distance from the centre. Each curve is one route, coloured by what it does, and each is equally likely.

    Run the clock and watch the strip below the board: it is the corridor at that moment, with a dot for each route. The dashed red lines are the two sweeps, which meet in the middle at time 1.

      Squeezing the answer

      Each plan sets a limit for the other side. Against the monster's plan, no princess path can average more than a certain time: a ceiling for her. Against the princess's plan, no monster path can average less than a certain time: a floor for him. The answer is between the floor and the ceiling.
